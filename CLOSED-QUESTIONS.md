# Questions the literature posed, and this project answered

*This file holds current state only: each update rewrites the affected row in place, and the history is in
git (`git log -p -- CLOSED-QUESTIONS.md`). It is linked from `CLAUDE.md` § The mission.*

**Why this file exists.** Write-ups are deferred by default (`CLAUDE.md` § The mission). One of the things
that clears the publication bar is *a result that answers a question the literature has independently and
repeatedly posed, whatever its size* — so "which posed questions have we answered?" governs what gets
written. This file is the answer.

**What goes in.** A row belongs here when (a) someone outside this project **posed the question in writing**,
in print or in a public archive, and (b) we have an answer — or, in §3, are unusually well placed to find
one. An observation, a table presented without comment, or a result nobody asked for does not qualify,
however novel our explanation of it; the ledger in `CLAUDE.md` holds those.

**What every row carries.** The question **verbatim**, its author and date, our answer, where the proof
lives, whether the kernel checked it, **and the prior art that constrains the claim**. That last column is
the one this project has been wrong about most often (`CLAUDE.md` G49, G50: the reduction everything here
rests on was derived independently in seven lineages that barely cite one another). A row with an empty
prior-art column has not been checked, and says so.

### 1.1 The chromatic number of the plane over `ℚ(√3, √11)`

**`χ(K²) = 4` for `K = ℚ(√3, √11)`.** Kernel-checked (`Proofs/HadwigerNelson/FieldWall4.lean`,
`chromaticNumber_seamPlane`, zero `sorry`, axioms exactly `[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]`).
Ledger row 57.

> **A prior claim exists, found 2026-09-19 (FS-61's prior-art gate), and everything below this box must be re-read against it
> before any of it is repeated outside this repository.** `[Fischer1994]` — K. G. Fischer, *A planar geometric graph of chromatic
> number four*, Congressus Numerantium 104 (1994) 73–79, Zbl 0836.05030. Its zbMATH record (an author's summary, no reviewer; read by
> de Bruijn) says that for squarefree coprime `p ≡ 3`, `q ≡ 11 (mod 16)` with `pq ≡ 1 (mod 32)` the plane over `ℚ(√p, √q)` has an
> additive 4-colouring — a group homomorphism from the group generated by the unit vectors to `ℤ/4`, nonzero on every unit vector —
> and: *"Since the graph `ℚ(√3, √11)²` contains finite graphs which require four colors, it follows that the chromatic number of this
> graph is 4."* **If the proof stands, the equality `χ(ℚ(√3,√11)²) = 4` has been in print since 1994**, the question was not open when
> rows 3–6 of the table below posed it, and none of `[Moorhouse2010]`, `[Madore2015]`, `[CranstonRabern2015]`, `[ExooIsmailescu2019]`,
> Polymath16 or this project knew. **Status: summary read; the paper is print-only (HathiTrust: Michigan and Penn State, search-only)
> and unseen by anyone here; library request owed.** What would remain this project's under that reading: the kernel proof,
> Theorem A's general form, and whatever the comparison of his `ℤ/4` homomorphism with the residue map onto `𝔽₄` shows. The sentence
> "this is the one result that has cleared the publication bar" is suspended until the paper is read.

**Posed or named as open six times across fifty years, and bracketed by Madore.** The three oldest postings
(rows 1, 2, 4) do not cite one another; the three from 2018 on cite Madore's bracket, and rows 5 and 6 share
an author. This is the one result that has cleared the publication bar.

`Real.sqrt` anywhere. That settles rows 2–7; row 1 asks for every algebraic extension of `ℚ²`, and this is

**Also closed by it:** `[CranstonRabern2015]`'s second question outright, and — with `[MatolcsiEtAl2025]` — their first

**Prior art that constrains the claim.** `[Madore2015]`'s bracket is the closest anyone came. The residue

**Searched for a contradicting claim** on 2026-09-08 and 2026-09-09: all four citations of `[Madore2015]`

### 1.2 The general form of the residue colouring

**Theorem A** — a graph *drawn* in an abelian group, edge differences in a subgroup and non-zero under a
homomorphism out of it, is `|G|`-colourable. No metric, field, ring, valuation or Cayley structure, and the
homomorphism may be **partial**. Kernel-checked (`Proofs/HadwigerNelson/ResidueColouring.lean`,
`colorable_of_residue`). Ledger row 57.

> **`[Madore2015]`**, Prop. 3.9, on needing a subgroup that is not a ring and doing the coset
> construction by hand: *"which the author has not found the courage to try to formulate under the
> most general auspices."*

**Our answer.** That formulation, kernel-checked.

**Prior art that constrains the claim.** His Prop. 3.2 *is* the valuation-subring form, so
`colorable_unitDistance_induce_of_valuationSubring` is his theorem, not ours, and the unit argument is
`[Fischer1990]` Thm 1. The general form — the step Madore declined — is ours. The delimitation is written into
ledger row 57 and must survive into any manuscript. **Not proved:** that a number field with a
conjugation-fixed prime supplies the valuation subring with residue field of size `N(𝔭)` (`CLAUDE.md` G35).

**Status:** the headline of FS-20's manuscript (§1.1); not yet written.

### 1.3 Which distances can carry a four-colour compulsion over `K`

**The 2-adic law** (ledger row 59, kernel-checked, `Proofs/HadwigerNelson/ValuationLaw.lean` and
`ResidueColouring.lean` §4): over `K`, a pair forced monochromatic at four colours lies at a distance
**inside** the prime 𝔭 above 2, and a virtual edge (a pair forced distinct) at a distance **outside** it.

> **`[Heule2021]`**, as quoted in `[PartsOdd2022]` §4: *"if an odd distance `d` is a `k`-virtual edge of a
> subset `S` of the Euclidean plane, then distance `1/d` is a `k`-virtual edge in `S` as well."*
>
> **`[PartsOdd2022]`** §4 (arXiv:2206.12632, June 2022), after noting that the distances `3^a` and `3^−a`
> are 4-virtual edges in `ℚ(√−3, √−11)`: *"But does this duality work in the general case?"* He gives a
> finite counterexample at `d = 5`, reports that Heule *"meant not a finite graph, but larger objects like
> `ℚ(√−3, √−11)`"*, and concludes: *"But even in this case, doubts remain about the correctness of the
> statement. Therefore, a more accurate formulation of the statement and its proof are of certain
> interest."*

**Our answer — the necessary half.** `ℚ(√−3, √−11)`, read as points of the plane, lies inside `K²`, so the
law applies to Heule's object. It is the accurate formulation on the necessary side, with its proof: a
virtual edge over `K` at four colours lies at 𝔭-adic valuation zero, and `v_𝔭(1/d) = −v_𝔭(d)`, so the
condition is **invariant under `d ↦ 1/d`**. The odd integers are exactly the integer distances of valuation
zero, which is why the duality was stated for odd `d`; and Parts's finite counterexample does not touch the
law, which is necessary and not sufficient. **Open: the duality itself** — whether valuation zero suffices
for a virtual edge in some finite `K`-object (ledger row 59).

**It also explains two published tables that their authors present as observations** (not questions, so not
rows here): the two columns of `[Parts2020a]`'s Table 1, each distance barred from the other column (two
rows' `√` are unverified against the PDF, with the same valuation either way); and `[Heule2019]` §5,
*"Observed patterns of points in `Q[√3,√11] × Q[√3,√11]`"*.

**Prior art that constrains the claim, and it is substantial.** `[Ducz2026]` (10 June 2026, three months
before the law was proved here) gives the identification half in coordinates: two points of the Moser lattice
share a colour iff `N + M ≡ 0 (mod 4)` where `‖z‖² = (N + M√33)/6`, and his congruence reproduces ten for ten
of the data the law explains. **Ours:** the field-level statement over `K²` (rank 8, not his rank-4 ring), the
valuation formulation, the **compulsion** consumers (from "this colouring identifies these pairs" to "no
proper four-colouring of any `K`-object can force the others"), and the kernel proof. The observed pattern is
older still: `[Heule2019]`, `[PartsOdd2022]` §1, `[Ardal2009]` (a mono-pair at distance 8), and first
`[Polymath16threads]`, second thread, Jannis Harder, **28 April 2018**: *"The graph `V + V + H` not only
precludes monochromatic triples, but forces the point `(8/3, 0)` to have the same color as the origin."*
`[Heule2021]` itself is **not obtained**; its statement is known only through `[PartsOdd2022]`'s quotation.

**Status:** not written up; the deferral stands for it, and `BIBLIOGRAPHY.md` lists `[Heule2021]` as
blocking any write-up of row 59. The delimitation above is the hard part of writing it.

### 1.4 Does `5/3` receive the same colour as `0` in every five-colouring?

> **Philip Gibbs**, `[Polymath16threads]`, third thread, **2 May 2018**: *"Form `Z` from the unit modulus
> numbers `ω^a η^b ζ^c` with `a = 0,1,2,3,4,5, b = −2,−1,0,1,2, c = −2,−1,0,1,2`. Then take the graph
> `Z ⊕ Z ⊕ Z`. Is the point 5/3 always the same colour as the origin for all 5-colourings in this graph? If
> not, how about using `ζ₂ = (−3+i√247)/16` instead of `ζ`"* — where `ω = (1+i√3)/2`, `η = (√33+i√3)/6`,
> `ζ = (−1+i√15)/4`.

Half an hour earlier he gave the motive: `5/3` *"is already a multiple of 5 in the ring `ℤ[ω₁, ω₃]` so it
will be the same colour as the origin in any homomorphic 5-colouring"*, and a positive answer would make it a
spindle distance for a sixth colour. Dustin Mixon restated the question opening the fourth thread (5 May
2018), and Gibbs proposed spindling on `5/3` again on 29 May 2018. No answer appears in the eighteen threads
(every mention of `5/3`, plain and LaTeX, read 2026-09-10).

**Our answer: no, for both graphs, and the reason is a congruence.** Both are drawn over fields where the
prime 11 is usable: `Z ⊕ Z ⊕ Z` has coordinates in `ℚ(√3, √5, √11)` with denominators built from 2 and 3, and
the `ζ₂` variant in `ℚ(√3, √11, √247)`, where 247 ≡ 5 is a square mod 11. By the distance law of ledger row 55,
a pair forced equal at five colours in such a graph has `11 ∣ d²`, while `(5/3)² = 25/9 ≡ 4 (mod 11)`. So the
five-colourings pulled back from the plane at 11 separate `5/3` from the origin; since those colourings exist,
the answer is not vacuous.

**Kernel status: kernel-checked for the first graph, as a statement about all of its certificates; a hand
argument for the second.** `UDCert.not_forces_five_thirds_11` (`Instances/ResidueObstruction11.lean`): no
certificate over the `√7`-free tower with `11 ∤ D` forces a pair at squared distance `25/9` equal at five
colours (and `not_separated_five_thirds_11`: nor separates one). It goes through the law at a rational squared
distance `s/t` (`UDCert.not_forces_of_distSq_ratio`, `ResidueObstruction.lean`), which needs only `s ≢ 0`; the
integer law is its `t = 1` face. The hypotheses have a model: Heule-510, which has 139 pairs at exactly `5/3`
(an exact search of its table; the kernel certifies one, `(19, 59)`, as `heule510_pairsAtRatio_five_thirds`) and
is 5-colourable, so "forced at five colours" is a live question there. `Z ⊕ Z ⊕ Z` is an instance of the
theorem's quantifier; no certificate of it has been built. The `ζ₂` variant needs `√247`, which the kernel's
field tower lacks, so it stays a hand argument.

**Prior art that constrains the claim.** The premise that `5/3` is monochromatic under every homomorphic
colouring is `[Fischer1990]`'s `k·G = 0` obstruction at `k = 5`. The distance law is ours (ledger row 55).

**Answered at every distance, not only at `5/3` (FS-38, 2026-09-13; ledger row 66).** The plane at 11 has a
five-colouring with a colour absent from one vertex's closed neighbourhood (`finitePlane11_fanGap`), and such a
gap, repainted along the residue homomorphism, frees every pair above it from being forced equal
(`not_forces_of_fanGap`, `Slack.forces_iff_of_hom`); a second table has one colour absent from the closed
neighbourhoods of both ends of an edge, so a virtual edge is a unit edge (`not_separated_of_sharedGap`). Hence
over every field where 11 is usable of residue degree one — every 5-sealed multiquadratic field containing
ℚ(√3,√11), Gibbs's ℚ(√3,√5,√11) and ℚ(√3,√11,√247) among them — **no two distinct points of any unit-distance
graph are forced equal by every five-colouring, at any distance, and the only pairs separated by every
five-colouring are unit edges** (`forces_iff_of_hom_finitePlane11`, `separated_iff_adj_of_hom_finitePlane11`).
Kernel for every `Valid` √7-free certificate with `11 ∤ D` (`UDCert.forces_eq_11`, `UDCert.separated_iff_adj_11`
— every pair, no distance hypothesis) and unconditionally for Heule-510 (`heule510_forces_iff`,
`heule510_separated_iff_adj`); the `ζ₂` graph over ℚ(√3,√11,√247) still needs M58's hand step, √247 being outside
the kernel's tower. So the answer to Gibbs's question is no for every distance, and his design — a forced pair
spindled for a sixth colour — has no instance over any such field (`CLAUDE.md` G48, G54); the residue law above
survives as the level-one shadow of the theorem.

**Why it matters beyond the negative.** They had the right shape — the design target this project called FS-6
(`CLAUDE.md` G48: a pair forced equal at five colours, then spindled) — and no filter on the distance; the
distance law was that filter, and the fan gap removes every distance at once.

**Status:** not written up.

### 1.6 The fractional chromatic number of the plane over `ℚ(√3, √11)`

> **`[CranstonRabern2015]`**, concluding section (Combinatorica 37, 2017): *"What is the fractional chromatic number of
> `ℚ(√3,√11) × ℚ(√3,√11)`?"* — after noting that their graphs give `χ_f(ℚ(√3,√11)²) ≥ 76/21`.

**Our answer: 4, exactly.** The upper bound is Theorem B (§1.1, kernel-checked). The lower bound is `[MatolcsiEtAl2025]`'s
27-point graph `G27`, which lies in the Moser lattice inside `ℚ(√3,√11)²` and has geometric fractional chromatic number 4, combined
with the observation that their blow-up (their Theorem 1) **never leaves the field its graph lives in**: every congruence between
subsets of `F²` is an isometry of `F²`, the group those generate is solvable, and the Følner copies of `G27` stay in `F²`. So finite
unit-distance graphs over `ℚ(√3,√11)` have fractional chromatic numbers arbitrarily close to 4 (and independence ratios
arbitrarily close to `1/4`, never below). Proof: `plans/FS56-field-blowup.md` (hand). **Not kernel-checked** beyond Theorem B.

**Prior art that constrains the claim.** Almost all of it is theirs: the geometric fractional chromatic number (`[AmbrusEtAl2023]`),
the blow-up and `G27` with its rational witness (`[MatolcsiEtAl2025]`). Ours is the field-internal reading of the blow-up and the
upper bound. Their witness was **re-verified here** by an independent exact checker (`tools/fs56_gf_check.py`: the graph rebuilt in
`ℚ(√3,√11)` arithmetic, every congruence decided by an exact isometry test, all 182,304 independent sets enumerated; CERTIFIED, with
five planted faults refused — `data/fs56/g27/`). **Searched for a contradicting or earlier claim** on 2026-09-16: every work citing
`[CranstonRabern2015]` (Semantic Scholar, 11 records; OpenAlex, 8) and `[MatolcsiEtAl2025]` (Semantic Scholar, 4; OpenAlex's preprint
record, 0); none addresses a field plane.

**Status:** not written up. It would sit naturally in FS-20's manuscript beside Theorem B, since the upper bound is Theorem B.

### 1.7 The chromatic number of the plane over real quadratic fields — thirteen fields answered

> **Benda–Perles, Open Problem 11.6** (`[Soifer2024]` ch. 11): *"Find the chromatic number of `ℚ²(√2)` and, in general, of any algebraic
> extension of `ℚ²`."* Also `[Fischer1990]` §1: *"whether some algebraic criterion can be found to determine the chromatic number of `F²`
> where `F` is a subfield of `ℝ`, of finite degree over `ℚ`."*
>
> **And for quadratic fields by name** — `[Moorhouse2010slides]` (found 2026-09-19, FS-61's gate; read in the PDF's text layer): *"We are
> especially interested in how much the chromatic number can grow for quadratic extensions. … What can we say about `χ(K²)` when
> `K = ℚ(√d)`, `d ≥ 2` a squarefree integer? … If `d ≢ 47, 59 or 83 mod 84`, then `χ(K²) ≤ 4`. **What about `χ(ℚ(√47)²)`?**"* The
> bracket `4 ≤ χ(ℚ(√47)²) ≤ 5` below is a partial answer to that question; FS-61's census (`plans/FS61-quadratic-census.md`) is the
> answer to the general one as far as finite witnesses reach.

**Our answer, field by field.** Below four, the classification is prior art: `χ = 2` iff `d ≢ 3 (mod 4)` (`[Johnson1987]`,
`[Fischer1990]` Thm 8) and `χ = 3` for `d ≡ 3, 7 (mod 12)` (`[Fischer1990]` Thm 9 and the sentence after it). So the only real quadratic
fields whose plane **can** need four are the squarefree `d ≡ 11 (mod 12)` — forty of them below 500 — and this project has now
certified a finite graph forcing four colours over **thirteen** of the forty:

| | fields | the matching upper bound, all prior art |
|---|---|---|
| **`χ(ℚ(√d)²) = 4`** — exact values | **11**, 23, **59**, 71, 95, **119**, 191, **359**, **431** | the dyadic prime, inert in `K` with residue field `𝔽₄`, for `d ≡ 3 (mod 8)` (`[Fischer1990]` Thm 9); the residue plane at 7 otherwise (`[Moorhouse2010]` Thm 8.1, Cor. 8.3) |
| **`4 ≤ χ(ℚ(√d)²) ≤ 5`** — brackets | 47, **143**, **311**, **335** | the residue plane at 11 |

*(Bold: first proved by FS-61's census. `d = 239` also has a refusing ball, but it is a **solver claim** — its certification is
resumable and not finished, `data/fs61/records/phase-F.md` §3.1a — and is not counted above.)*

**Only the lower bounds are ours**, and `ℚ(√11)`'s is the only one the Lean kernel has seen
(`four_le_chromaticNumber_planeOver_eleven`, 204 points, triangle-free, standard three axioms — FS-58's *chain* witness, a different
graph from the census's 189-point direct one). Every other field rests on `tools/fs58_check_witness.py`: standard library only,
importing nothing from `tools/`, re-deriving each edge in exact integer arithmetic and replaying the LRAT refutation, with twelve
planted faults each refused for its named reason and the same files refused over a neighbouring field — plus a second opinion in exact
`Fraction` arithmetic with three further solvers, an explicit verified 4-colouring, and the product owner's own decorrelated recheck.
Certificates: `data/fs58/witness-*/` (FS-58: 11, 23, 47, 71, 95, 191) and `data/fs61/witness-*/` (FS-61: all thirteen, from its own
ball selection, two of them smaller objects than FS-58's). Records: `plans/FS58-four-colours.md` §R-B3, §R-D2, §R-B5, §C;
`plans/FS61-quadratic-census.md` §R; `data/fs61/census.md`.

**What is answered and what is not.** Moorhouse's question about `ℚ(√47)` gets a bracket, not a value. His general question — *how much
can the chromatic number grow for quadratic extensions* — is answered as far as finite witnesses reach: **at least four for thirteen
fields, and no real quadratic field is known to need five.** The census's candidate class theorem (*every squarefree `d ≡ 11 (mod 12)`
needs four*) is **not proved**; 27 of the 40 fields are open, each with the kind of null recorded. And `ℚ(√167)`, where this project's
local–global conjecture predicts six or more, is still at a proven three (FS-59).

**Prior art that constrains the claim.** The upper bounds and the odd cycles are theirs — and the lower bound 3 is older than this
file said: **`[Fischer1990]` Thm 8 gives `χ ≥ 3` for every `N ≡ 3 (mod 4)`, and the sentence after his Thm 9 states `χ(ℚ(√N)²) = 3`
for `N ≡ 3, 7 (mod 12)`** (re-read 2026-09-19); `[Moorhouse2010]` Thm 8.6 and `[Madore2015]` (for `ℚ(√7)`) re-derive it. His Thm 10
also separates the two halves of the four-colour family: an additive 4-colouring for `N ≡ 3 (mod 8)`, none with few colours for
`N ≡ −1 (mod 24)` — and **the census's lower bounds cut ACROSS that division rather than along it**: `d = 11` and `d = 59` are both
`≡ 3 (mod 8)` and both land by the direct route, which is also what refuted the census's own reading R-mod8. FS-58's §PA searched for any lower bound above three for a real quadratic field — combinatorial geometry, number
theory, spectral graph theory; citation walks of `[Fischer1990]`, `[Johnson1987]`, `[BachocEtAl2014]`, `[MatolcsiEtAl2025]` in two
databases — and found none; FS-61's §PA added zbMATH author listings and abstract searches in Fischer's own vocabulary, and found none
either (it found `[Fischer1994]`, which is about `ℚ(√3,√11)` and is §1.1's problem, not this one);
`[Fischer1990]`'s Example 4 on `ℚ(√167)` is about additive colourings only. **Unread and the likeliest places
for a contrary claim:** `[Johnson2006]` (the compendium, which by `[Johnson2010]`'s own words discusses *"distance graphs
`G(ℚ(√m)², D)` for square-free integers `m > 1`"* — exactly these planes) and `[Johnson2000]`, the 2000 status report. After
`[Fischer1994]`, no claim of novelty in this lineage should go out unread.

**Status:** `ℚ(√11)`'s lower bound kernel-checked; the other twelve tools-certified (their kernel forms are FS-60); the upper bounds hand /
prior art. Not written up. `ℚ(√167)`, where the local–global conjecture predicts at least six, is not answered (FS-59).

## §2 — Answered in one direction, or with a caveat that must travel

### 2.1 Type-M constructions with other rotations

> **`[Parts2020a]`** §4, verbatim: *"Working constructions of type M with other rotations are not known
> yet."* Every published construction of the record's family uses one rotation, `ρ₁₅ = (7 + i√15)/8`.

**What we have: a candidate list where the literature has none.** A rotation carrying cross edges between
radii `a` and `b` must adjoin `√D` with `D = ab − k²` and `k = (a + b − 1)/2`, all in `K`; when `D` is a square
in `K` the union stays in `K²` and Theorem B four-colours it. So the enumeration is exact and finite. On one
873-point Moser-lattice base, **1,336 rotation classes escape `K`, and 412 admit the ≥ 2 cross-edge kinds Parts
calls essential** — the record's own rotation among them, carrying both of its known kinds. Forty-six seconds,
no floating point in any verdict (`tools/typem.py`, `data/m62/rotations-moser-r2.05.json`).

**The caveats that must travel.** Admissibility is necessary, not sufficient: no claim is made that any of the
other 411 yields a 5-chromatic object, and that is the search. The count belongs to that base's radius
spectrum; the list has not been re-run on Parts's own base (his §5.6 closed form, 1,603 points,
`typem.parts_lattice`). On that base the record's rotation admits seven radius families, and Parts uses two.

**Status:** unconsumed — consuming the list is FS-30 (rank 2, `plans/STUB-consume-rotation-list.md`). Not
written up.

### 2.2 *(retired — not a posed question)*

The clique-versus-chromatic gap on finite Euclidean planes is not a row. `[Krebs2022]`, read in full, poses no
question about it; its `χ > ω` content is Prop. 6.1 (`χ ≥ ω + 1` when `ω ∤ qⁿ`), weaker than what the kernel
holds at every usable prime — it gives `χ ≥ 4` at `p = 11`, where `χ = 5` is kernel-checked. How large the gap
is stays open and ours to work (`CLAUDE.md` G44), outside this ledger; the finite-plane values the literature
did pose are §1.5 and §3. The number is kept so that it is not reused.

### 2.3 Which planes over real multiquadratic fields are 2-colourable

> **`[Krebs2022]`** Remark 2.3, verbatim, on fields of characteristic zero: *"It might be interesting, however,
> to try to find necessary and sufficient conditions for UD(F,n) to be bipartite for such fields."*
>
> **`[Fischer1990]`** §1, verbatim: *"This still begs the general question, however, whether some algebraic
> criterion can be found to determine the chromatic number of F² where F is a subfield of R, of finite degree
> over Q."*
>
> Bearing on the same family: **`[BendaPerles2000]`** Open Problem 11.6 (via `[Soifer2024]` ch. 11; restated in
> `[GrahamTressler2010]` §3), *"Find the chromatic number of ℚ²(√2) and, in general, of any algebraic extension of
> ℚ²."*

**What we have: the answer at the value 2, in dimension 2, for real multiquadratic fields, as a hand theorem.**
For a real multiquadratic field `F`, `χ(F²) = 2` exactly when no odd square class of `F` is `≡ 3 (mod 4)` —
equivalently, when the 2-adic image of `F`'s square classes contains neither `[−1]` nor `[3]`.
- **"If":** conjugation then lies in the inertia group of the prime above 2 in `F(i)`, and an additive functional to
  `𝔽₂` two-colours the plane (`plans/FIELD-WALL-THEOREM.md` §10.2; checked on all 16 subgroups of the 2-adic group,
  `reference/ChatGPT-multiquadratic/VERIFICATION.md` row 4). The mechanism is **kernel-checked** in valuation-subring
  form (`colorable_two_unitDistance_induce_of_valuationSubring`, ledger row 63). That a field meeting the criterion
  supplies the valuation subring is **not** in the kernel (FS-37).
- **"Only if":** an odd class `N ≡ 3 (mod 4)` puts `√N` in `F`, and `ℚ(√N)²` already carries an odd cycle
  (`[Fischer1990]` Theorem 8).

**The caveats that must travel.**
- It answers Krebs's remark for `n = 2` and real multiquadratic `F` only, and Fischer's question only at the value 2.
  Which such planes are 3-colourable is untouched.
- The quadratic case of the iff is exactly `[Fischer1990]` Theorem 8. The mechanism is also Woodall's `χ(ℚ²) = 2`
  (`[Woodall1973]`, second-hand), `[Moorhouse2010]` Theorem 7.1 (odd degree) and `[Madore2015]` Proposition 3.9
  (`ℚ(√2)²`). What is new, if anything, is the multiquadratic iff.
- **Two works that could contain the multiquadratic iff are unread:** `[AxenovichEtAl2014]`, whose abstract names
  "extensions of the rational plane" and which is not held, and `[Johnson2006]`, which discusses `ℚ(√m)²` and is not
  held. Until both are read, no novelty is claimed.
- Nobody citing `[Krebs2022]` has answered the remark (OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar, 2026-09-11).

**Status:** answered for a class of fields, as a hand theorem with its mechanism in the kernel. Not written up;
candidate material for FS-20.

## §3 — Adjacent and open

*Questions others posed in writing that we have not answered but are unusually well placed to.*

- **The minimum order of a 5-chromatic unit-distance graph.** `[ExooIsmailescu2019]`, concluding section:
  *"What is the minimum order of a 5-chromatic unit distance graph in the plane?"* The record is
  `[Parts2020a]`'s 509 vertices. M62 is the search to beat it (paused for M63); M63 has reproduced Parts's
  reduction stage on every attested number, and M64 explained his overcompression obstruction (ledger
  row 62). Best object here: 559 vertices, census-certified.
- **The smallest field whose plane needs five colours.** `[ExooIsmailescu2019]`, right after the question of
  §1.1 row 5: *"More generally, what is the smallest subfield of E2 that requires 5 colors?"* `ℚ(√3,√11)` does
  not (Theorem B). The fields known here to need exactly five — `ℚ(√3,√5,√11)`, `ℚ(√3,√11,√247)`,
  `ℚ(√3,√11,√23)` (ledger rows 47, 52–53) — all have degree 8. Any field needing five must break the 2-adic
  wall (`CLAUDE.md` G1, G35), which screens candidates by congruence; whether a field of smaller degree needs
  five has not been examined here.
- **The smallest RING that admits a 5-chromatic single-distance graph.** `[Polymath16wikiMain]`, the main
  Polymath16 wiki page, under "Further questions", posted during the project's active period (2018) and
  unanswered in print since: *"If we view the plane as the complex plane, what is the smallest ring that
  admits a 5-chromatic single-distance graph? Every single-distance graph in the Eisenstein integers and
  Gaussian integers is 2-chromatic. David Speyer suggests looking at `ℤ[(1+√−71)/2]` next."* **We have not
  answered it.** It is the **ring-shaped sibling** of `[ExooIsmailescu2019]`'s smallest-*subfield* question
  listed above, and sharper for our purposes in one specific way: *"a single-distance graph **in** a ring"*
  is the full-module framing — every point of the ring, not a lattice generated from chosen unit vectors —
  which is the framing M63 phase H3 adopts and which, by that phase's prior-art gate
  (`plans/M63-phaseH3-prior-art.md` §PA.2), **no published construction has ever used**. Speyer's own next
  case, `ℤ[(1+√−71)/2]`, appears never to have been tried. Two searches found no published answer; the
  wiki's "Code and data" Dropbox has never been opened here and is where a private attempt would sit.

- **`χ(𝔽₁₇²) ∈ {5, 6, 7}`** — `[Moorhouse2010]` Table 6.1; open, and never touched here (289 vertices, against
  the 529 of the plane at 23). A 6 would answer the first digit (`CLAUDE.md` G48: is any finite plane exactly
  6-chromatic?), though `17 ≡ 1 (mod 4)` is never a usable prime, so it bears on no field's sieve.
- *Closed by others, listed so it is not re-closed:* **`d(6) = 1`** — `[deGreyParts2022]` §4 asks whether a
  more general polygonal tiling could reach it; `[SokolovVoronov2025]` answers no.

## §4 — The deferral, stated so this file cannot be mistaken for a publication plan

Everything here except §1.1 and §1.2, which are FS-20's manuscript, is **deferred**. The standing policy
(`CLAUDE.md` § The mission) defers write-ups and upstreaming; the bar clears on χ(ℝ²) ≥ 6 or its refutation,
on Kooshi's call for a certified 5-chromatic object of our own, and on a result answering a question the
literature has independently and repeatedly posed — which has fired once, for §1.1.

**A row entering §1 is not authorisation to write it up.** It is Kooshi's call, every time.

**Maintenance.** Rewrite a row in place at every milestone close that answers or partly answers a posed
question, and whenever a prior-art pass finds a new posing of a row's question or new prior art that
constrains it — §1.1's case rests on its count of postings.

## The comparison ruling 2 asks for, and the owner's decision: NOT RUN

**What was already spent on exactly this ask** (`UD(𝔽₂₃, 2)`, 529 points of degree 24, five colours, refusal by a SAT solver):
* one CaDiCaL solve through `pysat`, triangle pinned, **no precedence constraint on the two free colours, no cubes**: no verdict
  in 14,400 s (`data/fplane/chi-p23-k5.json`; its own note says "a decision needs cube-and-conquer");
* an equivariant sweep, 5,000,000 conflicts per row: no rotationally symmetric five-colouring, the informative rows undecided
  (`data/fplane/equivariant-p23-k5.json`);
* the finding side: 1,800 s × 6 threads, best 229 monochromatic edges of 6,348 (`data/fs53/PHASE-A.md`).
So about 5 core-hours on the refusing side: an 8 h × 32-thread run would **not** duplicate effort in size.

**But the instrument has been tried at scale one plane down, and it did not converge** (`data/fs53/runs/q17-cube-*`): the same
tool (`tools/fplane_sat_cube.py`, colour cubes with adaptive splitting) on `UD(𝔽₁₇, 2)` at five colours — 289 points, a smaller
instance — ran **22,649 s on 8 workers (≈ 50 core-hours)**: 7,500 cubes decided, 5,271 split, **28,063 still outstanding and not
falling**, depth 27; its depth pricing projected `≥ 31.6 h` to `≥ 1,400 h` on 24 threads, every figure a lower bound because cubes
were censored at the cap. (17's answer is not known to be a refusal, so that sweep could not have been expected to close; what it
measures is the instrument's splitting on this family: it does not converge.) At 23 the instance is 1.8× the points, the refusal
is a counting fact (`α ≤ 105` against the 106 five colours need — and the best set ever found is 87), and counting facts are what
resolution proofs are worst at.

**And the run has no consumer that could land.** (i) The fact it would establish — five colours refused at level one of 23 — is
already an exact rational certificate, checked by an independent checker and re-run by the owner (row 71). An LRAT would add a
second proof of a certified fact. (ii) Its only other purpose was to unlock the level-TWO refusal ask, which by the projection
argument (note §5) is at least as long, on a 279,841-point graph with 17.5 M clauses; at 11 the conflict count grew ×8 per level
and the cost per conflict grows with the formula.

**Decision (the owner's, under ruling 2's "greatest chance of success" test): plan §I7 (b), (c) and (d) are NOT run in this
milestone.** The chance of success is low on the measured precedent, and success would buy nothing not already certified. The
ℚ(√167) row stays numerics-only, as the plan's §Q4 already says. *What would change this:* a lookahead cuber (`march_cu`) pricing
sample at `(17, 5)` that converges where colour cubes did not — a half-hour experiment worth making only if FS-17 (the first digit
at 23) is opened as its own milestone, where a level-one refusal by SAT has a consumer.

**Where the overnight compute goes instead** — to the two asks that can land and that nothing certifies yet: (a) the all-levels
certificate's semidefinite solve at 11 (ruling 1; the GPU's job once the relaxation is built and its `p = 7` reading passes), and
(b) level two at 19 with four colours (plan §I5: level one first, precedence constraint, cubes across the cores only if a single
solve does not decide).

## 2026-09-21 — a ruling of the owner's OVERTURNED by a measurement: the ℚ(√167) row is re-opened

**What I had ruled** (plan §Q4; the interim report; this file above): certifying the all-levels bound at 23 with five colours
(`U < 529/5 = 105.8`) was "out of reach for any certifier we own", because any certificate must in particular prove
`α(UD(𝔽₂₃,2)) < 105.8` and FS-54's level-3 relaxation read 105.43 there — a margin of 0.4. I repeated it to Kooshi twice.

**What the run at 19 measured** (Phase 2C's go/no-go, float, untrusted): the second-level moment relaxation of `B₁(19)` reads
**≈ 77.5** — below FS-62's level-3 value **80.59** for the pure independence number, which the plan's §19 and my brief both said it
"cannot be below". So this relaxation is simply *stronger* than the one my impossibility argument rested on (at 11 it had already
read 28.20 against the three-point bound's 29.18, and I had drawn the wrong moral — I compared it with the wrong rung). **The
argument "it must beat 105.43" was never an argument that it cannot.**

**Consequences.** (i) Once `cert-p19.json` is `CERTIFIED`, `U` is also an upper bound on level one's own independence number:

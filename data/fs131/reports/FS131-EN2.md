What was found, in plain words.

First question. The deduction takes one thing from Shannon–McMillan–Breiman: a set of measure near full on which no atom of the n-th refinement is smaller than e^{−βn}, for every large n. The asker's scheme gets that at one scale and then needs it along a sequence of scales. I computed what the sequence must satisfy: two runs of the pigeonhole at heights M₁ ≤ M₂ and thinnesses δ₁ ≥ δ₂ can be compared only when (16ε)^{[L:ℚ]}·C_Λ·M₁³·M₂³·δ₁ < 1 (for X01D's pair M, M² at K = 16 this is its M^{−7}; for M, M^c it needs K > 3 + 3c). That forces rate × scale at each step to be at most a constant times the previous scale, and under that constraint the Markov bounds are summable exactly when Σ H_n/n² converges. An explicit invariant measure of entropy zero has H_n ≥ c·n/log n (a mixture of periodic orbits of de Bruijn words), so the scheme is not a proof. A second model shows the three facts the scheme reads — the information functions are non-negative, non-decreasing in n and of mean o(n) — are consistent with no good point at all; what a proof must read in addition is the invariance of the measure, across all lengths at once.

What does prove the needed set, with a short proof: choose ONE block length k at which, by Markov's inequality on the terms of H_k, all but a small fraction of the mass sits on at most e^{γk} names; the maximal ergodic inequality for the indicator of the bad set gives a set of measure near one on which, for every n at once, at most a fraction θ of the positions up to n are bad; the name of length n of such a point is a concatenation of good k-blocks and a few single letters, so there are at most e^{βn/2} such names; atoms smaller than e^{−βn} among them have total mass at most e^{−βn/2}, a convergent series. No limit is taken, nothing is assumed ergodic. Lemma 3.1′ then stands word for word, and Steps 3–7 and EF_F⁺ are untouched.

Second question. X02D § 3.2 cites the uniqueness of the ergodic decomposition at ll. 205 and 209 (l. 225 is in § 3.3, the second route, not on the path). At l. 205 the conclusion it yields — that U permutes the components — is read by no later step: step 2 gets the invariance of the non-Haar part from the formula (ν − a·m)/(1 − a). At l. 209 what is consumed is that the non-Haar components are not Haar. Both disappear once the two parts are named for what they are: the absolutely continuous and the singular part of the measure with respect to Haar measure m. The singular part is U-invariant because the Lebesgue decomposition is unique; the absolutely continuous part is a multiple of m because m is ergodic; and for each admissible Γ the canonical Γ-components of the singular part charge an m-null set fully, so they are not m, and Lemma Γ with the mixture lemma gives entropy zero. The decomposition names no Γ, so one a and one τ′ serve all of them — which is what the ergodic decomposition for U (step 5) was there to arrange. A variant found while reading the release's statements needs still less: take a to be the largest c with c·m ≤ τ outright; then neither the Lebesgue decomposition nor the ergodicity of Haar measure is read (EN2-PE2-MAX).

## Definitions

Those of `data/fs136/claims.md` (a piece; a family of pieces at a point; inner-pure, outer-pure and barred at a far point;
a corner, a labelled corner, a colouring that *has* one; inner, outer and crossing for a corner; the corners at a point
cut by unit circles; forces, empties, the forced closure), each at its dated line there, and one paragraph more, from the
card:

**What the count is, in the held words.** Let `c` have at `x` a finite set `F` of labelled corners filling a neighbourhood of `x`, and let `w = x + e(θ)`. A label of `F` is *inner-pure at θ* if every corner of `F` with that label is inner at `w` (C136-CLASS decides it), *outer-pure* likewise. Write `I(θ)` for the labels of no corner of `F` together with the labels inner-pure at `θ`, and `O(θ)` the same with outer: by C136-TABLE at one member these are the only labels almost every point near `w` inside (outside) `S(x)` can have. Call `I(θ)`, `O(θ)` the **palettes** of `x`, and the set of `θ` at which a label is inner-pure its **window**. A side is *forced* where the palette is one label (C136-FORCED), *empty* where it is none, *free* otherwise. A collar is an arc on which the palette is the one absent label.

## The claims, in the card's order (posed 20:10 UTC; none derived)

  **C137-BUDGET** (the card's claim 1: what the windows sum to; posed, not derived) — *Plane geometry; no colouring.* For labelled corners at `x`, each of angle at most 180°, filling a neighbourhood of `x`: the windows' lengths sum to at most `180°·(c − 2)`, `c` the number of corners, with equality exactly when every label has one corner; so with `m` labels, one corner each, the mean over `θ` of `|I(θ)|` is `k − 1 − m/2`. *Smallest form*: one corner of angle `α` is inner at `x + e(θ)` for `θ` in an arc of length `180° − α` (its two ends by curvature, C136-CLASS). *Control, no margin*: R7 at a lattice point — three corners of 120° and three cusps, six of seven labels: mean `3`; the bowtie fragment's `q₂` — cones 30°, 120°, 120°, 6.9°, 83.1°: windows 150° + 60° + 60° + 173.1° + 96.9° = 540° = `180°·3`, mean `2.5`, and the one direction set no window covers is `(150°, 210°)`, the collar's arc. **What was run**: nothing; both by hand from angles read in `data/fs136/e2/out/bowtie12.txt` and the stub. The control to run is the engine's scan of a unit circle, summed. *Reads* corners somebody keeps; *forces* nothing. *Side*: neither — it is the instrument, and it is where six and seven differ: one label fewer on every side of every ring, whatever the corners. *Not found held.*

  **C137-THREE** (the card's claim 2: a three-label point pays only along a unit arc; posed, not derived) — At a point with three labels on three corners filling a neighbourhood, edges rays or circular arcs, a direction at which one label is inner-pure and another outer-pure is perpendicular to an edge, and that edge begins along the unit circle about `x + e(θ)`. So in C136-COVER a three-label member separates at `w` only if one of its edges begins with an arc of `S(w)`. *Smallest form*: two corners sharing a straight edge — at the direction perpendicular to it neither is inner. *It restates, by another route*, C124-BOTH's second sentence ("each has an edge that contains an arc of S(w) with an end at the member"; one mind, over C124-LONE, the step its deriver flagged) and for maps FS112-N3-COR3; what would be new is the reason — it is BUDGET's equality case at `m = 3`, where the windows sum to exactly 180° and a window meets another's antipode in one direction — and that four labels or more pay on open sets of directions. *Control, no margin*: the strip's 17 pairs with both common unit points inside (`partners-held`: "both=17"). **What was run**: nothing new; the control to run reads the paying member's edge at those 17, exactly. R7 has no three-label vertex: it is silent there, say so. *Reads* kept corners; *excludes* (payment at first order). *Side*: both.

  **C137-SIXTY** (the card's claim 3: FS116-N2-FIVERING's second clause, in measure; posed, not derived) — Let `c` have at `x` corners carrying all labels but `ℓ`, and let `N` be the directions at which no label of them is inner-pure. If `θ` and `θ + 60°` are both interior to `N` and `x + e(θ)`, `x + e(θ + 60°)` lie in the domain, the two one-sided neighbourhoods inside `S(x)` hold a set of unit pairs of positive measure with both ends `ℓ` (points at depth `s` in directions `60° + δ` apart, `s` of the order of `δ`): so no domain holds both. Hence `N` has no two interior directions 60° apart, every arc of `N` had whole is at most 60°, `N` has length at most 180°, and two adjacent one-corner labels span at most 240°. The same outside. *Smallest form*: one arc longer than 60°. *Control, no margin*: the bowtie fragment's `q₂`, where `N = (150°, 210°)` is exactly 60° and its ends `(0, 0)`, `(0, −1)` are one unit apart — it must pass with nothing to spare. **What was run**: nothing new; read from the engine's `forced-arc` line and the seed line.   **C137-OWN** (the card's claim 4: a point with every label forces its own; posed, not derived) — In C136-COLLAR put "an open arc along which `I(θ)` is one label `ℓ`" for "whose labels are all the labels but one, `ℓ`": the conclusion and the argument are the same (C136-FORCED's hypothesis never asks that `ℓ` be absent at the member), and by SIXTY's argument each such arc is at most 60°.   **C137-MEET** (the card's claim 5: where two collars meet; posed, not derived) — *A corollary of C136-TABLE on the cell.* For `x`, `y` with `0 < |x − y| < 2`, corners at each, and `w` a common unit point: on the cell at `w` on given sides of `S(x)` and `S(y)` the labels left are the two palettes' common labels. If `x` lacks exactly `a` and `y` exactly `b ≠ a`, the cell is empty unless `a` is pure for `y` on its side, or `b` for `x`, or one label is pure for both. *On the held object*: `(√3, 0)` is the common end of the outer collar arcs of `q₂` (label 4) and `q₁` (label 2), and the cell outside both circles is empty (C136-STOP-GERMS (c), read this way); at the pair's other common unit point, the origin, the inner arcs end and the cell inside both is coloured, by the label of a tile both points carry (`t1`). So with both points' corners kept neither outer arc lies whole in any domain (C136-STOP-GERMS-RADIUS). *Control, no margin*: R7 — adjacent lattice points lack different labels, and `forced-r7-window` reads "empty=0" over 432 corners at the 108 common unit points of 54 pairs: at each, one of the three alternatives must hold. **What was run**: the second deriver's control (not by me); the control to run names which alternative, corner by corner. *Reads* kept corners; *forces* nothing; *excludes*. *Side*: both — a design rule (two five-label points lacking different labels need a common pure label at both their common unit points) and the mechanism of one held stop.

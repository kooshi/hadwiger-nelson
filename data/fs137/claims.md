## Definitions

Those of `data/fs136/claims.md` (a piece; a family of pieces at a point; inner-pure, outer-pure and barred at a far point;
a corner, a labelled corner, a colouring that *has* one; inner, outer and crossing for a corner; the corners at a point
cut by unit circles; forces, empties, the forced closure), each at its dated line there, and one paragraph more, from the
card:

**What the count is, in the held words.** Let `c` have at `x` a finite set `F` of labelled corners filling a neighbourhood of `x`, and let `w = x + e(θ)`. A label of `F` is *inner-pure at θ* if every corner of `F` with that label is inner at `w` (C136-CLASS decides it), *outer-pure* likewise. Write `I(θ)` for the labels of no corner of `F` together with the labels inner-pure at `θ`, and `O(θ)` the same with outer: by C136-TABLE at one member these are the only labels almost every point near `w` inside (outside) `S(x)` can have. Call `I(θ)`, `O(θ)` the **palettes** of `x`, and the set of `θ` at which a label is inner-pure its **window**. A side is *forced* where the palette is one label (C136-FORCED), *empty* where it is none, *free* otherwise. A collar is an arc on which the palette is the one absent label.

  *1.2 Notation, stated once (for the claim list)*

  *Beside the definitions of `data/fs136/claims.md` (a corner; a labelled corner; a colouring *has* one; inner, outer and crossing at a point one unit away; inner-pure and outer-pure).*

  - `e(θ)` is the unit vector of direction `θ`. The **cone** of a corner `(x; γ₁, γ₂)` is the closed arc of directions swept counter-clockwise from the direction of `γ₁` at `x` to that of `γ₂`: one direction if the two agree and `γ₁` comes before `γ₂` in the order by signed curvature (a *cusp*), every direction if they agree and `γ₂` comes first. Its **angle** is the length of its cone, from 0° to 360°. - A finite set `F` of labelled corners at `x` **fills a neighbourhood of `x`** if it has at least two corners and, for some `ρ > 0`, the parts of its corners within `ρ` of `x` are pairwise disjoint and cover `B(x, ρ)` but for `x` and the corners' curves. Those curves are the **edges** of `F`; there are as many as corners. (The engine's `full`.) - A corner at `x` is **inner**, **outer** or **crossing at the direction `θ`** if it is so at the point `x + e(θ)`; a label of `F` is **inner-pure** (**outer-pure**) **at `θ`** if every corner of `F` with that label is inner (outer) at `θ`. - `θ` is **generic for `F`** if neither `θ + 90°` nor `θ − 90°` is the direction at `x` of an edge of `F`. - For `|w − x| = 1`, an edge of `F` **lies along `S(w)`** if near `x` it is an arc of the unit circle about `w`: in C136-CLASS's order it is equal to one of the two branches of `S(w)` at `x`.

  *1.3 The six statements, verbatim, each with its *not claimed* line (sent to the coordinator from here)*

  **C137-BUDGET.** *Statement:* Plane geometry; no colouring. Let F be a finite set of labelled corners at x that fills a neighbourhood of x, c its number of corners, m its number of labels, and θ a direction generic for F. If one cone of F contains both θ + 90° and θ − 90°, that corner is crossing at θ and the other corners of F are all inner at θ or all outer at θ. Otherwise two corners of F are crossing at θ — those whose cones contain θ + 90° and θ − 90° — and each of the other c − 2 is inner at θ or outer at θ. The corners of F inner at θ are the corners of F outer at θ + 180°. Hence, if every corner of F has angle at most 180°: at every generic θ the number of labels inner-pure at θ plus the number outer-pure at θ is at most c − 2, and it is m − 2 if F has one corner for each label; the sets of directions at which the labels of F are inner-pure have lengths that sum to at most 180°·(c − 2), and to 180°·(m − 2) if F has one corner for each label; and then, among k labels in all, the number of labels that are labels of no corner of F or inner-pure at θ has mean k − 1 − m/2 over θ. *Not claimed:* anything about a colouring; that a label counted here is present on that side in any colouring (a palette is an upper bound: the kernel lane's finding 8); the bound by the number of labels when a label has two corners — with corners a, c, b, c in cyclic order of angles 10°, 170°, 10°, 170° the labels a and b are both pure wherever the two c's hold the tangent directions, and the three labels' sets of directions sum to 340°, more than 180°·(3 − 2); the classes at the finitely many directions that are not generic (curvature decides them: C136-CLASS), so nothing about which end points a set of directions contains; that the card's "one label fewer at six than at seven" is more than the identity that the same corners leave k − m labels absent.

  **C137-THREE.** *Statement:* Plane geometry; no colouring. Let F be a finite set of labelled corners at x that fills a neighbourhood of x, θ a direction, w = x + e(θ), and s the number of the two branches of S(w) at x that are edges of F. Then at most 2 − s corners of F are crossing at θ. If s = 0 and F has a corner inner at θ and a corner outer at θ, then two corners of F are crossing at θ and F has at least four corners. So if F has three corners, and one label of F is inner-pure at θ and another outer-pure at θ, an edge of F lies along S(w). *Not claimed:* anything about a colouring; that an edge along S(w) gives an inner-pure and an outer-pure label (it makes no corner beside it crossing; the labels beside it may recur at other corners); the case of three labels on four corners — a, c, b, c in cyclic order, the two c's holding the tangent directions, has a inner-pure and b outer-pure on an open set of directions and no edge along any unit circle; anything for edges that are not rays or circular arcs; that C124-BOTH or FS112-N3-COR3 follows from this alone (each also needs a count that makes both members separate).

  **C137-SIXTY.** *Statement:* About weak colourings that have the named corners; no weak six-colouring is known to have one. Let c be a weak k-colouring of an open set U, x a point, F a finite set of labelled corners at x that c has, and ℓ a label. Let N_in be the set of directions θ at which every label other than ℓ is the label of a corner of F that is not inner at θ, and N_out the same set with "outer" for "inner". If θ and θ + 60° both lie in N_in ∪ N_out, then U does not contain both x + e(θ) and x + e(θ + 60°). Hence, if U contains S(x): an arc of directions contained in N_in, or in N_out, has length at most 60°; N_in has total length at most 120°, and so has N_out; and if F fills a neighbourhood of x and its labels are all the labels but ℓ, the cones of any two adjacent corners of F span at most 240° together. *Not claimed:* that a weak colouring has such corners; that a label of a palette is present anywhere; anything for two directions 60° apart at which different labels are forced (FS116-Y2-SIXFRAG's centre: six directions 60° apart, six labels); anything when U misses one of the two points — at FS114-Y2-BOWTIE's five-label points the arcs are exactly 60° and their end directions are not in N_in; that N_in is non-empty for a given F; that 120° is attained (no object held attains it; the card's 180° is not the best); the map entry FS116-N2-FIVERING's conclusion, which is about arcs of the boundary on the ring and says more for maps.

  **C137-OWN.** *Statement:* About weak colourings that have the named corners; no weak six-colouring is known to have one. Let c be a weak k-colouring of an open set U, x a point, F a finite set of labelled corners at x that c has, ℓ a label, and A a connected subset of S(x) ∩ U such that at every point w of A every label other than ℓ is the label of a corner of F that is not inner at w. Then there is a connected open subset V of U inside S(x), almost every point of which has the label ℓ, such that every point w of A has a radius η > 0 with every point of B(w, η) inside S(x) in V; and every corner of F with the label ℓ is inner at every point of A. With "outer" in place of "inner" there is such a V outside S(x), holding the points of B(w, η) outside S(x), and every corner of F with the label ℓ is outer at every point of A. *Not claimed:* that a weak colouring has such corners; any width — η depends on w and on c and is bounded below by nothing; anything at a point of the closure of A that is not in A; **that a set A of more than one point exists on any held object when ℓ is a label of F — none is held** (FS116-Y2-SIXFRAG's centre gives six single points, R7's lattice points none; the arcs of FS114-Y2-BOWTIE are C136-COLLAR's, with ℓ absent); that V and the corner of F with the label ℓ belong to one piece (the map entry FS116-N2-SIXRING says so, in the non-Siamese class); what V bars one unit further on.

  **C137-MEET.** *Statement:* About weak colourings that have the named corners; no weak six-colouring is known to have one. Let c be a weak k-colouring of an open set U, w a point of U, x_1, …, x_n (n ≥ 1) distinct points with |x_i − w| = 1, F_i a finite set of labelled corners at x_i that c has, and σ_i a side of S(x_i), inside or outside. Let Π_i be the set of the labels that are labels of no corner of F_i, together with the labels of F_i that are inner-pure at w if σ_i is inside and outer-pure at w if σ_i is outside. Then there is η > 0 such that almost every point of B(w, η) lying on the side σ_i of S(x_i) for every i has a label in Π_1 ∩ … ∩ Π_n. So if n = 2, the labels of F_1 are all the labels but a, those of F_2 all the labels but b, a ≠ b, and for every η > 0 some point of B(w, η) lies on the side σ_1 of S(x_1) and on the side σ_2 of S(x_2), then a is in Π_2, or b is in Π_1, or some label of both F_1 and F_2 is in Π_1 and in Π_2. *Not claimed:* anything beyond C136-TABLE, of which this is a rewording in the words of the card — no new mathematics; that a label of Π_1 ∩ … ∩ Π_n is present; that a non-empty intersection gives a colouring; that the three alternatives exclude one another; the card's reading that "neither outer arc lies whole in any domain" (it is C136-STOP-GERMS-RADIUS's, not re-derived here).

  **C137-RING.** *Statement:* About weak colourings that have the named corners; no weak six-colouring is known to have one. Let c be a weak k-colouring of an open set U, x a point of U, F a finite set of labelled corners at x that c has and that fills a neighbourhood of x, y a point with |y − x| = 1, and G a finite set of labelled corners at y that c has. Then no corner of F that is crossing at y has the label of a corner of G. Hence, if F has one corner for each of its labels and every corner of F has angle less than 180°: G has at most k − 2 + s labels, s being the number of the two branches of S(y) at x that are edges of F; and for distinct points y_1, …, y_n of S(x) and finite sets G_j of labelled corners at y_j that c has, the numbers of labels of the G_j sum to at most (k − 2)·n + a, where a is the number of edges of F that lie along the unit circle about one of the y_j, and a is at most the number of labels of F. *Not claimed:* that a weak colouring has corners anywhere; **an exclusion of every labelled ring at six — the count is met at six by many assignments (seven points on straight two-label edges meet it with room), so it is not the inequality "no assignment meets at six" that the card asks for**; that a ring meeting the count exists as a colouring, or fits at first order; the count for a label with two corners at x (the bound is then by the distinct labels of the crossing corners, at least one); anything at a point of the ring where the colouring has no corners; the sum over the points of a lattice patch beyond the centre's own ring; a value of the chromatic number of the plane — with it, the announcement rule's sentence: it would bear on seven only with a statement that every weak six-colouring has such points, which nobody holds.

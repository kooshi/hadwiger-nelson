## 1. The answer

**Neither graph lies in ℚ(√3,√5). Both contain it strictly, and in both the √5 enters ONLY through the closing rotation
ρ₁₅ = (7 + i√15)/8 — the stub's target rotation.** Expectation E1 is met.

| graph | vertices | coordinate field | how √3 and √5 enter | the wall in the *fabric* field |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `[Voronov2021]` series 2, case 10 (Prop. 5) | 64,513 | **ℚ(√2, √3, √5)** (his Prop. 5 states `χ(ℚ(i,√2,√3,√5)) ≥ 5`) | base `M₃` generated by `φ₀ = ζ₂₄ = (√6+√2)/4 + i(√6−√2)/4` and `φ₁ = (√6 + i√3)/3` (§6, series 2) — so **√2 is in every base point**, e.g. `ζ₂₄ ∈ M₁`; the copy is `ψM₃` with `ψ = φ₀⁻¹·(7+i√15)/8` (proof of Prop. 5) | ℚ(√2,√3): square classes 2, 3, 6 — **standing**; χ(ℚ(√2,√3)²) = 4 exactly (G55, the 18-vertex chained-rhombus graph) |
| `[Haugland2026]` | 2,131 | **ℚ(ζ₈₄)⁺(√5)**, totally real of degree 24 | the 84 unit vectors are `u_{2j} = (cos(πj/21), sin(πj/21))` and a detuned family `u_{2j+1}` at angle `π(j+θ)/21`, with θ fixed by `u₂₉ ∥ Q₀P₀ ∥ (−√3, 4cos(π/7) − 1)` (§2, after Table 1); `cos(πj/21)`, `sin(πj/21)`, `cos(π/7)`, `1/sin(2π/7)`, √3 all lie in `ℚ(ζ₈₄)⁺` (degree φ(84)/2 = 12), and 5 ∤ 84 so √5 does not; the copies `V₁, V₂` are π/3 rotations (ℚ(√3)); the spindle is `(7 + i√15)/8` (§3, the graph `G₃`) | ℚ(ζ₈₄)⁺ ⊇ ℚ(√3, √7, √21): **broken already by √7** (7 ≡ 7 mod 8); his §4 nevertheless bounds the lattice's four-colourings at ≤ 6 up to equivalence, all Klein-four-group labellings (additive colourings, G41's family) |

The other thirteen series-2 graphs of `[Voronov2021]` share the base `M₃` and so contain √2 as well; the five series-1 graphs are

My reading, unverified: a trim of Voronov's graph by Heule's clausal-proof method, hence in ℚ(√2,√3,√5). The free `[Heule2021b]`
(fetched today, `library/pdf/naw-2021-heule-easier-variants.pdf`) does not mention it. **Until the paper is read, E1 is met for the two
graphs whose coordinates are in print and OPEN for Heule's 1,441.**

## 2. What the two papers hand the milestone (read as G46 asks: what do they teach about OUR object?)

1. **Haugland's mechanism is the stub's design target, executed one field over — and it factors into one ask.** His §3, in
   three steps: (a) `G₁` (740 vertices, 3,985 edges; the 7-core of paths of length ≤ 6 from `A = (0,0)` to `B = (0,√3)` in his 84
   unit vectors) makes **`A` and `B` a virtual edge at four colours** (CaDiCaL, with `A = B` pinned, refuses; *"the unsatisfiable
   core retained variables corresponding to all vertices"*). (b) Two copies of `G₁` rotated by ±π/3 about `(∓½, √3/2)` give `G₂`
   (1,066 vertices), in which the five points `(−1,0), (0,0), (1,0), (−½,√3/2), (½,√3/2)` are pairwise constrained — seven unit
   edges, two virtual edges at √3 — except the pair `(−1,0), (1,0)` at distance 2, **which is therefore forced equal in every
   four-colouring** (five points, four colours, one free pair: G16's distance-2 pigeonhole in its cleanest form). (c) The ρ₁₅
   spindle on that pair: `G₃`, 2,131 vertices, five-chromatic. **Steps (b) and (c) are field-free given √3 and √15**, and
   ℚ(√3,√5) has both. **So FS-70 reduces to one question: is there a finite ℚ(√3,√5)-graph in which `0` and `i√3` are a
   four-colour virtual edge?** A refusal there closes the milestone by hand (two π/3 copies, the pigeonhole, one ρ₁₅ copy and an
   edge) — the stub's routes 2–4 collapse into this one ask, and it is a *harder* pin than the stub's triangle pin (`A = B` is one
   extra constraint, so the refusing object is smaller, not larger).
2. **Voronov's construction is Heule's generative recipe with the seed swapped**: `L₁₀,₂`, a minimally rigid, 4-vertex-critical
   unit-distance graph on 10 vertices (§2, Table 2; drawn in ℚ(√2,√3)²), generates `M₁` (73 points: the origin plus the
   `φ₀^a φ₁^b`, `0 ≤ a < 24`, `|b| ≤ 1`), `M₃` by iterated Minkowski sums with clipping (32,257 points), and the rotation ψ is
   found by sweeping `|z − ψ′w| = 1` with `z = 2` (his Fig. 10) — i.e. **the rotations that create a unit edge at the point 2, the
   spindle at distance 2 once more**. The ℚ(√3,√5) analogue of his seed is FS-48's chained-rhombus four-forcer (six rhombi, by
   `1/√3 = −(1 + u + ū)(ζ₁₂ + ζ̄₁₂)`, `u = (−2 + i√5)/3`) — not rigid, which his heuristic prefers; whether rigidity matters is his
   heuristic, not a theorem.
3. **Both fabrics are four-forcing over fields poorer in √5 than ours, and one of them is wall-broken.** Voronov's base field
   ℚ(√2,√3) has a standing wall and χ = 4 (G55): his fabric forces four colours *below* the residue colouring's ceiling, the de Grey
   pattern. Haugland's ℚ(ζ₈₄)⁺ contains √7, so its wall is broken and no residue colouring at 2 exists — yet his §4 finds the
   lattice's four-colourings are exactly six additive ones. That is the situation ℚ(√3,√5) is in (wall broken by 15 ≡ 7 mod 8): a
   four-colouring, if any, of the fabric is additive or nothing, and the additive family is G41/G42's measured instrument
   (`tools/additive.py`, `tools/additive_rigid.py`). **A cheap screen before any SAT ask: does the ℚ(√3,√5) unit-vector lattice
   admit an additive four-colouring (a homomorphism to (ℤ/2)² or ℤ/4 nonzero on every unit vector)?** If none exists at some
   generating set, four-colourings of thick fabric are constrained to be non-additive, which is where compulsions live.
4. **Not in either paper:** any statement about ℚ(√3,√5); any Moser-free forcer over a field with allowance five; any use of the
   chain theorem (both use the spindle, m = 2). The conjecture's prediction here is untouched by both.

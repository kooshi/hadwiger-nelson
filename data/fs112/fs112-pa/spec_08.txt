### Vertices of high valency; the six-colour vertex
@08.14 ¦ * ¦ * ¦ **Mixon's argument: no tile is edge-adjacent to six tiles in a 6-colouring** — two same-colour neighbours A, B give points `x_i, y_j` all at distance 1, a unit rhombus, contradicting `ε < 1`.
@08.15 ¦ * ¦ * ¦ De Grey: Gibbs meant vertex valency of the tiling, not of the dual; Mixon's `|x_i − y_j| = 1` fails for small tiles inside each other's annulus of exclusion (his seven-colour heptagon example).
@08.16 ¦ * ¦ * ¦ **De Grey: two same-colour tiles can share a vertex if both lie inside a Reuleaux triangle with a corner there; he proposes to recolour the Reuleaux triangle's intersection with a small disk** ("I can’t think of a way to stop such a tiling from being transformed harmlessly") — stated, not proved.
@08.17 ¦ * ¦ with the details depending on how the tile boundaries emanate from ¦ **The six-colour-vertex sketch (de Grey)**: after that transformation, six tiles at a 6-valent vertex V "must jointly cover a disk of radius 1 centred on V", boundaries meeting its edge at a regular hexagon, each tile inside a Reuleaux triangle ("unless I’m missing something really obvious"; the comment is truncated in the held text).

### Constraints: Gibbs's definition, de Grey's rules, Mixon's labels

* **G46 — The rule the finite-plane milestones taught, and its corollary for choosing work.** (Minted 2026-09-07 on Kooshi's direction, from the two milestones that ran under the frontier-set discipline; a methodological invariant in G21/G30's lineage.) M58 and M59 were each stubbed as half derivation, half object search, and in both the object half either became unnecessary or failed while the derivation half delivered: M58's planned solver runs on the big planes were settled by a classical spectral bound before they ran, and the small planes it left to the solver never returned; M59's planned search for 6-critical cores failed (a spectral refusal is not a CDCL-sized one) while classical rigidity theory settled the lift question before any core existed, and the instrument's real product was an enumerator. What did the work both times was RECOGNITION: the finite planes are Cayley graphs of an abelian group, so their spectra are character sums (Kloosterman); a residue configuration is a framework over a ring, so its lifts are first-order rigidity (Hensel, self-stresses). **The rule: name the object in the vocabulary of the classical theory it already belongs to, and let that theory decide the question's shape — possible, impossible, where — before any search runs; the search's role shrinks to producing the instances the derivation predicts.** This is G30's "derivation kills, measurement generates" with its missing step made explicit — the derivation comes from recognizing WHICH discipline's theorem the measured wall is (Kooshi's ladder of disciplines, under G30) — and the measurements that mattered were the controls that corrected the derivation's SCOPE (ω = 3 iff p ≡ 11 mod 12; the lift's primes are the unramified ones), not the searches. **The corollary for ranking the frontier set: prefer a stub whose question becomes a finite exact computation plus a named theorem over a stub that searches for an object; treat a stub with no such recognition yet (the topological and Nullstellensatz rooms) as a reading room rather than a milestone until one appears; and read a search's failure as information about the search's genre, not about the objects.** Applied at the M59 close: FS-14 (a 121-vertex computation plus a homomorphism argument) over FS-6 (an object search); and the broad question the two milestones point at — every upper bound we hold is local at one prime and every forcer hides from every small prime — minted as the local–global tightness conjecture (FS-15, `plans/M61-local-global-tightness.md`): χ(F²) equals its best local bound wherever that bound is ≤ 6 (attained at all six fields where χ is known, twice predicted before the forcer was built), whose first digit is "is any finite Euclidean plane exactly 6-chromatic" and whose bold form is χ(ℝ²) = 7. **[the rule: the project's own record, two milestones; the conjecture: six instances, no counterexample, stated to be pushed back on]** **AMENDED 2026-09-08 (G49): the rule is half a rule.

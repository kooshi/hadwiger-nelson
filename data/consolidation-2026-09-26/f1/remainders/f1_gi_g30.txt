* **G30 — Measurement generates; induction kills. The failure is never the measuring — it is promoting an inductive reading without a derivation or a registered replication behind it.** (Minted 2026-08-13, from sorting the whole record by how each claim was reached; stated in this form on Kooshi's correction, which is the load-bearing half: *"the interesting patterns that become intuited and derived show up first from measurement, so it's a catch-22."*) Sorted by provenance, the record is close to a clean dichotomy. **Derived from structure first, then measured — survived, every one**, several becoming kernel theorems: the quadrature law (26 exact censuses, zero violations), the stacking law, the criticality factorization A1, M36's boundary-tight sharpening (worked by hand in §0a before any code), M42's exact-hexagon reach whose corner values ARE the band boundaries, M25's `√3·ζ₁₂ = 1+ζ₆`, G26's reduction. **Measured first, then interpreted — died, every one**, mostly refuted by our own later work: the seam-alignment kill mechanism (0/4 on registered replication), the concentration bet, the coset census, the triple-kill population (provably empty), G20's brick-interlock reading (refuted by our own M36 theorem), G26's universality (refuted by G28), M39's "finer is worse", M39 §0e's shell-exhaustion claim, M43's P8 core-shape prediction, M31's P6 band prediction. Roughly 7-for-7 against 0-for-10. […] The rule is about what a measurement BUYS: it buys a candidate pattern, never a conclusion, and the conclusion is bought separately by a derivation or by a pre-registered replication on fresh objects. Two design consequences already applied: prefer measurements that turn a DIAL (a parameter whose behaviour is informative in both directions — G29's ladder, the hetero-dimer effort curve against overlap) over searches that hunt for a POSITIVE (whose null result is uninterpretable and has now come back flat four times); and prefer CALIBRATED test beds — ask the new question on an object whose answer is already certified, so a zero reading bounds the instrument instead of being mistaken for a fact about the world (`plans/M45-arity-floor.md` is that pattern made into a milestone). […] **A NEW FAILURE MODE, from M48 (2026-08-15): a registered prediction can carry an inductive reading in its own escape clause.** P6 registered that if the enriched object's never-observed pattern fraction came back *lower* than the witness's, that "would be the first sign the closure does something structural." It came back lower — and the reading was never available: the witness is an INDUCED subgraph, so the closure's achievable patterns on those vertices are a **subset** of the witness's own and cannot be more numerous. The gap is the SAT sampler, necessarily, and one line of derivation says so. *The rule this adds: write the escape clause to the same standard as the prediction. […] Ask of every "if it comes back the other way, that would mean X" whether X is even consistent with what is already proven about the objects.* **A SECOND FAILURE MODE, from M49 (2026-08-17): a registered NEGATIVE can rest on the wrong reading of a theorem-shaped grokking.** P4b registered that seam-field unit material "cannot do a √15 job" — reasoning from G1 (5-forcers need the field jump). But a halo does a distance-2 edge's job IN CONTEXT, with the other defects still carrying the load, and that is a far weaker demand than forcing five on its own: 34 of 37 times a one-generation seam halo met it. G1 was never contradicted (the unit part alone stayed 4-colourable); the prediction applied a statement about pure objects to an in-context question. *The rule this adds: when a prediction is derived from a grokking, check that the grokking's quantifier matches the measurement's — "no seam-field object forces five" does not imply "no seam-field halo replaces a defect".* And the opposite direction cost P7: the k = 5 removals were CHEAPER than the k = 4 ones (median one point against 8.5), because the descent finds the cheap kills first — a selection effect the registration did not name. **A THIRD FAILURE MODE, from M50 (2026-08-23): a verdict message that names a cause it did not check converts one bug into two.** The iteration-2 cover deferred 3,023 provably-killable colourings because an oversized early rung broke the rung ladder — and the defer message said "cost budget", a cause that never fired, sending the first diagnosis toward budgets while the size guard was the culprit; the results row's scope sentence simultaneously asserted the stalls "survive the whole pool", false for deferrals. The rule: error and verdict messages may name only the branch that actually fired, and a conflated stall/defer vocabulary is itself a bug (fixed with a per-item reason taxonomy in which only 'survives-whole' means what the old scope claimed). The flip side, same milestone: the always-verify discipline PAID twice in one day — a coverage-subset verify caught an exact minimizer silently swapping a kept edge out of the hitting set, and a 25 ≠ 24 banner cross-check caught a stale commit field before any certify.

**A FIFTH FAILURE MODE, from the valuation law (2026-09-09): a theorem can pass every gate — kernel-checked, standard three axioms, statement read aloud against the informal claim — and still say NOTHING, because its hypothesis is a type one of your OWN earlier theorems emptied.** `core9_plane` and `core122_plane` (M34) quantify over proper four-colourings of the plane; rung B says there are none. Both are true, both cost real work, and both are vacuous — and a stub then rested a live argument on one of them, which is how the emptiness was finally noticed, four weeks later, by someone trying to *use* it. *What makes this the sharpest instance in this entry: the defence was already written down and simply not run.* `DEBRUIJN.md` § correctness-over-plausibility says it in as many words — *"exhibit a model of your hypotheses — one concrete instance satisfying all of them — before trusting any theorem that assumes them, because a `∀` over an empty domain proves everything and means nothing"* — and WORKFLOW step 3 repeats it. **The rule this adds is mechanical, because the principle plainly was not enough: every theorem whose hypothesis is a structure (a colouring, an embedding, a certificate, a homomorphism) ships with a named inhabitant of that structure, in the same file, or a one-line note saying which theorem supplies one.** **AND SINCE 2026-09-09 IT IS RUN BY THE MACHINE** (`scripts/vacuity.sh`, § The gate): the rule was a convention enforced by memory for one day, and FS-28 then found that the *check* had three defects of its own, of which the sharpest was that its negative controls could not be run at all. **A mechanical rule is not mechanical until its failure path has been watched** — which is `G21`'s newest instance and is why the gate now ships `--self-test`. `ValuationLaw.lean` does it (the `√3` row is instantiated on `{0, √3}`), and it is the cheapest possible check — the inhabitant is usually already lying around. Composes with G21 (a negative measures its enumeration; here an *affirmative* measured its domain) and with G30's own thesis, since the failure was not a bad inference from a measurement but a statement that had no measurement behind it at all.

**A SIXTH FAILURE MODE, minted 2026-09-09 from the consolidation surveys, and it is the quietest one yet: a CORRECT INSTANCE recorded where a THEOREM was available.** Two independent surveys — one over the Lean sources, one over the whole written record — came back with the same shape thirteen times. Four primes instead of all primes (the finite planes are pairwise free at *every* prime, by an orbit argument, where the record has four tables); one radius instead of the classification (the circles whose unit chords close are exactly the unit-polygon circumradii, where the record has `r² = 1/3` and a shrug at `r² = 3`); one fabric piece instead of the size law; a solver's circulant instead of its closed form; measured directions instead of the group; a theorem stated over `ℂ` whose proof needs only a metric space; a law stated at one prime whose proof needs only a residue map. […] *The detector is mechanical, and it is not "grep for unexplained": **grep for `[measured]` next to a small finite list of instances, and for a statement whose hypotheses are narrower than its proof uses, and ask each one what the general form is.*** Stubbed as `plans/STUB-explain-the-unexplained.md` (six items) and `plans/STUB-generalize-the-kernel.md` (seven). Composes with the fifth mode above — that one is a `∀` over an empty domain, this one is a `∀` over a domain smaller than the proof needs.

**The repair has a failure mode of its own, found on 2026-09-09 while running it (FS-24 G1/G7a).** Widening a theorem's hypotheses can widen them past the point where they do any work. The planned shape for the `CriticalSet` plane corollary read the two radii *off the critical vertex* — and at read-off radii the `Forces` and `CriticalSet` hypotheses are **dead**, because every point of the plane is a glue point of any two distinct points at its own two distances. That statement would have been true, kernel-checked, axiom-clean, and empty. **So the sixth mode's repair inherits the fifth mode's guard**: after generalizing, delete each hypothesis once and confirm the proof breaks. Here the fix was to quantify over the drawing's distance *set* rather than over nothing, which is where the content was. A second, smaller instance the same day: the metric-space form of `criticalSet_eq_empty_of_forces_of_two_mul_lt_dist` needs `0 ≤ M`, which the unit instance supplied silently — a generalization can *lose* a hypothesis the special case was getting for free.

**A sixteenth instance, and it closed the same day it was minted (2026-09-09, package P4).** The record's *"the sealing planes at 3, 7, 11, 19 are ALL pairwise free"* is four tables where one rotation argument covers every prime. Two things are worth keeping from how it closed. **First, the plan that named it over-specified the proof**: `plans/STUB-explain-the-unexplained.md` §E1 set out five steps whose fourth was an orbit–stabiliser count over the `(p+1)/2` circle-missing lines — correct, numerically verified, and **unnecessary**, because the argument needs one rotation that moves the period line rather than a transitive action on all of them. A derivation written down before it is formalized can carry incidental structure exactly as a measurement can. **Second, the theorem it produced was one hypothesis away from being vacuous** — `Forces` is true of every pair on a graph with no proper colouring — which is the fifth failure mode waiting inside the sixth; the shipped counterexample at `p = 3, k = 2` is what keeps them apart.

**A seventeenth instance, found while generalizing (2026-09-09, P6), and it is the sharpest kind: a proof that names the wrong mechanism.** `ValuationLaw.lean` cancelled a unit vector from a residue by *"`𝔽₄` has no zero divisors, by exhaustion over sixteen pairs"* — true, decided, and not the reason. The reason is that a ring homomorphism's **kernel is an ideal**, and an ideal absorbs units, which needs nothing at all of the residue ring. The sixteen-case `decide` is a correct instance standing where a one-line theorem was available, and no gate can see it: the statement is right, the proof is right, the hypothesis is merely stronger than the argument. *The detector, added to the mode's own list: a finite `decide` used to discharge a step that has a name in ring theory.*

**A SEVENTH FAILURE MODE, from the vacuity sweep of 2026-09-09 (FS-26), and it is the fifth mode's mechanism rather than another instance of it: a theorem whose HYPOTHESIS is implied by the EMPTINESS OF ITS OWN CONCLUSION'S DOMAIN.** The shape is `¬ Colorable(G⁺) k → ∀ C : G.Coloring (Fin k), P C` with `G ≤ G⁺`. If `G` itself refuses `k` colours then so does `G⁺`, so the hypothesis is free — and the conclusion quantifies over an empty type, so it is free too. **Both halves degenerate at the same moment**, which is exactly why no gate catches it: the statement is true, the proof is correct, the hypothesis is discharged by a genuine certificate, and the conclusion is empty. `core9_plane` was an instance of this, not a one-off. Four sites carry the shape (`Pigeonhole.lean:82`, `Fan.lean:217`, `Forcer.lean:106/117/137`, `AdditiveRigid.lean:100`), and it was found **twice independently, in two different layers**, which is what makes it a mode rather than a remark. *The remedy already exists in this repository three times over and is never the entry point instances travel*: the two-way form whose right side demands a colouring (`Pigeonhole.colorable_append_iff_exists_avoiding`); folding the witness into the checked `Bool` so the certificate carries a proper colouring of its own graph (`NarrowFan.lean`'s `NarrowCert.check` — the pattern to copy); and taking the inhabitant as a *hypothesis* so the trap is closed by the signature and travels with the theorem (`ResidueObstruction.lean`). *And the detector exists too*: `Arity.arityFloor_eq_zero_iff` makes vacuity a **value** — `arityFloor G k = 0 ↔ ¬ G.Colorable k` — which is the sixth mode applied to this project's own instrument, since it is a theorem being used as a per-tool convention.

**A FOURTH FAILURE MODE, from the prior-art check of 2026-09-08 (G49): a derivation can be correct, independent, hard-won — and second.** The derivation-first column's 8-for-8 record is a record of claims that were TRUE, not of claims that were NEW, and nothing in this discipline distinguishes them without a search. Four milestones of correct derivation reconstructed a 2010 draft's §§1, 3, 4, 6, 8 and 10. *The rule this adds: "derived, therefore ours" is exactly the inference this grokking forbids everywhere else, and it had been running unchallenged. A derivation's novelty is a MEASUREMENT — a literature search — and it belongs at the milestone's opening, where it can still change the work.*

**THE STRENGTHENING (Kooshi, 2026-09-06, on the finite-plane pivot — his reading of the whole record as a ladder of disciplines, each hop forced by a wall that turned out to be a theorem in the next room):** *graph theory* (the problem, the Moser spindle) → *computational combinatorics / SAT* (the wall: hand refutation dies at ~20 vertices; de Grey, Heule, Polymath16) → *formal verification* (the wall: SAT certificates are opaque bitstreams; Lean's kernel — this project's entry point) → *Euclidean geometry* (the wall: the graphs are not abstract — rotation groups, spindle angles, the 1/√3 circle; M1–M20) → *algebraic number theory* (the wall: "why do all 5-chromatic graphs contain √5 or √7?" — a question about the coordinates' field, not the graph; M20–M24) → *p-adic / local algebra* (the wall: "what makes K = ℚ(√3,√11) special?" — how 2 factors when i is adjoined: ramification, inertia, residue fields; Theorem B — the steepest hop, where CS-flavoured discrete mathematics has almost no overlap) → *additive combinatorics* (the wall: "why does the residue method go blind between 4 and 7?" — it only uses homomorphisms, linear maps; the Cayley-graph reframing, M56) → *finite field geometry* (the wall: "is linearity the only thing the finite picture can do?" — no: the reduction lands in an actual finite Euclidean plane whose chromatic number is non-linear and sees 5; Vinh 2005 studied the objects, nobody had connected them to the field descent). Suggested and not yet entered: *algebraic topology* (Borsuk–Ulam, box complexes — the one lower-bound technique sharp at every integer; FS-9) and *commutative algebra* (Nullstellensatz certificates — polynomial infeasibility proofs sensitive to integrality; FS-10). […] *Rule this adds: when a measured wall resists derivation in the discipline it was found in, name the discipline the wall's statement naturally lives in and look for the theorem there before building another instrument here.*

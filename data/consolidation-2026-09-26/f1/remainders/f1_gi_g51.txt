* **G51 — A competitor and an advisor beat one mind, and the mechanism is not more compute.** (2026-09-08/09, the mascot challenge, at Kooshi's design; a methodological invariant in G21/G30/G46/G49/G50's lineage.) The setup: **a friendly competitor** working the same problem independently, exchanging only teasing hints, plus **an advisor** (Poros, `~/source/poros`) holding the right-question standard, advising both identically and carrying nothing between them. Measured on one hard problem in one session, every item with its address:

- **The competitor found the prior art that killed my object.** I had misread a mangled `√` glyph in a PDF text extraction, turning distance `3` into `√3`; it read Table 1 of the same author's *other* paper and found the truth. My construction died on its evidence, not mine — and it handed the find over while believing it might lose it the competition.

- **The advisor moved the target before either of us committed.** Its sieve pass found that three of the four candidate fields are ones where the mission's actual goal (`χ ≥ 6`) provably cannot live, which reframed what winning would even mean.

- **Each corrected the others, in all directions.** I corrected the advisor's sieve conclusion as over-broad (the ladder does reach 6-open fields — just never at the *first* deep chord, which is the only one a minimal shape can target, so the verdict survived for a better reason); the competitor corrected my reading; the advisor and the competitor independently found the same hole in a *third* milestone's prior-art check (FS-20's), which neither was asked to look at.

- **Both independent attempts hit the same wall**, and that is what licensed turning "we failed" into "here is a kernel-checked proof that it is impossible" (ledger row 58). […]

- **The honesty held under incentive.** The competitor found my derivation sitting in the session scratchpad, disclosed it before using it, and declined to run my line anyway. *Design lessons, all cheap and all learned the hard way:* **isolate the scratchpad per agent** — a shared temp directory is a covert channel, and mine leaked my whole mechanism to my competitor through the filesystem (my fault, not its); brief the competitor with the *same* starting information so divergence is real rather than an artifact of who was told what; give the advisor the record and a written expectation rather than a question; and **rule at the outset that prior art is shared across the competition** — hoarding it would have let my dead object survive another day, which serves nobody. […] **The two session records are `plans/COXETER-2026-09-08-mascot.md` (the competitor) and `plans/POROS-2026-09-08-mascot.md` (the advisor)** — named here 2026-09-09 by the record audit, which found them the only two files in `plans/` reachable from nothing: this entry described them and never cited their paths. Composes with G30 (the corrections were the product — measurement generated candidates, cross-examination killed them) and G49/G50 (both agents' highest-value finds were literature the project already held or could have searched).*

## Theorems this phase proved

- **LV-T1 [hand, a second reader of LP's derivation]** — the fractional Benda–Perles–Tarski sandwich:
  `χ_f,0(ℝ²) ≤ χ_f(𝒜²) ≤ χ_f(ℝ²)` unconditionally (every finite subgraph of `(ℝ², 1)` is an induced subgraph of `(𝒜², 1)`,
  `[Johnson2000]` §3 p. 174 on `[BendaPerles2000]` pp. 117–118; `𝒜²` is an induced subgraph of `ℝ²`; `χ_f` is monotone under subgraphs,
  with Johnson's `χ_f = χ*` for infinite graphs, `[Johnson1996fcn]` p. 102 Proposition, proof p. 106). With row 129 (given MR at
  ℚ(√635087)) the sandwich closes: `χ_f(𝒜²) = χ_f(ℝ²)`. Grade of the consequence: row 129's.
- **LV-T2 [hand, a second reader of LP's derivation]** — for every real number field `F`, `(F², 1)` has infinitely many components: a
  unit vector `u` of `F(i)` has `v_𝔓(u) = v_𝔓(ū)` at every conjugation-stable prime `𝔓` (so `v_𝔓(u) = 0`, since `uū = 1`); infinitely many
  primes of `F` are inert in `F(i)/F` (Chebotarev), their primes above are stable; choosing stable `𝔓ₙ` over distinct rational primes `pₙ`,
  `1/pₙ − 1/pₘ` has `v_{𝔓ₙ} < 0`, so the `1/pₙ` lie in distinct cosets of the unit-vector group. No Lemma C′, so row 100's exception
  fields are included. In print for multiquadratic `F`: `[Fischer1990b]` Lemma 1 (p. 215, the integrality) and its Corollary (the
  components); for ℚ(√m), `m ≢ 7 (mod 8)`: Zaks, per `[Johnson2000]` p. 177.

### P1 — Theorem A's construction

- **`[Fischer1990]`** (Discrete Math. 82, text layer) p. 181: *"A group homomorphism ν: C₀(F^d) → G to an additive group G of order k so that
  ν(ξ) ≠ 0 whenever ‖ξ‖ = 1, is said to be an additive k-coloring of F^d."* (the Greek letter is garbled in the text layer; the proof on
  p. 183 writes it `v`). p. 183, **Theorem 1.** *"i. If F^d has an additive k-coloring, then F^d can be k-colored."* Proof: *"Since the
  connected components of F^d are translates of C₀(F^d), to k-color F^d it suffices to k-color C₀(F^d). The existence of a group
  homomorphism v : C₀(F^d) → G where G is of order k, assures that if ξ, η ∈ C₀(F^d) and if ‖ξ − η‖ = 1, then v(ξ − η) ≠ 0. Hence,
  v(ξ) ≠ v(η) and therefore v assigns a k-coloring to C₀(F^d). This provides the proof for part (i)."* So Thm 1(i) is the coset
  construction for the unit-distance graph on `F^d`, `F ⊆ ℝ`.
- **`[Johnson2006]`** p. 213: *"If S is an additive subgroup of ℝⁿ and D ⊆ (0, ∞), let G₀(S, D) denote the component of G(S, D) containing
  (0, …, 0). Clearly the vertices of G₀(S, D) are the points in the subgroup of S generated by {u ∈ S | |u| ∈ D} = S_D. Call this subgroup
  ⟨S_D⟩. An additive k-coloring of G(S, D) is a proper coloring achieved by finding a group homomorphism of ⟨S_D⟩ into some abelian group
  of order k so that each u ∈ S_D is mapped to a non-zero group element, which assures that G₀(S, D) is properly colored, and then copying
  the coloring on the other components of G(S, D), which are copies of G₀(S, D) associated with the cosets of ⟨S_D⟩ in S. It is easy to see
  [10] that if χ(G(S, D)) = 2 then every proper 2-coloring of G(S, D) is an additive 2-coloring."* A definition that asserts properness;
  `S ⊆ ℝⁿ` a subgroup, `D` any set of positive distances — a Cayley graph of `S`, the metric used only to name the connection set.
- **`[BendaPerles2000]`** (circulated 1976) p. 120, the two-colouring of `ℚ²`: *"It can be extended to the whole Q² since the other connected
  components, being cosets of H², are isomorphic to it in the graph structure."*; p. 121, `ℚ³`: *"H³ and consequently G₁(Q³) is 2-colorable
  as well."* **The coset construction is in print from 1976**, in instances (a 2-colouring of the unit-vector group copied to its cosets),
  fourteen years before Fischer's general Thm 1(i). LP's record does not name these pages for the construction.
- **`[Johnson2006]`'s [10] is `[Johnson1982]`** (p. 217: *"P. D. Johnson Jr., Coloring Abelian groups, Discrete Math. 40 (1982), 219-223"*) —
  **unread and not held**: one scripted fetch of its open-access PDF (ScienceDirect, the URL OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar list) returned
  HTTP 403; zbMATH (Zbl 0485.05009) has no review; OpenAlex has no abstract. Semantic Scholar's machine-generated TLDR (not the paper's
  words, not a reading): *"A sufficient condition for the existence of a particular kind of n-coloring of an abelian group is given, and applied
  to show that the real line is colorable with two colors so that the distance 1 is forbidden for one color."* **It is the paper most likely
  to hold the metric-free, arbitrary-abelian-group form that LP left "ours".** A debt for Kooshi (a browser fetch of an open-archive paper).
- **`[Madore2015]`** Prop. 3.9 (text layer, p. 8): the aside *"(which the author has not found the courage to try to formulate under the most
  general auspices)"* introduces χ(ℚ₂(√2)²) = 2, proved by an equivalence relation whose classes are cosets of `((1/√2)A)²`, a subgroup
  that is not a ring — a homomorphism out of a subgroup containing the unit vectors, which restricts to `C₀`. So Fischer's Thm 1(i)
  covers it.

### P2 — the bridge and the unit-vector ring of ℚ

- **`[BendaPerles2000]`** pp. 117–118, verbatim: *"Let us now look at the problem from a different angle. If the chromatic number for R²
  (= (R², e)) is not n then, as we mentioned above, there is a finite subgraph of G₁(R²) which is not n-colorable. Now we can write a
  first-order statement about the reals which express the fact that there are 2n reals (corresponding to n points on the plane) such that
  distances between certain pairs are 1 and that the resulting graph is not n-colorable (a long formula describing all possible
  colorings). ¹ The reason we go into the trouble of formulating this in the first-order language is that there is a theorem of Tarski which
  says that the theory of real closed fields is complete (see e.g. [1]). This implies that the above sentence if true in the reals is true in
  all real closed fields, in particular in the field of algebraic reals. Consequently, the Erdös four-color conjecture, is true iff it is true
  of the algebraic plane A², A being the field of the algebraic reals."* (Footnote 1: *"The use of n for both the number of colors and the
  number of points is clearly an unintended first-draft oversight."*) So BP print `χ(ℝ²) = χ(𝔸²)`, `𝔸` the real algebraic numbers; the
  number-field form ("attained at a finite extension") is one compactness line they do not write.
- p. 120: *"H² consists of all (a,b)/e with (a, b, e) = 1 where e is a product of primes of the form 4k + 1."* — `H²` the component of the
  origin in `G₁(Q²)`, which they call *"a group"* (p. 120), not a ring.
- Johnson's introduction p. 110: *"The original manuscript of "Colorings...", from which some copies were made and circulated (and then copies
  were made of the copies, etc.), was typed in Brazil in 1976."*; p. 111: *"you may as well publish the manuscript as it was circulated."*
- **`[Johnson2000]`** §3 p. 174: *"What they have proven really is that every finite subgraph of (ℝ², 1) is (isomorphic to) a finite subgraph
  of (𝔸², 1). This is the first algebraic embedding theorem that I have ever seen whose proof uses a theorem from logic."*
- **`[Johnson2006]`** p. 214: *"in one of the most spectacular and subsequently neglected sections of [2], it is shown, using some fancy Tarskian
  logic and the well known compactness result of de Bruijn and Erdős [6] on coloring infinite graphs, that for each n there is a finite
  (dimensional) real algebraic extension F of ℚ such that χ(G(Fⁿ, 1)) = χ(G(ℝⁿ, 1))."* — the number-field form, credited to [2] = BP.
- **`[Fischer1990]`** p. 182 already credits BP: *"That the answer to this question would suffice to find the chromatic number of R² has
  already been noted by Benda and Perles in [2]."* ([2] = *"M. Benda and M. Perles, Colorings of metric spaces (preprint)."*)
- **`[Madore2015]`** ¶5.4 (p. 14) derives `χ(ℝ^d) = χ((ℚ^{r−alg})^d)` from Tarski, and its footnote 5 says: *"This answers [Soifer 2009,
  problem 11.1], but the question is perhaps misstated since the MathSciNet review of [Benda & Perles 2000] (the present author does not have
  access to the paper itself) suggests that the remark above is already contained there."* So Madore's own footnote pointed at BP — held
  in our copy since the first reading, and missed (a G50 instance).

- B1 Rank 1 is decidable: for finite D ⊂ ℤ, k-colourings of G(ℤ,D) are bi-infinite walks in a finite transfer graph
  (window max D), so colourability ⟺ a cycle ⟺ a periodic colouring (Eggleton–Erdős–Skilton). The structural question has
  content only at rank ≥ 2; rank 1 is the lab and the control.
- B2 Decidability context: ℤ² SFT emptiness is undecidable (Berger); Burr 1984 for periodic graphs with several orbits;
  single-tile / two-tile translational tiling undecidability and the disproof of the periodic tiling conjecture
  (Greenfeld–Tao) show "one orbit" is no safe haven; the domino problem for hom-shifts specifically is searched.
- B3 G72 move 3's generic form at every k: translation-invariant measures on the hom-shift, projected to finite windows —
  a finite LP of locally consistent pattern frequencies whose infeasibility refuses at any k (one-sided); completeness is
  the question; tied to F_μ (G60, FS73-T38).
- B4 Lovász's topological method (box / Hom / neighbourhood complex bounds) named as the classical "topology of the
  solution space" invariant, with what it sees and does not (the graph's complex vs maps into the target's).
- B5 The target's topology one dimension up: K₄'s clique complex is S² (π₁ = 0, π₂ = ℤ); the square complex ℝP² has
  universal cover S²; G71's ℤ/2 is π₁(ℝP²) — the next integer is π₂. (Public via G72's "one dimension up, its spheres";
  scored for whether the plan turns it into a question, not whether it names it.)
- B6 Controls with known answers: de Grey's lattice F = 8Λ (the positive a complete invariant must explain; Hubai's stripes
  and chirality); G(ℤ,{1,3,4,5}) four-colourable with no linear colouring (T30); a five-chromatic TRIANGLE-FREE abelian
  Cayley graph as the hard negative (so an invariant is not merely detecting cliques — ℚ(√167)'s graphs are triangle-free).

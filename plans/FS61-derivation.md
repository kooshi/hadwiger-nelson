**FS61-T2 — The dyadic law's other half: no primitive unit vector of `ℚ(√d)²` has denominator `≡ 2 (mod 4)` when `d ≡ 7 (mod 8)`. [hand; the FS-61 implementer, "L4"; machine-checked by exhaustion]** Let `d ≡ 7 (mod 8)` be squarefree and let `((a + b√d)/N, (c + e√d)/N)` be a unit vector in lowest terms (`a, b, c, e, N ∈ ℤ`, `gcd(a,b,c,e,N) = 1`), so `a² + c² + d(b² + e²) = N²` and `ab + ce = 0`. Then `N ≢ 2 (mod 4)`. *Proof:* suppose `N = 2M`, `M` odd. Then `N² ≡ 4 (mod 8)` and `d ≡ −1 (mod 8)`, so the norm equation gives `a² + c² − (b² + e²) ≡ 4 (mod 8)`. A square is `≡ 0 (mod 8)` iff the root is `≡ 0 (mod 4)`, `≡ 4 (mod 8)` iff the root is `≡ 2 (mod 4)`, `≡ 1 (mod 8)` iff the root is odd; so a sum of two squares is one of `0,1,2,4,5 (mod 8)`, and the congruence forces `(a²+c², b²+e²) ≡ (4,0), (0,4), (5,1)` or `(1,5) (mod 8)`. The first two make `a,b,c,e` all even, contradicting primitivity with `N` even. In the last two, exactly one of `a,c` is odd and exactly one of `b,e` is odd, and the SECOND equation (`ab+ce=0`) decides it: if the odd ones are aligned (`a,b` odd or `c,e` odd) then `ab` is odd and `ce` even, so `ab+ce` is odd; if crossed (`a,e` odd or `c,b` odd) then one of `ab, ce` is `≡ 2 (mod 4)` and the other `≡ 0 (mod 4)`, so `ab+ce ≡ 2 (mod 4)`. Either way `ab+ce ≠ 0`, against orthogonality. ∎ (`plans/FS61-quadratic-census.md` §R-A, item A.4, in full; the norm equation alone does not decide it mod 8 — the second, orthogonality, equation read mod 4 is what closes the last two cases.) **Machine check:** exhausting `(a,b,c,e,N) mod 8` under the norm equation, orthogonality and primitivity leaves 0 surviving patterns at `d ≡ 7 (mod 8)`, against 256 at `d ≡ 3 (mod 8)` (where `N ≡ 2 (mod 4)` denominators really do occur, e.g. `6, 10, 18, 30, …` over `ℚ(√11)`) — the same exhaustion, confirming the argument is exactly as sharp as the phenomenon, not vacuous. **Field test:** 0 violations on 1,820 non-rational (field, denominator) pairs of `atlas.jsonl` at `d ≡ 7 (mod 8)`, `N ≤ 420`. *Proof verdict:* closes. *Rests on it:* `LEDGER.md` row 74 (item 2, "the dyadic law's other half"); `FS58-T9` (`plans/FS58-derivation.md`), which restates this exact congruence in "diamond chord" language over one field and cites it as "row 74's dyadic lemma" — that pointer now resolves to this entry, not to a paragraph; the atlas enumeration `tools/fs61_census.py atlas`, which uses the complementary fact (`d ≡ 3 mod 8` allows `v₂(N) ≤ 1`, proved separately and more simply from the dyadic prime's stability) to decide which denominators to search. *Prior art:* ours — a congruence exhaustion in two equations, mod 8 then mod 4. `[ZanardoZannier1991]` Thm 3 (read, `BIBLIOGRAPHY.md`) was checked first and is not what decides this — it gives a basis of the free part of the norm-one group through the split primes and explains why powers of 2 occur in denominators at `d ≡ 7 (mod 8)` at all (the dyadic prime splits there), but says nothing about parity; recorded as *check owed, ruled out* rather than *prior art*. zbMATH: `ab:"norm equation" & ab:"denominator" & ab:"2-adic"` (0), `ab:"norm one" & ab:"real quadratic field" & ab:"denominator"` (0). *Kernel:* declined — a congruence-exhaustion argument with no separate kernel consumer beyond FS58-T9, which follows this entry's decision; the project's one kernel congruence barrier at this level is the 3-adic one (row 72, `Proofs/HadwigerNelson/ThreeAdicBarrier.lean`), which this is the 2-adic analogue of, unformalised.

**FS61-T3 — Cross-field non-transfer: an integral shape's unit vectors over two distinct fields share only the rational ones. [hand; the FS-61 implementer, stated as the mechanism behind `tools/fs61_owner_shape_iso.py`'s search]** Fix a rank-4 integral shape `Λ ⊆ ℤ⁴` (a sublattice, in coordinates `(a,b,c,e)` standing for `((a+b√d)/M, (c+e√d)/M)` at a fixed common denominator `M`). For squarefree `d`, let `W_d = {(a,b,c,e) ∈ Λ : a²+c²+d(b²+e²) = M², ab+ce = 0}` be `Λ`'s unit vectors over `ℚ(√d)`. For distinct squarefree `d ≠ d′`: `W_d ∩ W_{d′} = {(a,b,c,e) ∈ Λ : b = e = 0, a² + c² = M², ac = 0}` — exactly the *rational* unit vectors of `Λ` (those independent of `d`), whichever two distinct `d, d′` are chosen. *Proof:* if `(a,b,c,e)` lies in both `W_d` and `W_{d′}`, subtracting the two norm equations gives `(d − d′)(b² + e²) = 0`; since `d ≠ d′` this forces `b² + e² = 0`, and since `b, e` are integers, `b = e = 0`. The converse is immediate — a rational vector's membership condition (`a²+c²=M², ac=0`) does not mention `d`, so it lies in `W_d` for every `d`. ∎ (the norm equation's being *linear in `d`* is the whole mechanism; the tool's own docstring, `tools/fs61_owner_shape_iso.py` lines 2–3, names it in one sentence and this entry completes the two-line algebra). *Proof verdict:* closes. *Rests on it:* `LEDGER.md` row 74 (item 3, "no witness transfers between fields"); the census's own conclusion that **T4** (every squarefree `d ≡ 11 (mod 12)` needs four colours — see this file's "Conjectures surfaced") has no proof route via a parametric witness, since T3 rules out the cheapest such route (one abstract graph, valid over several fields at once, drawn from a shared shape); FS61-T4, whose exhaustive search this lemma explains: three of its four checked pairs need no search at all, since a shared *non-rational* unit vector is impossible by this theorem, and the odd-girth invariant alone (a graph-isomorphism invariant, unaffected by which `d`) already separates them without appeal to a search. *Prior art:* ours — a one-line linear-algebra fact about the norm form. zbMATH: `ab:"family of quadratic fields" & ab:"same lattice" & ab:"solutions"` (0), `ab:"lattice" & ab:"unit vectors" & ab:"quadratic field" & ab:"colouring"` (0). Nearest held key: `[ZanardoZannier1991]`, whose Theorem 3 computes a *different* quantity (how many unit vectors of a given height exist over one field, via the class group) from the one this lemma decides (which vectors two fields' unit-vector sets share) — **check owed, not confirmed as the same theorem**: row 74 calls the class-group count "the quantity `[ZanardoZannier1991]` Thm 3 computes... a pointer... not re-read for this," and that check stays open (`BIBLIOGRAPHY.md`'s row already marks Thm 3 read for FS-61 phase A's different question, A.4, not for this one). *Kernel:* declined — an elementary lattice fact with no consumer beyond this milestone's own search design.

**FS61-T4 — The four census same-generator-count shape pairs admit no linear lattice automorphism carrying one field's unit vectors onto the other's. [tools; the FS-61 implementer, `tools/fs61_owner_shape_iso.py`]** For the four pairs of (shape, field, field) found in the census with equal unit-vector counts on both sides — `L8a8b1603` (`d=95` vs `d=119`, 28 = 28), `Lb0a7206c` (`d=23` vs `d=191`, 36 = 36), `L54356af8` (`d=95` vs `d=191`, 52 = 52), `L3e1c94c6` (`d=239` vs `d=359`, 52 = 52) — there is no `T ∈ GL_4(ℤ)` fixing the shared lattice `Λ` with `T(W_{d_1}) = W_{d_2}`. **Method, cheapest first:** for the first three pairs, the odd girths of the two fields' graphs on `Λ` already disagree (7 vs 5, 9 vs 5, 7 vs 5), and odd girth is preserved by any graph isomorphism, so no such `T` (which by FS61-T3's converse would BE a graph isomorphism `Cay(Λ, W_{d_1}) → Cay(Λ, W_{d_2})`) can exist — no search needed. For the fourth pair (`d=239` vs `d=359`), where the odd girths agree (7 = 7), an exhaustive exact search over `T ∈ GL_4(ℚ) ∩ \text{End}(Λ)` (Gaussian elimination in `Fraction` arithmetic, pruned level by level along a greedy basis of `W_{239}`) found **0** valid maps `T(W_{239}) = W_{359}`, against a **positive control** — the identical search run with `d_1 = d_2 = 359` on its own 52 unit vectors — which correctly recovers **16** linear self-automorphisms (the group of `Λ`'s own symmetries fixing `W_{359}` setwise). *Proof:* `data/fs61/owner/shape-iso.out` (the full transcript, all four pairs and the positive control); `tools/fs61_owner_shape_iso.py` (the search code, standard-library `Fraction` arithmetic, no solver). *Proof verdict:* closes (a finite, exact, exhibited computation with a positive control demonstrating the search can find a positive answer when one exists). *Rests on it:* `LEDGER.md` row 74 (item 3, "the four same-count pairs tested are not linearly isomorphic either"); the same "no witness transfers" reading as FS61-T3. *Prior art:* ours. zbMATH: `ab:"Cayley graph" & ab:"isomorphism" & ab:"lattice" & ab:"odd girth"` (0), `ab:"unit distance graph" & ab:"quadratic field" & ab:"isomorphic"` (0). *Kernel:* declined — a specific finite exhaustive check over four pairs, consumed only as a negative result informing where not to search next.

**FS61-T5 — The disc-overlap fraction, and the bidisc degree-prediction formula built from it. [hand; the FS-61 implementer, credited in the tool's own comment as "the owner's addition" (`tools/fs61_census.py` line 3296)]** (a) **Exact geometric fact.** For `R > 1/2` let `φ(R) = [2R²·arccos(1/(2R)) − (1/2)·√(4R²−1)] / (πR²)`. Then `φ(R)` equals the fraction of the area of a disc of radius `R` that is covered by that disc's own translate by any vector of length 1 (equivalently: the area of intersection of two radius-`R` discs whose centres are distance 1 apart, divided by `πR²`) — the standard circle–circle intersection ("lens") area, `2R²·cos⁻¹(d/2R) − (d/2)√(4R²−d²)`, evaluated at separation `d = 1` and normalised by one disc's area `πR²`; `φ(R) = 0` for `R ≤ 1/2` (the discs are disjoint) and `φ(R) → 1 − 1/(πR)` as `R → ∞`. This is elementary, standard plane geometry (the two-circular-segment decomposition of the lens), reproduced here because it is nowhere else derived in this project's files, only asserted. (b) **The degree-prediction heuristic**, which uses (a) but is not itself a theorem: a rank-4 lattice `Λ` over a real quadratic field, holding `g` unit vectors of a fixed class set, has a ball `B_R` (points of `Λ` within radius `R` of the origin under BOTH real embeddings — a "bidisc" region, since `ℚ(√d)²` has two real places). Each point of `B_R` has `g` candidate unit-vector neighbours; if a candidate's landing inside `B_R` under each embedding independently has probability `φ(R)` (translating a disc by a unit vector covers a `φ(R)`-fraction of it, in each embedding separately, by (a)), the predicted fraction landing in `B_R` under both is `φ(R)²`, giving predicted average degree `g·φ(R)²`. The independence assumption is NOT proven — the two embeddings' offsets need not be independent for a specific finite set of lattice points — and the formula is accordingly a heuristic, not an identity: its accuracy is a *measurement* (observed/predicted ratio has median 1.0000 and range 0.715–1.184 over 775 asked balls across the census's 40 fields plus 7 controls, 0 outside ±30%; the un-squared alternative `g·φ(R)` reads median 0.584 on a 60-ball sample, the planted control that shows the square is load-bearing). *Proof:* (a) is elementary calculus/geometry, verified here; (b)'s mechanism is stated in `tools/fs61_census.py` lines 3296–3305 (the "THE LAW" comment) and in `data/fs61/readings.md` ("E.2 — the degree phase diagram"); the accuracy measurement is `tools/fs61_readings.py`'s `degree_law_control`, reproduced in `data/fs61/readings.md`. *Proof verdict:* closes for (a); (b) is explicitly NOT filed as a theorem (see this file's "Readings and measurements" section) — its accuracy is measured, not proven, per this audit's kind-before-grade rule (`CLAUDE.md` G65 is graded `[measured]` for exactly this reason). *Rests on it:* `LEDGER.md` row 74 (item 4) and `CLAUDE.md` G65 in full — the census's own re-aiming of tiers 1c, 1u and 2l at predicted degree rather than predicted size, which found five fields (`59, 119, 239, 359, 431`) that three earlier size-aimed tiers had not reached; FS61-T1's certificates at those fields. *Prior art:* ours for the application; the geometric fact (a) is textbook (two-circle intersection area, "lens" or "vesica" area — e.g. Weisstein, MathWorld, uncited here as it needs no citation). zbMATH: `ab:"circle intersection" & ab:"lattice point" & ab:"average degree"` (0), `ti:"unit disk graph" & ab:"expected degree"` (0). *Kernel:* declined — an aiming heuristic for a search tool, with no kernel-shaped consequence.

## Readings and measurements that were called theorems

- **The registered readings, scored** (`data/fs61/readings.md`, phase E items E.2, E.3, E.6): **R-cycle won** — among 83 live rank-4 lattices with a decided ball, a shortest odd cycle of length ≤ 9 is met by every one of 22 refusing lattices and by 25 of 56 colourable ones (Fisher exact `p = 1.2e-06`); necessary, far from sufficient. **R-mod8 lost, both strengths** — `d = 11` and `d = 59` (both `d ≡ 3 mod 8`) refuse on their own lattices (not by inheritance from any kernel witness), and 65 lattices over `d ≡ 3 (mod 8)` fields remain `Open`. **R-L lost** — landing rates for `L = 4` and `L ≥ 5` fields among `d ≡ 7 (mod 8)` do not differ beyond chance (Fisher `p = 0.648`; 8/12 vs 4/8). **R-C lost** — G60's ratio reading as a decider of finite landing: all 24 refusing lattices measured have least character mean below `−1/2` (range `−0.8223` to `−0.5380`), the opposite of the reading's prediction. **R-size not decided** — a log–log slope of 1.15 (95% interval `−0.14` to `2.44`, `R² = 0.23`) over 12 landed `d ≡ 7 (mod 8)` fields is consistent with the claimed band `[0.5, 2]` but the population is too small and confounded with covolume to separate the reading from its negation. Ten predictions registered before the first census solver call: 4 won, 4 lost, 2 not measured (`data/fs61/readings.md` §E.6). None of these five readings is a theorem — each is a claim scored against a finite, named population, explicitly "not against the family" (`data/fs61/readings.md`'s own opening line).
- **The typed-null classification** (`data/fs61/census.md`): of the 40 census fields, 14 are `Landed (certified)` (FS61-T1); of the 26 not landed, each null is typed — 1 *by certificate* (no `Open` lattice remains at that depth: `d = 227`), 10 *by budget* (every candidate ball is over the tier's point cap, so nothing was measured: `d = 107, 299, 347, 371, 383, 395, 419, 443, 467, 491`), 15 *asked, no refusal* (a ball was put to a solver and came back 3-colourable or `Unknown`). This is a classification of measurements, not a theorem, and it is the reason row 74 insists "not landed" is never read as "3-colourable".
- **A.7's negative result: no rule found for the hidden group of a non-affine periodic 3-colouring** (`plans/FS61-quadratic-census.md` §R-A item A.7). The plan's own target shape — a homomorphism `ρ : Λ → A` onto a small finite abelian group with a proper 3-colouring of `Cay(A, ρ(generators))` — was searched by computing, for each dead lattice, the least-order 3-colourable quotient by exhaustive search over subgroups of index ≤ 36. The groups found are `27, 108, 108, 243, 1728, 2187, 4096`; the most common order (108) covers only 29% of the sample against the gate's 90% target, and `167`'s `q1+1008.1` colouring factors through `(ℤ/8)⁴`, an order with **no factor of 3**, which kills the natural candidate rule (that `ρ` is the kernel's `𝔽₃`-shaped barrier with a larger 3-group substituted). Recorded as a negative result and a registered prediction lost (P8: 0.35 probability, needed 90%, got 29%), not a theorem.
- **The degree-prediction heuristic's accuracy** (FS61-T5(b) above) is a measurement, not a theorem, per the kind-before-grade classification (M.1 of `plans/FS71-theorem-audit.md`): checked on a finite, named population (775 balls) with a control that can fail (the un-squared alternative reads median 0.584), and stated as a prediction with a bounded error, not an identity.
- **`[Sokolov2018]`'s rank-2-layer question, settled by a rank, not a theorem** (`data/fs61/shapes.md` Table 3). `[Sokolov2018]` Thm 4 asks whether `χ_√ℚ(ℝ²) = max_n χ(ℚ × √n·ℚ) = 3`; a 4-chromatic unit-distance graph lying in a rank-2 ℚ-subspace `ℚ × √d·ℚ` (up to rotation) would bear on it. Every one of the 14 certified witnesses of FS61-T1 has ℚ-rank exactly 4 (computed in exact rational arithmetic), and a set of ℚ-rank 4 cannot lie in any 2-dimensional ℚ-subspace, rotated or not — a one-line linear-algebra fact, not filed as a theorem here since it is a trivial dimension count, not new mathematics. The finding is that this census's objects are silent on Sokolov's question, exactly as FS-58's informal remark said ("their coordinates mix 1 and √d in both slots"), now made exact.
- **D-H.1 (the height floor `N ≥ √d`) and D-H.2 (the prime-support law)** (`plans/FS61-quadratic-census.md` §3) are elementary design lemmas used to build the atlas enumeration (`tools/fs61_census.py atlas`), not filed separately: D-H.1 is a two-line consequence of the norm equation (`b² + e² ≥ 1` for a non-rational unit vector forces `N² ≥ d`). D-H.2's "only if" direction (an odd prime `p ≡ 3 (mod 4)` divides some class denominator only if `(d/p) = −1`) is an **instance of a held theorem** — the stable-prime integrality argument of G35/`[Fischer1990b]`'s prime inversion (every unit vector is a unit at every stable prime), already attributed in this project; its "if" direction cites Hilbert 90 directly, standard.
- **`[ZanardoZannier1991]` Thm 3, check owed, not confirmed** (row 74, this file's FS61-T3 entry). The census's own text calls the per-field unit-vector count of a shared shape "the quantity Thm 3 computes... a pointer — that theorem was not re-read for this." `BIBLIOGRAPHY.md`'s row for `[ZanardoZannier1991]` correctly records "FS-61 phase A reads Theorem 3" for a *different* question (A.4's parity argument, which explicitly found Thm 3 does NOT decide it); the connection to the shape-count finding remains an unread pointer and is recorded here as still owed, not as confirmed prior art — no edit to `BIBLIOGRAPHY.md` is proposed, since its existing text is accurate about what was actually read and why.

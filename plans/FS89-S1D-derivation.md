## §1 — D-0: the rank-two orbit model (derivation)

*Read for this section:* `[Berend1984min]` in full (pp. 499–507: Thm 2.1, Lemmas 3.1–3.4, Prop. 4.1, Lemmas 4.1–4.2, the
reduction steps); Haynes–Munday §1–§2 (arXiv 1303.1661); S0B's record in full; `tools/fs89_s0b_orbits.py`.

**Layers.** For `m = (m_p)_{p∈S} ∈ ℤ^S` put `w^m = ∏ w_p^{m_p}` and `A_m = w^m ℤ[i]`, the fractional ideal with exponent
`m_p` at `π_p` and `−m_p` at `π̄_p`. A character `θ` restricts to `A_m` as `y ↦ ⟨e_m, w^{−m} y⟩` for a unique `e_m ∈ ℂ/ℤ[i]`
(the dual of `ℤ[i]` under `⟨,⟩` is `ℤ[i]`; `⟨e, a y⟩ = ⟨ā e, y⟩`).

**D0-L1 (steps).** `A_m ∩ A_{m+ε_p} = w^m π_p ℤ[i]`; on `x = w^m π_p y` the two layers read `⟨π̄_p e_m, y⟩` and
`⟨π_p e_{m+ε_p}, y⟩`, so they agree iff
`e_{m+ε_p} − w̄_p e_m ∈ π_p^{−1}ℤ[i]` (one step rule per prime; well defined mod `ℤ[i]` since `w̄_pℤ[i] ⊆ π_p^{−1}ℤ[i]`).
At `S = {5}` this is S0B-T1's rule verbatim.

**D0-L2 (gluing).** Adjacent compatibility for all `m` and `p` implies that the `e_m` come from one character of `R_S`.
*Proof.* (a) `A_m ∩ A_{m'}` has exponents `(max(m_p, m'_p), −min(m_p, m'_p))` at `(π_p, π̄_p)`, so it lies in every `A_{m''}` with
`m''` in the box spanned by `m, m'`; along a monotone lattice path in that box adjacent compatibility carries agreement from `A_m` to
`A_{m'}` on `A_m ∩ A_{m'}`. So the family is pairwise compatible. (b) Fractional ideals of the Dedekind domain `ℤ[i]` form a
distributive lattice (`A ∩ (B + C) = A∩B + A∩C`); for a finite distributive family of subgroups the kernel of `⊕A_j → ΣA_j` is
generated by the pairwise relations (induction: for `(a₁,…,a_k)` in the kernel, `a_k ∈ A_k ∩ Σ_{j<k}A_j = Σ_{j<k}(A_k ∩ A_j)`;
subtract those pairwise relations and recurse). So pairwise compatible characters on the box `|m_p| ≤ K` glue to a unique
character of `Σ A_m = ∏_p p^{−K}·ℤ[i]`, and the union over `K` is `R_S`. ∎

**D0-L3 (squares complete uniquely).** Given `e_m`, `e_{m+ε_p}`, `e_{m+ε_q}` (`p ≠ q`) compatible, there is exactly one
`e_{m+ε_p+ε_q} ∈ ℂ/ℤ[i]` compatible with both. *Proof.* It must lie in `w̄_q e_{m+ε_p} + π_q^{−1}ℤ[i]` and in
`w̄_p e_{m+ε_q} + π_p^{−1}ℤ[i]`; the difference of the two base points is `w̄_q a − w̄_p b` with `a ∈ π_p^{−1}ℤ[i]`,
`b ∈ π_q^{−1}ℤ[i]`, which lies in `(π_pπ_q)^{−1}ℤ[i] = π_p^{−1}ℤ[i] ⊕ π_q^{−1}ℤ[i]` (mod `ℤ[i]`, orders `p` and `q`, coprime);
two cosets of coprime-order subgroups of that group meet in exactly one point (CRT). It also satisfies the diagonal condition
`A_m ∩ A_{m+ε_p+ε_q} = w^m π_pπ_q ℤ[i]`. ∎

**D0-T1 (the rank-`r` orbit model).**
`Θ_β(U_S) ≅ {(e_m)_{m ∈ ℤ^S} : e_m ∈ Q_β for all m;  e_{m+ε_p} − w̄_p e_m ∈ π_p^{−1}ℤ[i] for all m, p}`,
with `θ(iᵏ w^m) = ⟨e_m, iᵏ⟩` (band ⟺ `e_m ∈ Q_β`, unique lift since `Q_β` has side `< 1`). By D0-L3 **an array is determined by
its restrictions to the coordinate axes**; without the band, `Hom(R_S, ℝ/ℤ)` ≅ {one bi-infinite `p`-orbit per prime, all sharing
`e_0`}, with no other constraint; the band then couples them on every orthant. Level `K` (window `|m_p| ≤ K`) is the set of
characters of `∏_p p^{−K}ℤ[i]` with the band on the window's unit vectors. [hand]

**Rows are restrictions.** At `S = {5, 13}` write `e_{m,n}`. The row `n` is `(θ∘w₁₃ⁿ)|_{R₅}` in S0B-T1's model (its layers are
`w₁₃ⁿA_{m,0}`), and column `m` is `(θ∘w₅ᵐ)|_{R₁₃}` in the 13-analogue. **So the `n = 0` slice equals S0B-T1** (acceptance of D-0),
and every row is an S0B orbit.

**Adelic form of the gluing.** `R̂_S = (ℂ × ∏_{v | S} ℚ_v)/Δ(R_S)` with the four places `π₅, π̄₅, π₁₃, π̄₁₃`. For `m ≥ 0` the
layer `A_{(m,0)}` has its poles at one 5-adic place only, so the four half-axes read `e` plus one 5- or 13-adic component each, and
the quadrant `(±, ±)` reads `e` plus the two components of its two half-axes. Explicitly, with
`δ₅(m) := w̄₅ᵐe_{0,0} − e_{m,0}` (a 5-primary element of `ℚ(i)/ℤ[i]`) and `δ₁₃(n) := w̄₁₃ⁿe_{0,0} − e_{0,n}`:
`e_{m,n} = w̄₅ᵐw̄₁₃ⁿ e_{0,0} − w̄₁₃ⁿ ⋆ δ₅(m) − w̄₅ᵐ ⋆ δ₁₃(n)  (mod ℤ[i])`,
where `⋆` is multiplication in the module `ℚ(i)_{(5)}/ℤ[i] ≅ ⊕_{v|5} ℚ_v/ℤ_v` by the 5-adic unit `w̄₁₃ⁿ` (and likewise at 13).
The complex product `w̄₁₃·x` and `w̄₁₃ ⋆ x` differ by an element of `π₁₃^{−1}ℤ[i]` — exactly the column step's freedom — which is
D0-L3 in coordinates. [hand]

**Leaves and `T + A` in the model** (S0B-T2 at rank `r`). `τ + λ_d` has `e_m = c_τ(m) + w̄^m d` with `c_τ` periodic in every
direction; `τ + λ_d ∈ Θ_β` iff `|d| ≤ r_τ` (the proof of S0B-T2 carries over: each value class of `τ` on `U_S` is a coset of a
finite-index subgroup of `U_S`, dense on the circle because `w₅` is not a root of unity). `θ ∈ T + A` iff its `2r` `S`-adic
components are the images of one `q ∈ ℚ(i)` (modulo the global ambiguity of FS86-dB-HDint).

**The action in the model.** `θ∘w_p` is the shifted array `m ↦ e_{m+ε_p}`; `θ∘i` is the array `m ↦ −i·e_m`
(since `(θ∘i)(w^m y) = ⟨e_m, i y⟩ = ⟨−i e_m, y⟩`), which maps `Q_β` to itself mod `ℤ[i]` (a quarter turn about `P = (1+i)/2`). Minimal sets
of `Θ_β(U_S)` are therefore minimal subshift-like sets of arrays under the `ℤ^S`-shift and the quarter turn.

## §2 — D-1a: minimal sets. **`MS₄(S)` holds for every finite `S` with `|S| ≥ 2`.**

*Route: Berend's Prop. 4.1, as the brief asked. Case 2 goes through with the archimedean plane in place of the strictly
contracted subspace; case 1 goes through too. Case 1's contradiction at the band needs no Furstenberg Prop. IV.1 and no Berend
1983 §4: one line about the parity character replaces it (D1-L4). KT-2 does not fire.*

**Setting.** `G = R̂_S = (ℂ × ℚ_S)/Δ(R_S)`, with `ℚ_S = ∏_{v|S} ℚ_v` over the `2|S|` places above `S`. `u ∈ U_S` acts by
`θ ↦ θ∘u`; in these coordinates that is `(v, b) ↦ (ū v, u b)`, since `Re(v̄ u x) = Re(conj(ū v) x)`. So `u` rotates the
archimedean coordinate and multiplies each `b_v` by `u ∈ ℚ_v^×`. `|w_p|_v = p^{∓1}` at the two places above `p`, and `|w_p|_v = 1`
at every other place.
- **Chart.** For small `ε` and a small compact open subgroup `O ⊆ ℚ_S`, the map `D(ε) × O → G` is injective (`Δ(R_S)` is
  discrete) and its image `N(ε, O)` is a neighbourhood of `0`. Each `z ∈ N(ε, O)` has a unique **`S`-adic part** `b(z) ∈ O`.
- The image of `D(ε) × {0}` lies in `A`, and `A ∩ T = 0` (S0A-2).

**The dichotomy** (Berend's). Let `M ⊆ G` be closed and `U_S`-invariant.
- *Case 2:* for some `ε, O`, every `z ∈ (M − M) ∩ N(ε, O)` has `b(z) = 0`.
- *Case 1:* otherwise. Then there are `z_k ∈ M − M` with `z_k → 0` and `b(z_k) ≠ 0`.

**D1-L2 (case 2 ⟹ a point of `T + A`).** If `M ≠ ∅` is in case 2, then `M ∩ (T + A) ≠ ∅`. If `M` is minimal, then `M ⊆ T + A`,
and `M` is a finite union of sets `τ + {λ_d : |d| = r}` (one `r`, possibly `0`). [hand]
*Proof.*
1. Take `x` in a `⟨w_p⟩`-minimal subset of `M` (Zorn), for some `p ∈ S`. It is recurrent, so `x∘w_pⁿ − x ∈ (M − M) ∩ N(ε, O)` for
   some `n ≥ 1`. By case 2 this difference is `λ_v`, with `v ∈ D(ε)`.
2. Put `c = w_pⁿ − 1 ∈ R_S`; `c ≠ 0` because `w_p` is not a root of unity. `λ_{v'}∘c = λ_{c̄v'}`, so `y := x − λ_{v/c̄}` satisfies
   `y∘c = 0`. So `y` is a character of `R_S/cR_S`, which is finite (a nonzero ideal of the Dedekind domain `R_S`). Hence
   `y ∈ T`, and `x ∈ T + A`.
3. If `M` is minimal, then `M` is the orbit closure of `x = y + λ_d`: the finite orbit of `y`, each point carrying the closure of
   `{λ_{ū d}}` over a coset of `y`'s stabiliser. That closure is the full circle of radius `|d|`, because a finite-index subgroup of
   `⟨w_p⟩` is dense in `S¹`. ∎

**D1-L3 (case 1 ⟹ `M − M = G`, when `|S| ≥ 2`).** If `M` is closed, invariant and in case 1, and `|S| ≥ 2`, then `M − M = G`.
[hand] This is Furstenberg's non-lacunary step, done `p`-adically. The second prime turns the unit part.
*Proof.*
1. `D := M − M` is closed and invariant. Pass to a subsequence with `b(z_k)_v ≠ 0` at one fixed place `v`, above `p`. By
   conjugation and `w ↦ w^{−1}` we may take `v = π̄_p`, where `|w_p|_v = p`. Pick `q ∈ S`, `q ≠ p`: `w_q` is a `v`-adic unit, and
   not a root of unity.
2. Write `b(z_k)_v = β_k π̄_p^{L_k}`, with `β_k` a unit and `L_k → ∞`. For `R ≥ 0` and `j ∈ ℤ`, `l ∈ ℤ/4`, put
   `u = w_p^{L_k + R} w_q^j i^l`. The point `z_k∘u` is the image of a vector that is:
   - `c_{k,j,l} := β_k π_p^{L_k+R} π̄_p^{−R} w_q^j i^l` at `v`;
   - `ū·(small)` at `∞`;
   - contracted at `π_p`;
   - multiplied by `q^{±j}` at the places above `q` (fixed `j`), and unchanged elsewhere.

   So for fixed `(j, l)`, `z_k∘u − image(c_{k,j,l} at v) → 0`.
3. Pass to a subsequence with `β_k π_p^{L_k+R} → γ_R` (units are compact). `D` is closed, so
   `D ⊇ image(γ_R π̄_p^{−R} w_q^j i^l)` for all `j, l`. Taking the closure,
   `D ⊇ image(γ_R π̄_p^{−R} H₀)`, where `H₀ = closure⟨i, w_q⟩ ⊆ ℤ_v^×`.
4. `H₀` is open. `w_q^{4} ∈ 1 + pℤ_v` and it is not `1`, so the closure of `⟨w_q^4⟩` is `1 + p^aℤ_v` for some `a`.
   Hence `D ⊇ image(c_R) + K_{R−a}`, where `K_j := image(π̄_p^{−j}ℤ_v)`.
5. The `K_j` are increasing compact subgroups of `G`. Their union is the image of `ℚ_v`, which is dense: `R_S` is dense in
   `ℂ × ∏_{v' ≠ v}ℚ_{v'}` by strong approximation with `v` omitted. So `K_j` is `η`-dense for large `j`, and so is every coset
   of it. Since `R` is arbitrary, `D` is `η`-dense for every `η`. `D` is closed, so `D = G`. ∎

**D1-L4 (the parity line; band `[1/4, 3/4]` only).** Let `θ₂` be the parity character. If `M ⊆ Θ₄(R_S)` and `θ₂ ∈ M − M`, then
`M` contains a **corner**: a character `x` with `2x = θ₂`, i.e. `x = ⟨e, ·⟩` with `e ∈ (1+i)/4 + ½ℤ[i]`. There are four
corners; they form one `U_S`-orbit of order-4 torsion characters, of margin 0. [hand]
*Proof.*
- `θ₂(u) = ½` for every `u ∈ U_S`: `u` reduces to `1` or `i` in `ℤ[i]/(1+i)²`.
- Take `x, x + θ₂ ∈ M`. Then `x(u) ∈ [¼, ¾] ∩ [−¼, ¼] = {¼, ¾}`, so `(2x − θ₂)(U_S) = 0`.
- `ℤ[U_S] = R_S`, so `2x = θ₂`. ∎

**D1-T1 (`MS₄(S)`).** For every finite set `S` of primes `≡ 1 (mod 4)` with `|S| ≥ 2`, every `U_S`-minimal closed subset of
`Θ₄(R_S)` is either one finite orbit of torsion characters, or a finite union of leaf circles `τ + {λ_d : |d| = r}` with
`0 < r ≤ r_τ`. In both cases it is contained in `T + A`. **Inhabitant** `S = {5, 13}`: `{θ₂}`, and the parity circle of radius ¼.
[hand]
*Proof.*
- In case 2, D1-L2 applies.
- In case 1, D1-L3 gives `θ₂ ∈ M − M`, and D1-L4 puts a corner in `M`. So `M` is the corner orbit, which is finite. Then `M − M`
  is finite, which contradicts case 1. ∎

## §3 — D-1b: points. **`RS₄({5, 13})` holds: `Θ₄(R₆₅) ⊆ T + A`. No heteroclinic or switching character exists at `{5, 13}`.**

*Mechanism in one line:* a band character outside `T + A` has an orbit closure in case 1. D1-L3 and D1-L4 then force that closure
to reach a corner, and at `{5, 13}` the corners are isolated points of `Θ₄`: no band array comes near one without being one.

**D1-L5 (near a corner the deviation rotates rigidly and dies).** In the model D0-T1 at `S = {5, 13}`, let `x` be a corner. Its
array is `c_{m,n} = (−1)^m c₀`, with `c₀` a corner of `Q = [¼, ¾]²`, because `w₅ ≡ −1` and `w₁₃ ≡ 1 (mod 4)`. Suppose a band
array has `|e_{m,n} − c_{m,n}| < 0.13` at `(m, n)` and at `(m+1, n)`. Then `e_{m,n} = c_{m,n}` and `e_{m+1,n} = c_{m+1,n}`. [hand + tools exact]
*Proof.*
1. A nonzero class of `π^{−1}ℤ[i]/ℤ[i]` has no representative shorter than `1/√5` (at 5) or `1/√13` (at 13) (`corner-check`
   E). So two sites within `0.13 < 1/(2√13)` of the pattern are joined by the pattern's own digit.
2. Hence the deviations `ζ = e − c` satisfy `ζ_{m+1,n} = w̄₅ζ_{m,n}` exactly.
3. Near the corner `c₀`, membership in `Q` puts `ζ` in the closed quadrant pointing into `Q`; near `−c₀` it puts `ζ` in the
   opposite quadrant.
4. For `ζ = s + ti` in the closed first quadrant, `Re(w̄₅ζ) = (3s + 4t)/5 > 0` unless `ζ = 0`, so `w̄₅ζ` is not in the third
   quadrant. The other corners follow by the quarter-turn symmetry. So `ζ = 0`. ∎

**D1-L6 (corner exit lemma, `S = {5, 13}`).** Suppose a band array equals the corner pattern at `(m, n)` and `(m, n+1)`. Then it
equals the pattern at `(m ± 1, n)` and `(m ± 1, n+1)`. Suppose it equals the pattern at `(m, n)`, `(m+1, n)` and `(m+2, n)`. Then
it equals the pattern at `(m, n ± 1)`, `(m+1, n ± 1)` and `(m+2, n ± 1)`. [hand + tools exact]
*Proof.*
- **Right exit.** `e_{m+1,n} = −c₀' + δ` with `δ ∈ π₅^{−1}ℤ[i]/ℤ[i]`. By D0-L3 the completed site is
  `e_{m+1,n+1} = −c₀' + w̄₁₃⋆δ`, and `w̄₁₃ ≡ 3 (mod π₅)`.
  - Exactly one nonzero class keeps `−c₀' + δ` in `Q` (at `c₀ = (¼, ¼)` it is `−(1+2i)/5`).
  - Multiplication by 3 moves it, so `δ = 0`.
- **Left exit.** The same, with `π̄₅` and `w̄₁₃ ≡ 2 (mod π̄₅)`.
- **Up exit.** The deviation `ε ∈ π₁₃^{−1}ℤ[i]/ℤ[i] ≅ 𝔽₁₃` is multiplied by `w̄₅ ≡ 7 (mod π₁₃)` at each step along the row, and the
  admissible sets alternate: `{5, 6, 10}` at `c₀`, `{3, 7, 8}` at `−c₀`. Every nonzero orbit leaves them within three steps
  (`6 → 3 → 8`, `7 → 10 → 5`).
- **Down exit.** Symmetric.

All four exits are checked exactly on all four corners by `tools/fs89_s1d_corner_check.py`
(`data/fs89/s1d/corner-check.txt`: 0 admissible nonzero exits in each direction; D0-L3's uniqueness asserted on every completion).
Three plants fire, each derived to fire:
- `w̄₁₃ → 1` in the right exit (the rank-one freedom): 4 exits;
- `w̄₅ → −1` in the up exit: 12 exits;
- the band widened to `[1/20, 19/20]`: 16 exits.
A first pair of plants could not fire and stayed silent; it was replaced (logged in the tool's docstring). ∎

**D1-C1 (corners are isolated in `Θ₄(R₆₅)`).** A band array that equals a corner pattern on a `3 × 2` box is that corner.
Hence every `θ ∈ Θ₄(R₆₅)` within `0.13` of a corner on the window `|m| ≤ 2, |n| ≤ 1` is that corner. [hand + tools exact]
*Proof.*
1. Two rows of the box extend to all `m` by D1-L6's first half, applied inductively.
2. Each full corner row extends to the rows above and below by the second half.
3. Induct on the rows. The array determines the character (D0-T1). The second sentence follows from D1-L5. ∎

**D1-T2 (`RS₄({5, 13})`).** `Θ₄(ℤ[i][1/65]) ⊆ T + A`. [hand + tools exact] **Inhabitant:** `Θ₄(R₆₅) ∋ θ₂` (margin ¼).
Every character of the plan's positive-control list (parity, `(x+y)/4`, `(3x+2y)/8`, the order-3 and order-7 characters) lies in
`T + A`, so none is a counterexample.
*Proof.*
1. Let `θ ∈ Θ₄(R₆₅) \ (T + A)`, and let `M` be its orbit closure.
2. *`M` is in case 1.* Otherwise, every minimal `L ⊆ M` is in case 2 (`L − L ⊆ M − M`), so `L ⊆ T + A` by D1-L2. Take `y ∈ L`.
   `T + A` is invariant and `θ ∉ T + A`, so `y` is a limit of `θ∘u_k`. Then `θ∘u_k − y → 0` in `M − M`, and these differences lie
   outside `A` (else `θ∘u_k ∈ T + A`). So their `S`-adic parts are nonzero, which is case 1.
3. By D1-L3, `M − M = G ∋ θ₂`. By D1-L4, `M` contains a corner `x`, and `x ∉ Γθ`.
4. So `θ∘u'_k → x`, and by D1-C1, `θ∘u'_k = x` for large `k`, so `θ ∈ T`. Contradiction. ∎

**D1-C2 (the band set at `{5, 13}` is explicit and finite-type).** The torsion characters in `Θ₄(R₆₅)` are finitely many. So
`Θ₄(R₆₅)` is a finite union of leaf discs `τ + D(r_τ)`. [hand + tools exact]
*Proof.*
1. Let `𝒯′` be the non-corner band torsion characters, and suppose it is infinite. It accumulates at some `y`, which lies in
   `T + A` by D1-T2.
2. `y = τ + λ_d`, and `τ_j − y ∈ A` only if `τ_j = τ`. So the closure `M` of `𝒯′` is in case 1.
3. By D1-L3 and D1-L4, a corner lies in `M`, hence is a limit of non-corner band characters. That contradicts D1-C1. ∎

The list itself is not computed here (the ban; see §5).

## §4 — D-2: **RQ₄ holds: `Θ₄(Q) ⊆ T + A`.**

*Stage-1b choice, logged:* D-1 ended in a proof at `{5, 13}` (§3), so the brief's branch is D-2. D-2 turned out to need no weak
approximation: D1-C2's bounded torsion order at `{5, 13}` carries the layering, with `R₆₅` in place of every `R_S`.

**D2-L1 (bounded-order layering).** Let `R ⊆ Q` be a subring with `U_R := U_ℚ ∩ R` and `ℤ[U_R] = R`. Suppose `R` is
`p`-divisible for some prime `p` (so `λ_c` vanishes on a nonzero ideal of `R` only if `c = 0`). Suppose also that
`Θ_β(R) ⊆ T_N + A` for some `N`. Then `Θ_β(Q) ⊆ T_N + A`. [hand]
*Proof.*
1. Let `θ ∈ Θ_β(Q)` and `u ∈ U_ℚ`. Then `(θ∘u)|_R ∈ Θ_β(R)`, because `uU_R ⊆ U_ℚ`.
2. So `θ|_{uR} = t_u + λ_{a_u}`, with `N t_u = 0`.
3. On `I_u = R ∩ uR`, a nonzero ideal of `R`, we get `t_1 − t_u = λ_{a_u − a_1}`. Multiplying by `N`, `λ_{N(a_u − a_1)}`
   vanishes on `I_u`, so `a_u = a_1 =: a`.
4. So `ψ := θ − λ_a` has `Nψ = 0` on every `uR`, hence on `Σ_u uR ⊇ ℤ[U_ℚ] = Q` (S0A-1). ∎

(This is U1, as V proved it, with the order bound made uniform.)

**D2-T1 (RQ₄).** `Θ₄(ℤ[i][1/p : p ≡ 1 (4)]) ⊆ T + A`. [hand + tools exact, resting on §2–§3]
*Proof.*
- D1-T2 and D1-C2 give `Θ₄(R₆₅) ⊆ T_N + A` with `N` the lcm of the finitely many band torsion orders there. (`τ + λ_d ∈ Θ₄`
  forces `τ ∈ Θ₄`, by S0B-T2's density.)
- `R₆₅` is 5-divisible, and `ℤ[U₆₅] = R₆₅`. Apply D2-L1. ∎

With verified U2 (V-A2), `Θ₄(Q)` is then exactly the explicit compact set of 13 leaf discs and 16 margin-0 points. **Inhabitant:**
`θ₂ ∈ Θ₄(Q)`.

**Consequences, at their own grades (not re-derived here):**
- **C-HD1** (S0A-6, hand at FS86-dB-Theta's grade): HD1 (`Θ₄(R_F) ⊆ T`) for ℚ(√59), ℚ(√83), ℚ(√131).
- **C-47** (S0A-8, hand, given Lemma R and Weil): `Θ₄(R_{ℚ(√47)}) = ∅`. Every character four-colouring `x ↦ ⌊4θ(x)⌋` of ℚ(√47)'s
  plane is excluded.
- **Scope (plan §0.2).** These are statements about character colourings only. Nothing here bears on `χ(ℚ(√47)²)` itself, or on
  non-character colourings.

**Audit, at the rate I would audit a counterexample I disliked.** The load-bearing steps and how each could fail:
- (a) **D1-L3's density**, which is new here. It fails if `H₀` is not open (it is open: `w_q^4 = 1 + p^a·unit`), or if the
  `v`-line is not dense (strong approximation), or if the other coordinates are not kept small (they are: `j` is fixed before
  `k → ∞`).

  plant (i) shows it fails without it.

- (e) **D2-L1's `a_u = a_1`.** It needs `N` uniform, which is D1-C2.

The weakest link, by my own reading, is (a), because nothing in Stage 0 exercised it. It is the step I most want V to re-derive.

**Toward RQ₅ (not proved; the next step, sketched with its failure points).** At `[1/5, 4/5]`, D1-L4 becomes:
- `θ₂ ∈ M − M` ⟹ `2x ∈ Θ_{2/5}(R_S)` for some `x ∈ M`.

D1-L5 then says that near that leaf the array is exactly on the leaf on windows. What is left is the exit lemma with a rotating
displacement. A two-minute hand check of the right exit finds:

## §5 — D-2 continued: **RQ₅ holds: `Θ₅(Q) ⊆ T + A`** (the §4 sketch, closed)

*Stage-1b continuation, logged:* RQ₄ landed with budget left, and the plan's D-2 asks "how far the argument reaches toward RQ₅".
Every step below is the band-1/5 version of a §2–§4 step. The one new ingredient is FS81-T3 (C1(5)): `Θ₃(U₅)` is the parity disc
of radius 1/6 together with `X₃` [hand + tools exact, FS-81, two derivations].

**D5-L1 (band-1/5 replacement for D1-L4).** Let `M ⊆ Θ₅(R_S)` be closed, with `5 ∈ S`, and suppose `θ₂ ∈ M − M`. Then `M` meets
`𝒞 := ⋃_{x₀ corner} (x₀ + {λ_d : |d| ≤ 1/20})`, the four **corner leaf discs**. At band 1/5 the corners have margin 1/20. [hand]
*Proof.*
1. From `x, x + θ₂ ∈ M`: `x(u) ∈ [⅕, ⅘] ∩ ([⅕, ⅘] − ½) = [⅕, 3/10] ∪ [7/10, ⅘]`, so `2x(u) ∈ [⅖, ⅗]` for all `u`.
2. By FS81-T3, `Θ_{2/5}(R₅) = θ₂ + D(1/10)`: the `X₃` values ⅓, ⅔ lie outside `[⅖, ⅗]`.
3. D2-L1, in its general form (from `R = R₅` to any `R′ ⊇ R` with `ℤ[U_{R′}] = R′`; the same proof), gives
   `Θ_{2/5}(R_S) ⊆ T₂ + A`. The only element of `T₂` with values in `[⅖, ⅗]` is `θ₂`. So `2x = θ₂ + λ_d` with `|d| ≤ 1/10`.
4. Hence `x₀ := x − λ_{d/2}` has `2x₀ = θ₂`, so it is a corner. ∎

**D5-L2 (rigidity near a corner leaf).** Let `y = x₀ + λ_d` with `|d| ≤ 1/20`. Suppose an array is within 0.13 of `y`'s array on a
connected window. Then it equals the leaf array of `x₀ + λ_{d′}` there, for some `d′`. [hand]
*Proof.* The digit spacing argument of D1-L5 says the digits agree, so the deviation rotates exactly: `ζ_{m,n} = w̄^{(m,n)}ζ₀`. No
cone argument is needed: the array is on the leaf. ∎

**D5-L3 (corner-leaf exit lemma, `S = {5, 13}`, band 1/5).** Suppose an array equals a corner-leaf array `c_{m,n} + w̄^{(m,n)}d′`,
with `|d′| ≤ 0.06`, on a column segment of height 3. Then it equals it on the two neighbouring columns at those rows. On a row
segment of length 4 it equals it on the neighbouring rows. [hand + tools exact]
*Proof.*
- An exit changes one digit by a class `δ`. Along the exit line `δ` is multiplied by the other prime's unit (D0-L3), and the leaf
  part cancels. So `δ` must stay admissible for some displacement of size `≤ 0.06`.
- `tools/fs89_s1d_band5_check.py` (`data/fs89/s1d/band5-check.txt`) computes, exactly, the admissible sets. There are 2 nonzero
  5-classes and 5 nonzero 13-classes per corner. Every one of the 128 exit lines, on all four corners and in all four directions,
  leaves them by step 2 (columns) or step 3 (rows).
- Plants fire: a displacement of 3/10 lets 64 exits survive; `w̄₁₃ → 1` lets 8 survive.
- Consistency: at band 1/4 with `r = 0` the check reproduces D1-L6. ∎

**D5-T1 (`RS₅({5, 13})`).** `Θ₅(ℤ[i][1/65]) ⊆ T + A`. [hand + tools exact]
*Proof.* Let `θ ∉ T + A` have orbit closure `M`.
1. As in D1-T2, `M` is in case 1.
2. D1-L3 gives `M − M = G`, and D5-L1 then puts a point `y ∈ 𝒞` in `M`. `y` is not in `Γθ`, because `𝒞 ⊆ T + A`.
3. So `θ∘u_k → y`. For large `k` the window `|m| ≤ 3, |n| ≤ 2` is within 0.13 of `y`'s array, and D5-L2 puts `θ∘u_k` on a corner
   leaf there, with `d′_k → d`.
4. Take `k` large enough that `|d′_k| ≤ 0.06`. D5-L3 then extends the leaf, first along rows and then to every row.
5. So `θ∘u_k ∈ x₀ + A` and `θ ∈ T + A`, a contradiction. ∎

**D5-C2.** `Θ₅(R₆₅) ∩ T` is finite. [hand + tools exact] *Proof.* As D1-C2, with `𝒞` for the corners. `𝒞 ∩ T` is the four corners,
and a torsion point on a corner leaf is that corner. ∎

**D5-T2 (RQ₅).** `Θ₅(ℤ[i][1/p : p ≡ 1 (4)]) ⊆ T + A`. [hand + tools exact] *Proof.* D2-L1 with `R = R₆₅`, and `N` from D5-C2. ∎
**Inhabitant:** `θ₂`.
- RQ₅ implies RQ₄. So §4's result has a second route, but not an independent one: it shares D1-L2 and D1-L3.

  five-colouring `x ↦ ⌊5θ(x)⌋` of ℚ(√167)'s plane exists. **Scope:** character colourings only. `χ(ℚ(√167)²)` itself is untouched

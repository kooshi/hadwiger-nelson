## §1. First route (written before any message to Poros)

**Observation that shapes everything (it is §0 consequence 3, restated as a finding about the challenge).** At both proved
instances `F` has finite index, so every colouring is `F`-periodic, `X` is a finite set, and `P = F` is automatic.
The two instances are instances of **rigidity** (`X` finite), not of inheritance: any statement proving C3 on a class
containing them proves, on them, only that `X` is finite. So "explain C3 at both" is "explain why `X` is finite" there, and
the inheritance content proper lives on lattices where `X` is infinite. I will say this in the report as "the challenge is
subtly two challenges".

**Route H (heights, three colours).** `K₃`'s universal cover is the path `ℤ`, and every closed 4-walk in `K₃` has winding 0.
The Cayley graph `Cay(Λ, U)` is the quotient of the grid `Cay(ℤⁿ, std)` (`n = |U|/2`) by the relation lattice
`R = ker(ℤⁿ → Λ)`, and the grid's cycle space is generated by squares. So every proper 3-colouring `c` lifts to a height
`h̃ : ℤⁿ → ℤ` with steps `±1`, `h̃ ≡ c∘π (mod 3)`, and `h̃(m + ρ) − h̃(m) = w_c(ρ)` for a homomorphism `w_c : R → 3ℤ` (its
*winding*), independent of `m`. `h̃` is 1-Lipschitz in ℓ¹, so `|w(ρ)| ≤ ‖ρ‖₁`; Hahn–Banach extends `w` to a slope
`a ∈ [−1,1]ⁿ`. Planned consequences, each to be proved on paper:
- **H-i (existence).** `Cay(ℤ^r, S)` is 3-colourable iff it has a *linear circular 3-colouring*: a character
  `θ ∈ Hom(ℤ^r, ℝ)` with `‖θ(s)‖_{ℝ/ℤ} ≥ 1/3` for all `s ∈ S`. (Saturation of `R` — `Λ` torsion-free — is used.)
  Consequence: C3-aper holds at three colours in the whole abstract class, and 3-colourability of a lattice graph is
  decided by a polytope computation on the torus `Hom(Λ, ℝ/ℤ)`.
- **H-ii (tightness forces affinity).** A generator `u` on which every slope of the winding's fibre is `±1` is *tight*:
  some relation `ρ` has `w(ρ) = ‖ρ‖₁` with `u ∈ supp ρ` (LP duality), and then `c(x + u) − c(x)` is constant. If every
  cell of the linear-colouring set `Θ` is a point, every 3-colouring is periodic and `X` is finite.
- **H-iii (inheritance at three colours).** Given a colouring and a finite window, a McShane-type extension with a slope in
  the relative interior of the winding's fibre gives a periodic colouring agreeing on the window. So C3-inherit holds at
  `k = 3` for **every** `Cay(ℤ^r, S)` — no arithmetic needed. To be proved with the tight directions handled.
- **Instance.** At ℚ(√11)'s `Λ₁₀`, compute `Θ`: prediction — its points are exactly the additive colourings
  `φ : Λ → ℤ/3`, `φ(U) ∌ 0`, so every 3-colouring is affine and `F = ⋂ ker φ`, to be compared with `3Λ`.

**Route W (walls, four colours).** `K₄ = Cay(V₄, V₄∖0)` has flat squares (`αβαβ`), so no height exists at four colours and
nothing above forbids domain walls. In rank 1 (distance graphs `G(ℤ, D)`, a 1-D SFT) C3 fails exactly when a two-site
pattern lives only on transient points; with reflection symmetry that needs a *chiral* phase pair `A`, `rA` with walls one
way only. Plan: decide by exact transfer-matrix computation on small `D` whether such a `D` exists at `k = 4`, and if so
turn it into a rule-plus-proof counterexample in the abstract class. If it lands, the headline is a dichotomy: C3 holds at
three colours for topological reasons and fails at four in the abstract class, so at `k = 4` (the K instance) the
arithmetic must enter.

**Route K (the K instance, fallback (b)).** Only if time remains: what H says nothing about (no height for `K₄`), and what
G70 plus the V₄-difference structure of a 4-colouring with triangles gives.

## §3. Derivations

**Setting.** `Λ` a finitely generated **torsion-free** abelian group (rank `r`), `S = −S ⊂ Λ∖0` finite, generating. Choose
`S⁺` with `S = S⁺ ⊔ −S⁺`, `n = |S⁺|`, `π : ℤⁿ → Λ`, `e_s ↦ s`, `R = ker π`. **`R` is saturated** (`ℤⁿ/R ≅ Λ` is
torsion-free), so `R` is a direct summand and every homomorphism `R → ℤ` extends to `ℤⁿ`. The grid `Cay(ℤⁿ, ±e_s)` is
bipartite and its square complex is simply connected. `‖·‖₁` is the grid metric. Every lattice of this project qualifies
(`Λ ⊂ ℂ`), with `S = U` or any generating subset (the census's generator graphs too).

**Lemma 1 (lift).** For a proper 3-colouring `c : Λ → ℤ/3` there is `h : ℤⁿ → ℤ` with `h(m ± e_s) − h(m) ∈ {±1}` and
`h ≡ c∘π (mod 3)`, unique up to adding a constant in `3ℤ`.
*Proof.* Let `δ(m, s) ∈ {±1}` be the lift of `c(π m + s) − c(π m) ∈ ℤ/3∖0`. Around a grid square the four `±1`'s sum to an
even integer in `[−4, 4]` that is `≡ 0 (mod 3)`, hence to 0. So `δ` is a closed 1-cochain on a simply connected complex and
integrates to `h`. ∎ (This is `K₃`'s universal cover `ℤ` — Chandgotia's height functions for four-cycle-free targets,
here on a grid *quotient*.)

**Lemma 2 (winding).** `w(ρ) := h(m + ρ) − h(m)` (`ρ ∈ R`) does not depend on `m`; `w : R → 3ℤ` is a homomorphism with
`|w(ρ)| ≤ ‖ρ‖₁` and `w(ρ) ≡ Σ_s ρ_s (mod 2)`.
*Proof.* `δ(m, t)` depends on `π m` only, so moving `m` by `e_t` changes `h(m+ρ) − h(m)` by
`δ(π(m+ρ), t) − δ(π m, t) = 0`. `h(m+ρ) ≡ c(π m) ≡ h(m) (mod 3)`; 1-Lipschitz; parity of a ±1 walk. ∎

**Lemma 3 (slope fibre).** `A(c) := {a ∈ [−1,1]ⁿ : a·ρ = w(ρ) ∀ρ ∈ R}` is a nonempty rational polytope.
*Proof.* `w ≤ ‖·‖₁` on `R`, hence on `R_ℚ` by homogeneity and on `R_ℝ` by continuity; Hahn–Banach (finite-dimensional LP
duality) extends it with dual norm `‖a‖_∞ ≤ 1`. ∎

**Theorem H-i (three colours = a linear circular colouring).** `Cay(Λ, S)` is 3-colourable iff there is
`θ ∈ Hom(Λ, ℝ)` with `‖θ(s)‖_{ℝ/ℤ} ≥ 1/3` for every `s ∈ S`.
*Proof.* (⇐) `c(x) = ⌊3{θ(x)}⌋`: two reals of `[0,1)` in one third differ by `< 1/3`, but `θ(x+s) − θ(x) ∈ [1/3, 2/3] + ℤ`.
(⇒) The functional `(w(ρ) − 3Σρ_s)/6` is integral on `R` (Lemma 2: `≡ 0` mod 3 and mod 2), so extends to `b ∈ ℤⁿ`:
`w = (3·𝟙 + 6b)|_R`. For `a ∈ A(c)`, `a − 3·𝟙 − 6b` vanishes on `R`, so equals `−6θ∘π` for a `θ ∈ Hom(Λ, ℝ)`. Then
`|a_s| ≤ 1` reads `θ(s) ∈ b_s + [1/3, 2/3]`. ∎
The map `a ↦ θ` identifies `A(c)` with a **cell** `Σ(c) = {θ : θ(s) − b_s ∈ [1/3, 2/3] ∀ s ∈ S⁺}` of
`Θ := {θ : ‖θ(s)‖ ≥ 1/3 ∀s}`; a cell embeds in the torus `Hom(Λ, ℝ/ℤ)` (a nonzero integral `φ` would need `φ(s) ∈ [−1/3, 1/3]`
for all `s`), and `Θ` is a finite union of cells modulo `Hom(Λ, ℤ)`.

**Theorem H-ii (the tight directions are affine).** Let `T(c) = {s ∈ S⁺ : a_s = ±1 constant on A(c)}` (equivalently,
`θ(s) − b_s ∈ {1/3, 2/3}` constant on the cell). For `t ∈ T(c)`, `c(x + t) − c(x)` is the same for every `x ∈ Λ`.
*Proof.* Say `a_t ≡ 1` on `A(c)`. LP duality for `max_{A(c)} (−a_t) = −1`: `min_{ρ ∈ R_ℚ} [w(ρ) + ‖e_t + ρ‖₁] = −1`. With
`σ = −ρ`: `w(σ) = 1 + ‖σ − e_t‖₁ ≥ ‖σ‖₁ ≥ w(σ)`, so `w(σ) = ‖σ‖₁` and `σ_t ≥ 1`. Scale `σ` into `R` (saturation). Every
monotone grid path from any `m` to `m + σ` has `‖σ‖₁` steps of `±1` summing to `‖σ‖₁`, so every step is `+1`; a step `+e_t`
at any `m` begins such a path. So `h(m + e_t) − h(m) = 1` for all `m`. ∎
**Corollary (rigidity criterion).** If every cell of `Θ` is a single point, every 3-colouring is periodic (its tight
directions span a finite-index sublattice `Λ_T`, and `c(x + y) = c(x) + φ(y)` on `Λ_T`), so `X(Λ, 3)` is finite. If moreover
every point is **fully tight** (`θ(s) ∈ ±1/3 + ℤ` for all `s`), the points are exactly the additive colourings
`φ = 3θ mod 3 : Λ → ℤ/3` with `φ(S) ∌ 0`, every 3-colouring is `c(0) + φ`, and `F = ⋂_φ ker φ ⊇ 3Λ`.

**Theorem H-iii (periodic 3-colourings are dense; C3-inherit and C3-aper at three colours, whole abstract class).** For every
proper 3-colouring `c` of `Cay(Λ, S)` and every finite `B ⊂ Λ` there is a periodic proper 3-colouring agreeing with `c` on
`B`. Hence `P = F` at `k = 3` for **every** such Cayley graph, and a 3-colourable one has a periodic 3-colouring.
*Proof.* Take `a*` rational in the relative interior of `A(c)`: `|a*_s| = 1` exactly for `s ∈ T := T(c)`, and
`η := min_{s ∉ T}(1 − |a*_s|) > 0`. Let `L = π⁻¹(NΛ) = R + Nℤⁿ` with `N` even, a multiple of `6·den(a*)` and of the exponent of
the torsion of `Q := ℤ^{F}/pr_F(R)` (`F := S⁺∖T`). Let `B̃` be lifts of `B` and put
`ĥ(m) = min_{β ∈ B̃, ℓ ∈ L} [h(β) + a*·ℓ + ‖m − β − ℓ‖₁]`.
- *Well-defined, a height:* each term is 1-Lipschitz, the terms have one parity (`a*·ℓ ≡ Σℓ`: on `R` by Lemma 2, on
  `Nℤⁿ` since `N a*·ν ∈ 6ℤ`), and `|a*·ℓ| ≤ ‖ℓ‖₁` bounds it below; so `ĥ` has steps `±1`.
- *A periodic colouring:* `ĥ(m + ℓ) = ĥ(m) + a*·ℓ` for `ℓ ∈ L`; `a*·ℓ ∈ 3ℤ` on `L`; so `ĥ mod 3` descends to a proper,
  `NΛ`-periodic colouring of `Cay(Λ, S)`.
- *It agrees on `B`* iff (C′): `h(β) − h(β′) ≤ a*·μ + ‖β − β′ − μ‖₁` for `β, β′ ∈ B̃`, `μ ∈ L` (the `R`-part of `ℓ` cancels
  by Lemma 2). On `M := R + ℤ^T`, (C′) is `h(β) − h(β′ + μ) ≤ ‖β − β′ − μ‖₁`, true by Lipschitz, because `h` is exactly
  affine along `T` (H-ii). For `μ ∈ L∖M`: `pr_F μ ∉ pr_F R` (else `μ − ρ ∈ ℤ^T`), so the class of `pr_F μ` is a nonzero
  element of `NQ = N·Q_free`, which forces `‖pr_F μ‖₁ ≥ N/C` for a constant `C` of the lattice. Then the right side minus the
  left is `≥ ‖μ‖₁ + a*·μ − 2 diam₁B̃ ≥ η‖pr_F μ‖₁ − 2 diam₁B̃ ≥ 0` for `N` large. ∎
The three facts used are all topological/convex: `K₃`'s cover is a tree (Lemma 1), the winding is global (Lemma 2), and
the fibre is convex (Lemmas 3, H-ii). **No arithmetic of the lattice enters.**

**Corollary H-iv (the rigidity dichotomy at three colours).** Let `X = X(Λ, 3) ≠ ∅`. Then
**`F` has finite index ⟺ `X` is finite ⟺ every cell of `Θ` is a single point.**
*Proof.* (points ⟹ finite) is the rigidity corollary. (fat cell ⟹ infinite): H-iii's construction, run with no window, turns
every rational `a*` in a cell's relative interior into a periodic colouring whose lift satisfies `ĥ(m + ℓ) = ĥ(m) + a*·ℓ` on
a full-rank `L`, so `a*` is an invariant of the colouring; a fat cell has infinitely many rational relative-interior points,
hence `X` is infinite. (`F` finite index ⟺ `X` finite): `F` of finite index makes every colouring a colouring of the finite
quotient `Λ/F`; conversely a finite `X` is a finite set of colourings each of finite orbit, so `⋂ Per(c)` has finite
index. ∎ So at three colours the milestone's own question — *when does a lattice force a pair of full rank?* — is decided by
a finite polytope computation, and the forced group is then `⋂ Per(c)` over finitely many explicitly listed colourings.
**Decidability, for contrast.** `[Burr1984]` proves the colouring-extension problem undecidable for doubly periodic graphs
from three colours; H-i makes 3-colourability of a *Cayley* graph of `ℤ^r` (one vertex orbit) decidable: `Θ ≠ ∅` is a
finite union of rational polytopes, each with a vertex of denominator dividing `3·det` of `r` generators.
**A one-line consistency check against published work.** `[FourUnitVectors2025]` Theorem 1.2: relation matrix
`((1,a),(1,b),(1,c),(0,1))` gives `χ = 4` iff `3 ∣ a+b+c`. By H-i (torsion-free case): the triangle `u₁+u₂+u₃ = 0` pins
`(θ(u₁), θ(u₂)) = ±(1/3, 1/3)`, and then `θ(u₄) = (2c − a − b)/3 ≡ −(a+b+c)/3`, which is admissible iff `3 ∤ a+b+c`. ✓

**C-6. Rank-1 walls** (`work/walls1d.py`, `work/walls2.py`, `work/pf.py`; transfer graphs of `G(ℤ, D)` as 1-D SFTs). Here a

  windings `±`, and no edge between them.
- **Four colours, `max D ≤ 9`:** no walls. `P = F` was also checked directly for `δ ≤ 30` at `max D ≤ 7`: there are 16

  phases) with a common period `p`, then `p ∈ P`. And any wall between two distinct phases separates `p`, since a colouring
  that agreed with its `p`-shift everywhere would be `p`-periodic, hence one phase. **So any wall between rigid phases of a
  common period is a rank-1 counterexample** (sufficient, not necessary). **Triangle-chain family** (`scan12.py`, under the lock): `D = {1,2,M}` plus at most two more distances, `M ≤ 18`, 695

## §6. Four colours: what the method needs, where it breaks, and what a counterexample must look like

**Why H stops at three, in two independent facts, each demonstrated.**
1. **No height.** Label a 4-colouring by `V₄ = 𝔽₂²`; edge differences lie in `V₄∖0 = {α, β, γ}`, and around a grid square
   they pair up (flat: opposite edges agree; or "corner": adjacent edges agree). Lifting through `K₄ = Cay(V₄, {α,β,γ})`,
   squares close in the cube `Q₃ = Cay(𝔽₂³, e₁, e₂, e₃)` — the square-universal cover of `K₄` is the **finite** 3-cube (the
   antipodal double cover), not a tree. So a 4-colouring lifts only to a `Q₃`-colouring of a double cover, with a winding in
   `Hom(R, ℤ/2)`: there is no Lipschitz height, no slope, no fibre, and no McShane extension. Lemma 1 has no `k = 4` form.
2. **No linear criterion.** C-5: `G(ℤ, {1,3,4,5})` is 4-colourable with no `θ`, `‖θd‖ ≥ 1/4`. H-i fails at `k = 4` in rank 1.

**Why the record holds no informative four-colour instance.** C3 at a lattice with `P` of finite index is equivalent to
`X` finite (§0 consequence 3; H-iv at `k = 3`). At K, `F = 8Λ`, so `X` is finite and C3 there says nothing about
inheritance. The rich ℚ(√3,√5) generator graphs are the census's one abstract four-colour candidate, and Poros's N1 shows
their `P_found` targets are in `P_found` partly by construction (every found colouring is `2Λ`- or `4Λ`-periodic, and the
five `P_found` targets are exactly those in `4Λ`). **So at four colours C3 has no instance in the record that could fail.**

**The shape a four-colour counterexample must take (a sharpening of the previous advisor's H-1; reading, not proved).** A
counterexample periodic in `r − 1` directions needs a one-way wall between two phases (H-1). The antipode `x ↦ −x` maps an
`A|B` wall of normal `n` to a `(−B)|(−A)` wall of the same normal. **Three-colour phases are never chiral**: reflection
negates the winding (`h ↦ h∘(−1)` sends `w` to `−w`) and the colour negation of `{0,1,2}` restores it. So a wall between two
3-colour phases — for instance one built from a fourth colour — is reversible up to one global colour permutation, whenever
a winding class is a single irreducible component (unproved; Chandgotia's pivot property is the tool that would prove it).
**A four-colour counterexample therefore needs a genuinely four-colour chiral phase**: a periodic 4-colouring not equivalent
to its reflection under any colour permutation. Additive colourings (into `V₄` or `ℤ/4`) are never chiral (`−φ` is a colour
permutation of `φ`), so the phase must be non-additive, like C-5's `0,1,0,1,2,3,2,3`.

**The one height available at four colours (reading, unexplored).** On a lattice containing the hexagonal directions
(`√3 ∈ F`, as at K), every triangle `x, x+1, x+ζ₆` of a 4-colouring has three distinct `V₄`-labels, so the labels on each
triangular coset form a proper 3-edge-colouring of the dual honeycomb — Baxter's three-colouring model, which carries a
**two-dimensional height** (Kondev–Henley; the Moore–Newman height of the 4-state triangular Potts antiferromagnet, named in
the brief and not read). That is the only place an H-type argument could enter K; whether the other 48 unit vectors of de
Grey's lattice act on it by translations of the height lattice (which would make a slope theory possible) is the question.


**The nearest four-colour certificate (Kooshi's question, answered briefly).**
- **Why no Θ-type certificate exists at four colours.** The three-colour certificate works because every 3-colouring has a
  *linear shadow*: the slope `θ`, whose absence is a polytope fact. At four colours:
  - C-5 already shows colourable graphs with no linear 4-colouring, so `Θ₄ = ∅` would prove nothing.
  - A structural reason sits underneath. Label the colours by `V₄`. Every closed 4-walk in `K₄` has even label counts, so
    every 4-colouring does lift, but through the cube `Q₃` (with `K₄ = Q₃/antipode`). `Q₃`'s square complex is a sphere,
    already simply connected. So the whole invariant is a winding in `Hom(R, ℤ/2)`: a choice among finitely many double
    covers, with no ℤ-valued height and no slope polytope.
  - This is a fact about `K₄`, not about the lattice.
- **Nearest one-sided certificates for "needs five", each solver-free:**
  1. **Fractional.** The dual weights of an LP on a finite `H ⊂ F²` with geometric fractional chromatic number `χ_gf(H) > 4`
     (G60), checkable exactly. Capped by `χ_f` of `F`'s usable residue planes, and `χ_f(K²) = 4` rules it out over K. It is
     available only over fields where `χ_f(F²) > 4` is possible, which is to be checked for ℚ(√3,√5) (FS-72).
  2. **Conditional on a certified forced group of finite index.** Colourability then reduces to a finite Cayley graph (at K:
     `Λ/8Λ`, 4,096 vertices). The residual refusal is finite and exhaustively replicable, but it is still a search, and the
     forced group needs its own certificate (at K, the 2,044-point LRAT).
  3. **Restricted to a subclass.** "No additive 4-colouring" is exact linear algebra (FS70-T2), but it points the wrong way:
     it proves nothing about colourability.
  4. **The one height available** is Baxter's on hexagonal fabric (§6). It would give a slope certificate only if every
     other unit direction acts on that height by translations. That is unproved, and it is where I would look first.
- **What two-sided would take.** A two-sided finite certificate scheme exists exactly when 4-colourability of Cayley graphs
  of `ℤ^r` is decidable.
  - The refusal side always has one, by compactness: a finite refusing subgraph.
  - The colourable side has one if **C3-aper holds at four colours**: every 4-colourable one has a periodic 4-colouring.
  - At three colours, H-i gives both sides, and compresses the refusal side from a finite subgraph to a polytope.
  - Whether 4-colourability of vertex-transitive ℤ²-Cayley graphs is decidable, I do not know. `[Burr1984]`'s
    undecidability is for doubly periodic graphs with several vertex orbits. Undecidability there would mean no two-sided
    certificate can exist, and that is the first prior-art question to ask.

**Rank-1 laboratory (C-6).** No four-colour walls at all for `max D ≤ 7`. Extending to `max D = 8, 9` is queued behind the
`L21cc6506` run.

## §7. Outcome (for the report)

**Classification: a theorem at three colours, for the whole abstract class. Not landed at four colours.**
- **The theorem.** For every Cayley graph of a finitely generated torsion-free abelian group with a finite symmetric
  generating set, at `k = 3`:
  - (H-i) it is 3-colourable iff it has a linear circular 3-colouring;
  - (H-iii) its periodic 3-colourings are dense, so `P = F` (C3-inherit) and C3-aper both hold;
  - (H-iv) `F` has finite index iff every cell of `Θ` is a point.
  This contains the ℚ(√11) instance, and at `Λ₁₀` it **derives `F = 3Λ` from the geometry of `Θ`** (C-2), with no forcer and
  no solver. It does **not** contain the K instance, which is at `k = 4`.
- **The challenge is subtly two challenges.**
  1. At both proved instances, C3 is equivalent to rigidity (`F` of finite index). So a class containing both is a class of
     *rigidity* theorems, and inheritance has no informative instance in the record.
  2. At three colours inheritance is a theorem with no arithmetic in it, so what is left of C3 lives only at `k ≥ 4`. There
     the height mechanism provably has no analogue (§6).
- **Fallbacks, each as exactly what it is:**
  - (a) **Sharpened reduction.** C3 at `k` ⟸ periodic points dense in `X(Λ, k)`. At `k = 3` this holds for every
    lattice (H-iii). At `k ≥ 4` a counterexample periodic in `r − 1` directions needs a *chiral, non-additive* four-colour
    phase, since 3-colour phases and additive phases are never chiral (§6, a reading).
  - (b) **K explained.** Not landed. The one height structure available at K (Baxter's, on the hexagonal fabric) is named
    as the route and was not explored.
  - (c) **The sufficient-condition class** is `k = 3`, every lattice: a full theorem, not a partial class.
  - (d) **Candidate counterexamples: none.** The rank-1 laboratory finds four-colour walls, first at `max D = 10`, but every one
    sits in a shift with `P = 0` (7 walled sets among ~1,300 scanned); a counterexample needs a wall between rigid phases
    with a common period (C-6's criterion).

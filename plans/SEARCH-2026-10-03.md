### 2.2 The encodings, and why the symmetry breaking is sound

Variables x[v][c], exactly one colour per vertex, one clause per edge and colour. The vertex is placed at the origin by translation (every graph
here is a Cayley graph on its additive group), in both encodings.
- **plain**: selectors y[c] → colour c absent from N[0]; at least g selectors true. No other symmetry used.
- **sb** (RUNNING.md's checklist): C(0) = 0; the g gap colours are the top colours (absent from N[0]); a fixed neighbour u₀ of 0 has colour 1;
  for g = 2, precedence over the two gap colours only (colour 4 at v only if colour 3 at an earlier vertex). Sound: given any solution, permute
  colours so that C(0) = 0 and the gap colours are the top ones (N(0) then uses only the remaining colours, among them 1); the stabiliser of 0
  is transitive on N(0) (Γ_q: rotation by the norm-one group μ_{q+1} of 𝔽_q(i); U_Q: multiplication by μ_{Q+1}, an additive automorphism
  preserving the connection set), so rotate a neighbour coloured 1 — after a swap of the non-gap colours other than 0 if needed — onto u₀;
  the gap colours are interchangeable under every other constraint and no pinned vertex carries one, so the precedence chain never reaches a
  pinned colour (the FS81-T20 defect is absent). Every verdict below was reached by **both** encodings, so none rests on this argument alone.

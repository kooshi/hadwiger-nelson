### 1.3 What is enumerated — the family F

*The request does not determine a finite family; these are the choices that make it one.*

- **The object** is a fragment in FS110-Y's sense: seven flowers, one about `x = (0, 0)` and one about each ring point
  `y_k = e(60k°)`, `k = 0..5` (the ring's first step put at angle 0). Nothing between the flowers is modelled.
- **A ring flower** is the six curvilinear 60° wedges that the three unit circles through `y_k` (about `x`, `y_{k−1}`, `y_{k+1}`)
  cut at `y_k`: outside the ring `O_cw`, `O_far`, `O_ccw` (FS132-E4's cw, far, ccw on the outer side), inside it `I_ccw`,
  `I_lens`, `I_cw`. Each wedge carries **one** label out of all six. So the only interfaces at a ring point are arcs of those
  three circles — the request's "at most three wedges per side".
- **The x flower** is twelve 30° sub-sectors, each carrying one of the three labels of `x` (written 3, 4, 5); a ray at an odd
  multiple of 30° — the directions tangent at `x` to the unit circles about `y_k` and `y_{k+3}` — is straight, or an arc of one
  of those two circles; a ray at an even multiple (the representative of the open 60° range between two tangent directions) is
  straight. All three labels occur. Sub-family *simple*: each label on one contiguous arc (three sectors); *straight*: no arcs.
- **The ring** is the turning ring when, at every `y_k`, the two wedges on the ring's clockwise side (`O_cw`, `I_cw`) carry the
  sector's label `L_k` or a label of `x`, `L_k` at least once, and the two on the counter-clockwise side (`O_ccw`, `I_ccw`)
  likewise with `L_{k+1}`; `L = (a, b, n, a, b, n)`. The alternating ring is the same with `L = (a, b, a, b, a, b)`.
- **Triangle points only** is: at most three labels among the six wedges of every ring point. Also run: at most 4, 5, 6.
- **Proper** is checked exactly: for every two tiles of one label, `tools/fs110_y_check.pair_verdict` — REJECT iff 1 is interior
  to the exact range of distances between them (data in ℚ(√3); a tight contact at exactly 1 is accepted, as the held checker
  treats it). The tables hold all 666 pairs of ring wedges and all 1,296 pairs (sub-sector with its ray type, ring wedge).
- **Radius.** Rays are arcs of angle `2·atan(1/8)` ≈ 14.25° (length ≈ 0.249); every table is recomputed at `2·atan(1/20)` and
  required equal, so a verdict is a verdict about germs and not about one size.

**Not in F** (a null is a null of F and nothing more): an interface at a ring point that is not an arc of one of the three
circles — in particular a lens split between two labels along another curve; twelve sectors; sectors of unequal angle; a ring
with a sector carrying two outside labels; rays at `x` at other angles than multiples of 30° (the hand argument of § 1.5 does
not need this restriction for the three-label null; the four-label models do); curved rays at `x` other than the two unit
arcs; anything between the flowers; colourings that are only measurable.

### 1.5 The argument's shape (for the derivers' lane; [hand], one mind — mine — and unchecked)

Write `L_k`, `L_{k+1}` for the ring's labels just clockwise and counter-clockwise of `y_k`; on the turning ring the third outside
label at `y_k` is `L_{k+2}`. A pair "(A at `y_k`, B at `y_{k+1}`) rejects" means: two wedges of one label there hold a positive
measure of unit pairs; each such line below is a row of the exact table (Stage 2 checks all 36 against the checker).

1. **`O_far` at `y_k` carries neither ring label of `y_k`.** (`O_far`, `O_cw`) and (`O_far`, `I_cw`) reject, and one of
   `O_cw`, `I_cw` at `y_{k+1}` carries `L_{k+1}`; mirror for `L_k`. So `far_k` is `L_{k+2}` or a label of `x`.
2. **`I_lens` at `y_k` does not carry `L_{k+2}`**: (`I_lens`, `O_ccw`) and (`I_lens`, `I_ccw`) reject, and one of those at
   `y_{k+1}` carries `L_{k+2}`.
3. **With three labels at `y_k`, the third is `far_k`. It is not `L_{k+2}`.** If it is, all six wedges carry outside labels, so
   `O_ccw` carries `L_{k+1}`; then `I_lens` at `y_{k+1}` is not `L_{k+2}` ((`O_far`, `I_lens`) rejects), not `L_{k+1}`
   ((`O_ccw`, `I_lens`) rejects), not `L_k` (step 2 at `y_{k+1}`), so it is a label `t` of `x`; as the third label at `y_{k+1}` it is
   also on `far_{k+1}`; but a label of `x` on an outer wedge at `y_{k+1}` has its sectors at `x` in the closed half-plane facing away
   from `y_{k+1}`, and on an inner wedge in the one facing it. So **`far_k` is a label of `x` at every ring point**.
4. **`far_k ≠ far_{k+1}`**: (`O_far`, `O_far`) rejects — FS132-E4's O, O case of C132-ALT.
5. **Three labels cannot serve six far wedges.** A label on `far_k` has its sectors at `x` in the closed half-plane `H_k` facing
   away from `y_k`. The ring points a label can serve are those `k` with `ψ_k` in a closed arc of length `180° − w`, `w` the angular
   width of the label's hull at `x`; the three widths sum to at least 360°, so the three arcs hold at most `3 + 3 = 6` ring points
   in all, hence exactly partition the six into consecutive runs, and a run of two gives adjacent far wedges one label. ∎

Step 5 reads nothing of the x flower but half-planes: it holds for any finite number of sectors at `x` at any angles, straight
or curved. Steps 1–4 read the ring flowers through F's wedges. **The mechanism in one line: with three labels per ring point the
far wedge must take a label of `x` at all six ring points, adjacent far wedges must differ, and three labels of `x` cannot do
that through half-planes.**

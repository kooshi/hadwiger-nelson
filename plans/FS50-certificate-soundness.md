# Why the certificate proves the bound

*The one page a reviewer needs. It is self-contained: it assumes no acquaintance with this repository,
cites nothing of ours, and its only claim is that the four mechanical checks in
`tools/fplane_cert_check.py` imply the sentence the certificate file states. Written for FS-50 phase D′,
2026-09-14. The companion objects are the certificate `data/fs50/cert-p11-N29.json`, the checker
`tools/fplane_cert_check.py` (standard library, exact arithmetic), and the producer
`tools/fplane_cert_round.py` (untrusted: a floating-point solver proposes, exact arithmetic disposes).*

## 0. The claim

Fix a prime `p` congruent to 3 modulo 4. Write `V = 𝔽_p²` for the plane over the field with `p`
elements, `n = p² = |V|`, and `Q(x, y) = x² + y²` for the **quadrance** of a vector — the squared
length, computed in `𝔽_p`. Two points are **adjacent** when their difference has quadrance 1, and a set
`S ⊆ V` is **independent** when no two of its points are adjacent. The largest size of an independent
set is written `α`.

> **The claim a certificate makes.** Every independent set has at most `N` points, for an explicit
> rational number `N`. Since a number of points is an integer, `α ≤ ⌊N⌋`.

Nothing below is new mathematics. It is Schrijver's 2005 strengthening of the Delsarte bound — the
"triple" level of the Lasserre hierarchy for the stability number — written out for the Cayley graph
above, in the one form a stranger can check by hand: an algebraic identity between two polynomials of
degree one, plus the positive semidefiniteness of two matrices of rational numbers.

## 1. The two matrices a set carries

Let `S` be a nonempty independent set of size `s`. For each point `z ∈ V`, let `x^z` be the 0/1 vector
indexed by `V` whose entry at `u` is 1 exactly when `u + z ∈ S`. Put

    Y_S = (1/s) · Σ over z IN S       of  x^z (x^z)ᵀ
    M_S = (1/s) · Σ over z in ALL of V of  x^z (x^z)ᵀ

so that, entry by entry,

    Y_S[u, v] = (1/s) · #{ z ∈ S : z + u ∈ S and z + v ∈ S }
    M_S[u, v] = (1/s) · |S ∩ (S − (v − u))|.

`Y_S[u, v]` counts the **triples** `{z, z+u, z+v}` of points of `S`, normalised; `M_S[u, v]` counts the
**pairs**, and depends on `u` and `v` only through the difference `v − u`. Ten facts, each immediate:

| | fact | why |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | `Y_S ⪰ 0` and `M_S − Y_S ⪰ 0` | each is a sum of matrices `x xᵀ`, over `z ∈ S` and over `z ∉ S` |
| P2 | `Y_S[u, v] ≥ 0` | it counts something |
| P3 | `Y_S[u, v] = 0` if `Q(u) = 1` or `Q(v) = 1` or `Q(v − u) = 1` | in each case two of `z`, `z+u`, `z+v` would be adjacent points of `S` |
| P4 | `Y_S[u, u] = Y_S[0, u]` | both count `{z ∈ S : z + u ∈ S}` |
| P5 | `Y_S[0, 0] = 1` | it counts `S` itself, divided by `s` |
| P6 | `Y_S[u, v] ≤ Y_S[u, u]` | the first set of `z`'s is contained in the second |
| P7 | `trace Y_S = s` | swap the two summations: `Σ_u #{z ∈ S : z+u ∈ S} = Σ_{z ∈ S} |S| = s²` |
| P8 | `M_S[u, v] = Y_S[0, v − u]` | both equal `(1/s)·|S ∩ (S − (v−u))|` |
| P9 | `Y_S[u, v] = Y_S[v, u]` | the triple `{z, z+u, z+v}` does not know which of `u`, `v` came first |
| P10 | `Y_S[u, v] = Y_S[−u, v − u]` | the same triple, described from `z + u` instead of from `z`: substitute `z ↦ z + u` in the count |

P9 and P10 generate an action of the symmetric group on three letters — the three points of a triple —
on the ordered pairs `(u, v)`. **P7 is the one that carries the whole argument**: the size of the set is
a linear function of the matrix.

## 2. Averaging over the symmetries of the plane

Let `O` be the group of linear maps of `V` that preserve the quadrance (the rotations by the `p + 1`
points of the circle `Q = 1`, and those composed with the reflection `(x, y) ↦ (x, −y)`; for
`p ≡ 3 mod 4` it has `2(p+1)` elements). Each `g ∈ O` permutes the points and hence the independent
sets, and `Y_{gS}[gu, gv] = Y_S[u, v]`. Replace `Y_S` by its average

    Ȳ = (1/|O|) · Σ over g in O of  P_g Y_S P_gᵀ,    where (P_g Y P_gᵀ)[u, v] = Y[g⁻¹u, g⁻¹v],

and `M_S` by `M̄` likewise. Averaging a matrix over conjugation by permutation matrices preserves
positive semidefiniteness, and every one of P2–P7, P9, P10 is a linear condition invariant under the
relabelling (because `Q(gu) = Q(u)`), so **all ten facts survive**; P8 survives because `g` fixes the
origin and is linear, so `M̄[u, v] = Ȳ[0, v − u]`; and `trace Ȳ = s` because the trace is unchanged.

`Ȳ` is now invariant under the whole group **Γ** generated by

    (u, v) ↦ (gu, gv)  for g ∈ O,      (u, v) ↦ (v, u),      (u, v) ↦ (−u, v − u),

so its entries are constant on the **orbits** of Γ acting on ordered pairs of points. Number those
orbits `0, 1, …, K−1` (the checker numbers each by its least pair, so the numbering depends on `p` and
on nothing else), and collect the entries into one vector of unknowns `y`, with `y_k` the common value
of `Ȳ` on orbit `k`. Write `Y(y)` for the matrix with `Y(y)[u, v] = y_{orbit(u,v)}` and `M(y)` for the
matrix with `M(y)[u, v] = y_{orbit(0, v−u)}`; both are linear in `y`, and by the above

    Ȳ = Y(ȳ),   M̄ = M(ȳ)   for the vector ȳ that the set S produces.

Facts P1–P8 become conditions on `ȳ`, and every one of them is either an inequality or an equation that
is **affine in `y`**, or one of the two positive-semidefiniteness conditions:

| row | says | from |
|---|---|---|
| `g0` | `y_{k₀} = 1`, where `k₀` is the orbit of the pair `(0,0)` (which is a singleton) | P5 |
| `killed` | `y_k = 0` on every orbit whose pairs touch a vector of quadrance 1 | P3 |
| `nonneg` | `y_k ≥ 0` | P2 |
| `le_diag` | `y_k ≤ y_{d(k)}`, where `(u, v)` represents orbit `k` and `d(k)` is the orbit of `(0, u)` | P6 with P4 |
| — | `Y(y) ⪰ 0` and `M(y) − Y(y) ⪰ 0` | P1 |
| — | `Σ_k t_k · y_k = s`, where `t_k = #{u : orbit(u,u) = k}` — this is `trace Y(y)` | P7 |

**The checker rebuilds exactly this list from the single number `p`** and compares it with the file's
copy, refusing a mismatch; so the file cannot smuggle in a row that is not one of these.

## 3. The certificate, and the two lines that make it work

A **certificate for `N`** is: a rational number `N`; two rational positive semidefinite matrices `Z₁`,
`Z₂` of size `n × n`; a nonnegative rational multiplier `λ_i` for each inequality row; and a rational
multiplier `ν_j` of either sign for each equation row — such that the following holds **identically in
`y`**, that is, coefficient by coefficient over the `K` orbits and in the constant term:

    N − trace Y(y)  =  ⟨Z₁, Y(y)⟩ + ⟨Z₂, M(y) − Y(y)⟩ + Σ_i λ_i · c_i(y) + Σ_j ν_j · e_j(y).            (★)

Here `c_i(y) ≥ 0` and `e_j(y) = 0` are the rows of the table above, each written as an affine function
(`Σ coefficients · y − right-hand side`), and `⟨A, B⟩` is the entrywise sum of `A` against `B`.

**Soundness.** Let `S` be a nonempty independent set of size `s`, and put `y = ȳ` from section 2.

* `⟨Z₁, Y(ȳ)⟩ ≥ 0` and `⟨Z₂, M(ȳ) − Y(ȳ)⟩ ≥ 0`, because the inner product of two positive
  semidefinite matrices is nonnegative — write one of them as `Σ a a ᵀ` and the product becomes
  `Σ aᵀ B a ≥ 0`.
* `λ_i · c_i(ȳ) ≥ 0`, because `λ_i ≥ 0` and `c_i(ȳ) ≥ 0`.
* `ν_j · e_j(ȳ) = 0`, because `e_j(ȳ) = 0`.

So the right-hand side of (★) is at least 0 at `y = ȳ`, while its left-hand side is `N − s`. Hence

    s ≤ N   for every independent set S,   and therefore   α ≤ ⌊N⌋.                                 ∎

That is the entire argument. The empty set needs `N ≥ 0`, which holds because a one-point set is
independent and already forces `N ≥ 1`.

## 4. What the checker checks, and what it does not

It checks, in exact rational arithmetic and in this order: (1) that the orbits and the constraint rows
it rebuilt from `p` agree with the file's; (2) that every `λ_i` is `≥ 0`; (3) that `Z₁` and `Z₂` are
positive semidefinite — either because the file gives a rational matrix `B` and the certificate uses
`Z = BᵀB`, which is positive semidefinite for *any* rational `B`, or by an exact `LDLᵀ` factorisation
with nonnegative pivots when the matrix is given densely; (4) that (★) holds, coefficient by
coefficient.

It does **not** check section 1, section 2 or section 3 — that is what this page is for, and it is
hand-checkable. It trusts no solver, reads no floating-point number (a float where a rational belongs is
refused by name), and imports nothing outside Python's standard library.

Two things deliberately **not** on the trust surface. The block maps: a certificate may present each
`Z` as `Σ_b U_b Z_b U_bᵀ` for rational `U_b` and positive semidefinite blocks `Z_b`; this is positive
semidefinite whatever the `U_b` are, so a wrong `U_b` can only make the certificate weaker, never
unsound (this is how the same shape will be used at larger `p`, where the full matrix is too big). And
the producer: *any* rational positive semidefinite pair `(Z₁, Z₂)` extends to a valid certificate for
*some* `N` (section 5), so rounding errors can produce a weak certificate and never a false one.

## 5. How the file was made — for the curious only

Given `Z₁` and `Z₂`, the multipliers and the smallest `N` for which (★) can hold are **determined**, by
reading (★) one orbit at a time. Write

    a_k = −t_k − Σ_{(u,v) ∈ orbit k} Z₁[u,v] − Σ_{(u,v) : orbit(0, v−u) = k} Z₂[u,v]
                                            + Σ_{(u,v) ∈ orbit k} Z₂[u,v]

for what the multipliers still have to supply at orbit `k`. Each `le_diag` row contributes `+λ` at its
own orbit and `−λ` at the orbit `d(k)`, which is always one of the `p` "class" orbits `orbit(0, u)`; and
a class orbit's own `le_diag` row points at `k₀`. So, in order:

1. for an orbit `k` that is not a class orbit, `λ^{le_diag}_k = max(0, −a_k)` and the `nonneg`
   multiplier is `max(a_k, 0)`;
2. for a class orbit `k ≠ k₀`, let `T_k` be the sum of the `λ^{le_diag}` already fixed at step 1 whose
   row points at `k`; then `λ^{le_diag}_k = max(0, T_k − a_k)` and its `nonneg` multiplier is
   `max(a_k − T_k, 0)`;
3. at `k₀`, with `T_{k₀}` the sum of every `λ^{le_diag}` pointing there, **`N = T_{k₀} − a_{k₀}`**, and
   the `g0` multiplier is `−N`;
4. on a killed orbit the equation's multiplier is free, so it takes the value `a_k`.

Every step is a maximum against zero, so the rule never fails: **any** rational positive semidefinite
pair yields a certificate. The semidefinite solver's whole job is to make the resulting `N` small, and
because `N` is computed exactly, every candidate rounding is scored by the bound it actually proves
rather than by a residual.

## 6. The instance this page ships with

At `p = 11`: `n = 121` points, `K = 151` orbits of ordered pairs, 36 of them killed by a unit vector,
302 constraint rows. The floating-point solver reports 29.1768; the rounded rational certificate proves

    every independent set of UD(𝔽₁₁, 2) has at most 291768068882684687/10000000000000000 points,

that is `α ≤ 29` — against the eigenvalue ("ratio") bound of 34.55, and the true value 28. Because
`4 × 29 = 116 < 121`, it also re-proves that this plane needs at least five colours, a fact already
known here by other means, which is a check on the whole pipeline rather than a new result.

## 7. The circulant block form — the same argument at a prime too large to write the matrix down

At `p = 83` the plane has 6,889 points, so `Z₁` and `Z₂` have 47 million entries each and neither the
file nor the checker can hold one. Nothing above changes; only the way the two matrices are *presented*
does, and it is the option section 4 already allows — `Z = Σ_b U_b Z_b U_bᵀ` for rational `U_b`, which is
positive semidefinite whatever the `U_b` are.

**The presentation.** For `p ≡ 3 (mod 4)` every nonzero quadrance `c` occurs, its circle
`C_c = {Q = c}` has exactly `p + 1` points, and the rotation group — cyclic of order `m = p + 1`,
generated by some `ζ` on the unit circle — acts simply transitively on each circle. So fix the base
point `u_c` (the least vertex index on the circle) and write every nonzero point as `u_c ζ^θ`. A block
of the certificate is then three rational weights — vectors `a` and `b` over the `m` angles, and one
number `o` at the origin — together with a positive semidefinite rational matrix `Z_b` over `2C + 1`
slots (`C = p − 1` cosine slots, `C` sine slots, one origin slot). Its map is

    U[(c, θ), c] = a[θ],     U[(c, θ), C + c] = b[θ],     U[origin, 2C] = o,

everything else zero, and the certificate's matrix is `Σ_b U_b Z_b U_bᵀ`. The producer takes `a` and `b`
to be rounded cosines and sines of angle `2πjθ/m`, one block per Fourier index `j = 0 … m/2`, because
those make the block the `j`-th Fourier block of a rotation-invariant matrix and hence make `N` small —
but that is a fact about the producer. **Any** rational weights give a sound certificate, so the weights
are not on the trust surface either, exactly as the `U_b` of section 4 are not.

**Why the checker never builds the matrix.** Section 3's identity needs `Z₁` and `Z₂` only through their
sums over the orbits. Group the pairs first by the ROTATION orbits: for nonzero `u, v` these are the
triples `(c, c', d)`, each the `m` pairs `(u_c ζ^t, u_{c'} ζ^{t+d})` for `t ∈ ℤ/m`, together with
`2(p − 1) + 1` orbits touching the origin. Over one of them the sum of `U Z_b Uᵀ` is

    Z_b[c,c'] A_aa[d] + Z_b[c,C+c'] A_ab[d] + Z_b[C+c,c'] A_ba[d] + Z_b[C+c,C+c'] A_bb[d],
    A_xy[d] = Σ over t of x[t] y[t+d],

because summing over the orbit slides both weights together: the sums the identity needs are the
weights' **correlations**, and those are `m` integer products each. Every Γ-orbit is a union of rotation
orbits, and every pair of one rotation orbit has its difference in a single class, so the two sums of
section 3 — over an orbit, and over the pairs whose difference lies in a class — are plain bucketed sums
of these. The work is `m` multiplications per block per ordered pair of circles instead of the `n²` the
matrix would cost: about a hundred million integer operations at `p = 83`, minutes rather than never.

**What the checker rebuilds from `p`, and the control on it.** The orbit numbering of section 2 is
unchanged — orbits are still numbered by ascending least flat pair index — but it is computed through the
rotation orbits too, since the `n²` union-find does not run at 83 (47 million nodes, four billion
unions). The least flat index of `(c, c', d)` is `index(u_c)·n + index(u_{c'} ζ^d)`, because `u_c` is the
least index on its circle and `n` separates the first coordinates. That this scalable construction is the
same object as the direct one is a **control, run in `--self-test`**: at `p = 3, 7, 11, 19, 23` the two
agree on the orbit count, every representative, every killed flag, every trace weight, and the orbit and
difference class of every rotation orbit. A second control takes a small circulant certificate, assembles
the matrix it stands for, and checks that the orbit sums read off the correlations equal those read off
the assembled matrix — and that the assembled matrix is positive semidefinite by an exact `LDLᵀ` that the
circulant path never performs.

## 1. Mission context, in plain language

The project hunts new mathematics about the plane's chromatic number; the kernel holds 5 ≤ χ(ℝ²) ≤ 7. A
proof of χ(ℝ²) ≥ 6 by the only known construction needs a finite unit-distance graph that is **5-tight**:
5-chromatic, and so rigidly so that some pair of its points is forced to share a colour (or forced apart
while non-adjacent) in every proper 5-colouring. Rotating the graph about that pair then refuses five
colours (`OneDefect.lean`, the spindle theorem, kernel). Nobody has a 5-tight unit-distance graph.

Two milestones just mapped the obstacles from the graph side. M54: a compulsion at five colours can live
only where the **critical set** (vertices whose deletion drops the chromatic number) allows — at most two
points in the plane, none if the pair is far — so the graph's 5-chromaticity must be redundant. M55: a
4-colourable piece hanging off the rest by one fused hub and a matching of chords is **absorbed** (every
colouring of the rest extends over it), so a union of spindle copies over one fabric carries no
compulsion however many copies there are; the only construction that moved the redundancy dial was
thickening the witness in its own wall-breaking field F₂₄₇ (T24).

This milestone changes the input from the graph to the FIELD. Every 5-chromatic unit-distance graph we
hold is "K-fabric plus one wall-breaking rotation" (K = ℚ(√3, √11), the seam field; Theorem B says K-fabric
alone is 4-colourable), and Kooshi's questions are the right ones: how tight can the material of a given
field be made, and which vertices tighten it? The G30 discipline says: derive the theory on a bed where
the answer is already measured, register what it predicts one level up, then measure. The bed is K at FOUR
colours, where the fabric's rigidity is measured exactly (163 of 178 pairs at distance 8/3 forced equal in
every 4-colouring; zero forced at every distance of 2-adic valuation 2 or 4) and where a candidate theory
exists in the literature: Fischer's **additive colourings**.

## 3. The derivations to do first (G30: before any instrument)

**D1 — the finite additive theory of a chunk.** A finite unit-distance graph X with points in a number
field F has a **difference lattice** Λ(X) = the ℤ-span of its unit vectors (a finitely generated subgroup
of F² ≅ ℚ^{2[F:ℚ]}, hence free abelian of rank ≤ 2[F:ℚ]; for K, rank ≤ 8; for de Grey's field ≤ 32).
An **additive k-colouring of X** is a homomorphism φ : Λ(X) → G with |G| = k and φ(u) ≠ 0 for every unit
vector u of X; it colours X by c(p) = φ(p − p₀) (proper: adjacent points differ by a unit vector). This is
Fischer's notion restricted to the chunk, and every restriction of a residue colouring is one. The set of
additive k-colourings of X is FINITE and ENUMERABLE: by the Smith normal form of Λ(X) ⊂ ℤ^{2[F:ℚ]},
Hom(Λ, G) ≅ G^{rank}, so at most 4⁸ = 65,536 candidates for K at k = 4 (G = ℤ/4 or ℤ/2 × ℤ/2), each
filtered by "nonzero on every unit vector". Then:

* the **additively forced pairs** of X are the pairs whose difference lies in the intersection of the
  kernels of all admissible φ (forced equal in every additive colouring), and the additively separated
  pairs those whose difference lies in no kernel;
* X admits no additive k-colouring iff no φ survives the filter — an ARITHMETIC certificate of
  χ(X) > k, solver-free.

**D2 — the prediction this makes on the bed.** If every 4-colouring of the fabric U were additive, the
forced-pair classes of the M53 dial would be exactly the additively forced classes: the pairs whose
difference is in every admissible kernel. The residue colourings mod 𝔭₁ and 𝔭₂ are two admissible φ; their
kernels contain every positive-valuation difference; if further admissible φ exist whose kernels miss the
valuation-2 and valuation-4 differences but contain the valuation-6 ones, the dial is reproduced. **This is
the milestone's first registered prediction** (§5 P1): the finite additive theory of U predicts the dial's
forced classes — valuation 6 forced, valuations 2 and 4 free — and (P2) the 15 unforced pairs at 8/3 and 3
at 8/√3 are exactly the pairs some admissible kernel misses. If P1 holds, the **additive rigidity
conjecture** — every proper 4-colouring of a rich enough K-chunk is additive — has its first evidence, and
it is a theorem worth wanting: one level down it says no rich K-chunk is 3-colourable (no additive
3-colouring of K exists), which is the Moser spindle's fact from arithmetic; at its own level it makes the
4-tightness of K-fabric a computation on a lattice, no solver.

**D3 — what a vertex does, additively.** Adding a point p to X adds unit vectors to Λ(X); each new unit
vector u KILLS the admissible φ with φ(u) = 0. So "which vertices tighten a chunk" has an additive answer
per candidate point: the number of admissible colourings it kills, and the kernel intersection it shrinks
— computable from the field's arithmetic alone, with no colouring search. This is the additive analogue of
the descent's kill census (M49–M53) and of G34's fusion preference, and it is the first concrete meaning of
"the field as input": the candidate points are the field's glue points (`PartnerLattice.lean`: the two unit
neighbours of a pair), and their tightening power is a function of their difference vectors' images.

**D4 — the residue-plus-repair family at five colours, and the interface calculus.** On material made of
K-cosets (K itself and its images under the field's rotations — for our witnesses two cosets, for de
Grey's graph the half-spindle group's words), a natural family of 5-colourings is: each coset residue-
coloured under its own palette permutation π_A of 𝔽₄, plus a fifth-colour **repair set** on the vertices
whose chords still clash. Two cosets A, B meet through (i) FUSED points (one vertex in both: the equality
π_A(res x) = π_B(res x)) and (ii) CHORDS (unit edges between them: the inequality π_A(res a) ≠ π_B(res b)).
The interface (A, B) is summarized by its **pattern** P_{AB} ⊆ 𝔽₄ × 𝔽₄ of residue pairs joined by chords
and its fusion constraints; WLOG π_A = id, and the interface is **repair-free** iff some permutation σ of
𝔽₄ satisfies the fusions and avoids every pattern pair — a Hall-type condition on a 4 × 4 bipartite graph.
An interface with no such σ FORCES a repair (a fifth-colour vertex) on it. The spindle re-derives itself
in this calculus: the fused centre forces σ(res x) = res x, the spindle chord (y, R y) demands σ(res y) ≠
res y with res y = res x (the pair is forced equal, so residue-equal) — impossible, one repair forced, ν ≥ 1
from the interface alone (M55 D4 in residue language). Within one coset, a chord whose endpoints are
residue-equal (a "cheat": any pair at a positive-valuation distance, e.g. 2) is an unavoidable clash with
no permutation to save it — which is exactly why the seed's 446 cheats force ν = 38: no residue-like
colouring survives them. **The unit-distance analogue of a cheat is an interface whose pattern violates
Hall.** The family gives an UPPER bound on ν (it constructs colourings) and the hitting-set number of the
unavoidable clashes is the family's own ν; whether actual 5-colourings lie in the family is D5's question.

**D5 — the additive defect, the gauge.** For a proper 5-colouring c of a coset-union X, its additive
defect is the least size of a set S such that c restricted to X ∖ S is residue-per-coset up to palette
(equivalently: the fewest points whose removal makes c additive on each coset). ν(X) ≤ min over c of the
defect only if the family contains the optimum; the gauge measures how far the object's actual colourings
are from the family — on the seed's 35 colourings first (are they residue + repair, with the repair set
the fifth class? §5 P3), then on the witnesses' harvested colourings (the M48 mask engine's pools, the
M54–M55 sweep checkpoints). A defect of zero everywhere means the family IS the colouring space and
tightness is the interface calculus; a large defect means 5-colourings of wall-broken material are
non-additive in a way the calculus does not see — either outcome is the finding.

**D6 — the rigidity theorem's ghost at five.** If every 5-colouring of a rich chunk of a wall-broken field
F were additive, and no additive 5-colouring of F exists (Fischer's obstruction — to be checked per field:
is 1/2 ∈ Σ₀(F)? is 1/3?), rich chunks would be 6-chromatic outright. We KNOW this fails at the richness we
hold (de Grey's 1581 and its 24,065-point closure are 5-colourable, floppily): their 5-colourings are
non-additive by necessity. So the theory of 5-tightness is the theory of how far from additive the
5-colourings of a field's material can be pushed — D4's interfaces are the mechanism, D5's defect is the
measure, and "how tight can Heule's field be made" is: how many vertex-disjoint Hall-violating interfaces
per point can ℚ(√3, √5, √11)'s geometry supply. Nobody knows the value for any field; it is the
milestone's measurement, and the K-side derivation (D1–D3) is the control that the yardstick is right.

## 6. Failure and jackpot clauses (registered before any instrument runs)

* **Jackpot**: an additively forced pair at five colours on any chunk that the sweeps never saw — verify
  by the SAT ask (`five_dial.py ask` protocol: second encoding, jackpot file, exit 3); a confirmed hit is
  χ(ℝ²) ≥ 6 by the spindle theorem. An additive prediction contradicted by a complete sweep (a pair the
  theory forces that a colouring separates) is an INSTRUMENT BUG in the lattice or hom code — halt and
  audit; the sweep's colouring is the witness.
* **P1 misses**: the 4-rigidity of K-fabric is non-additive — a bigger finding than P1 holding (rigidity at
  k = χ_res carried by non-additive colourings); the milestone then measures the non-additive 4-colourings
  of U (their defect against the residue colouring) before touching five.
* **P3 misses**: the seed's tightness is carried by non-additive colourings; the gauge (D5) is then the
  instrument, and the milestone's centre moves to characterizing those colourings (the M20 automaton genre:
  what structure do 35 colourings have?).
* **P4 misses**: additive arithmetic does not see the 1581's 5-chromaticity at chunk level; record, and
  test whether it sees it on the gen-1 closure (24,065 points, `build/m31-*`) — the closure is the right
  "rich chunk".
* **P6's density reads high somewhere**: that object is a tightness candidate; the criticality census and
  the sweep follow (M54/M55's pipeline).

### S1. The derivations, done (D1–D3 sharpened; two claims of the stub withdrawn)

**L1 — the exponent-divisibility lemma.** Let X be a finite unit-distance graph with points in F(i), Λ = Λ(X)
the ℤ-span of its unit vectors, and φ : Λ → G a homomorphism into an abelian group of exponent e. Then φ
vanishes on Λ ∩ e·Λ₍ₑ₎ = {d ∈ Λ : n·d ∈ eΛ for some n coprime to e} (n·φ(d) = φ(n d) ∈ φ(eΛ) = 0 and n is
invertible mod e). Every additive k-colouring has G of exponent dividing k, so **every pair whose difference
lies in k·Λ₍ₖ₎ is forced equal in every additive k-colouring** — at k = 4: everything in 4Λ₍₂₎; for the
exponent-2 groups (𝔽₂², i.e. every residue colouring) everything in 2Λ₍₂₎; at k = 5: everything in 5Λ₍₅₎.
*Corollary (Fischer's obstruction, chunk form):* if some unit vector u lies in k·Λ₍ₖ₎, X has no additive
k-colouring. (Fischer's plane form: 1/k ∈ Σ₀(F²) ⟹ no additive k-colouring; and 1/5 ∈ Σ₀(ℚ²) ⊆ Σ₀(F²) for
every F by his Example 1, so **no plane F² has an additive 5-colouring** — the stub's P7 is a theorem
already, for every field, and the additive theory at five colours lives only on finite chunks.)

**L2 — the direction of every certificate (the stub's D1 corrected).** Additive colourings are proper
colourings (Fischer Thm 1(i)). Hence, with F_true(X, k) the pairs forced equal in EVERY proper k-colouring
and F_add(X, k) := the pairs whose difference lies in every admissible hom's kernel:

    F_true(X, k) ⊆ F_add(X, k),   and every additively separated pair is truly separated (the hom is the witness).

The additive theory therefore EXHIBITS colourings and separations solver-free and bounds from above where
forcing can live; it never proves forcing, and **"no admissible hom" says nothing about χ(X)** — the stub's
`certify-not-colorable` ("no admissible hom ⟹ χ > k, an arithmetic certificate") is WITHDRAWN: the Moser
spindle has no additive 3-colouring and no 3-colouring, but the first does not prove the second (a chunk of

separating an additively forced pair means a NON-additive colouring separates it — that is the expected
finding, not a bug (the bug branch is only: an additively SEPARATED pair that a complete sweep calls forced,
because the hom is an explicit colouring). The theory's value is (i) the localization F_true ⊆ F_add — a
pre-filter for any sweep, like M54's critical-set theorem, computable from Λ alone; (ii) the gauge of how

## §M1. Domain modeling pass (2026-09-06, fresh invocation after the §S6 commit)

**M1.1 The carrier is finite: (Λ/kΛ, S_k).** Every group of order k has exponent dividing k, so every
additive k-colouring of X factors through A_k := Λ/kΛ ≅ (ℤ/k)^r; the unit classes S_k ⊂ A_k ∖ {0} and the
same-component difference classes are all the data. Admissibility, F_add, the enumeration (r ≤ 8) and the
SAT form (r > 8, A_p = 𝔽_p^r, S_p the hyperplane normals) are statements about (A_k, S_k) alone; Λ (its HNF
basis) exists to compute the coordinate map. And L1 loses its localization: for d ∈ Λ, n·d ∈ eΛ with n
coprime to e forces d ∈ eΛ (Λ/eΛ is e-torsion and n invertible on it), so **eΛ₍ₑ₎ ∩ Λ = eΛ** — "2-adic
depth ≥ 2 in Λ" IS membership in 4Λ, which is what §S6 computed. The localization is needed only for
differences not known to lie in Λ, which are exactly the cross-component pairs (M1.4).

**M1.2 Two notions, named, and which one each claim quantifies over.** An *additive k-colouring of X* is a
hom on Λ(X) (the chunk's own lattice, the ℤ-span of its edge vectors — the chunk analogue of Fischer's Σ₀,
which is the ℤ-span of ALL unit vectors of F). An *additive k-colouring of F²* is Fischer's. The chunk
family is the larger one, so its F_add is the SHARPER localization (F_true(X) ⊆ F_add^{Λ(X)} ⊆ F_add^{Σ₀|X});
the plane notion is the directed limit as chunks grow. P1′, P3′, P4′, P5′, P8 quantify over the chunk
notion; P7′ over the plane notion — and the plane obstruction has FINITE witnesses: Σ₀ is the ℤ-span of the
unit vectors, so 1/k ∈ Σ₀(F²) iff 1/k ∈ Λ(X) for some finite X, and the instrument's `obstruct` on that
X is the plane-level certificate.

**M1.3 "The same colouring up to palette" is equality of the induced partitions of V.** That is the
intrinsic notion for a finite point set; Aut(G)-orbits of homs (𝔽_p×-scaling for ℤ/p, GL₂(𝔽₂) for 𝔽₂²,
{±1} for ℤ/4) are the hom-side proxy, equal to it when the difference set is rich enough to separate kernels.
Headlines count partitions; populations count homs (G21). The test "is a given colouring c additive up to
palette": the same-colour differences {p − q : c(p) = c(q)} impose LINEAR conditions φ(d) = 0 on A_k; solve
for that subspace, then ask whether some solution avoids the different-colour differences and the units —
enumeration below rank 9, SAT above. So `additive-test` is linear algebra plus avoidance, never a search
over palettes.

**M1.4 Components are a third verdict.** A pair in different unit-components is never additively forced or
separated (each component's base colour is free): the instrument reports `not-comparable`, never `free`.
The seed and U are connected (§S6); the witnesses are checked, not assumed.

**M1.5 The Kempe object, and the two-line theorem it carries (P8 restated).** For an admissible φ and
c ∈ G ∖ {0} let Λ_c^φ := ⟨u ∈ E : φ(u) ∈ {c, −c}⟩ — a sublattice (symmetric generators), with
Σ_c Λ_c^φ = Λ. The {a, a−c}-subgraph of the colouring c_φ has its edges exactly on the units with
φ(u) = ±c, so each of its components lies in one coset of Λ_c^φ. **Theorem (Kempe localization):** if
c_φ(p) = c_φ(q) and q − p ∉ Λ_c^φ for some c, then swapping a ↔ a − c on the component of p is a proper
colouring separating p from q; hence F_true(X, k) ⊆ F_K(X, k) := ∩_φ ∩_c Λ_c^φ (on same-component pairs),
and F_true ⊆ F_add ∩ F_K. This refines the additive bound exactly where the bed showed the gap: a depth-2
difference 4u lies in ker φ (additively forced) and may still miss some Λ_c^φ. **P8, restated:**
F_K(U, 4) = the dial's forced set (163 + 8 pairs), i.e. every depth-3 difference lies in every Λ_c^φ of the
two residue homs and the six ℤ/4-homs, every depth-2 difference misses some Λ_c^φ, and the 18 free
depth-3 pairs are the ones some Λ_c^φ misses. A lattice computation; if it holds, K-fabric's 4-rigidity is
"the intersection of the Kempe sublattices of its additive colourings", solver-free, and the same computation
runs on any wall-broken chunk at five colours wherever ℤ/5-homs survive.

**M1.6 The gauge.** The *additive defect* of a colouring c is its least Hamming distance to an admissible
additive colouring up to palette — a distance to a finite family, cheap wherever the family is enumerable
(the seed: 18 homs × 120 palettes on 214 points). Kempe distance (fewest swaps) is a structural claim, not a
metric, and is measured only where the colouring space itself is enumerable: the seed's 35 colourings, whose
Kempe graph (35 vertices, edges = one swap) is B2's second reading (are the 17 non-additive colourings
Kempe-reachable from the 18 additive ones?).

**M1.7 L7 — the spindle rotation is the obstruction, and the fields' additive spectra follow.** A spindle

for every spindle-able distance d with d² ∈ ℚ**. Instances: d² = 3 (Moser) gives 1/3; d = 5 gives 1/25;
d² = 7/3 gives 1/7 (4d² − 1 = 25/3, ρ = (11 + 5i√3)/14 ∈ ℚ(√3)(i)); the Pythagorean (3 + 4i)/5 gives 1/5
in ℚ² already; d = 2 (√15) gives 1/4 and d = 8/3 (√247) gives 9/64 hence 1/64. So Fischer's 1/k obstruction
is the field wall in his language, and: **over K, 1/3, 1/5, 1/7 ∈ Σ₀ and 1/2 ∉ Σ₀** (Σ₀ lies in the local
ring of the conjugation-fixed prime) — additive k-colourings of K² exist only for k coprime to 105, the
residue colouring at 4 among them; **over Heule's, de Grey's and F₂₄₇, 1/2 ∈ Σ₀ too, so no additive

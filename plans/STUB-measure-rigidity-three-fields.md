## 1. Mission, in plain language

**The field and the prediction.** Colour the points of the plane whose coordinates lie in the real quadratic field ℚ(√47), so that two points
at distance exactly 1 never share a colour. Four colours are necessary and five suffice: `4 ≤ χ(ℚ(√47)²) ≤ 5` (four by a certified witness and
by the field-level theorem, `LEDGER.md` rows 73 and 82; five by the plane at 11). The local–global conjecture (`CLAUDE.md` G48) predicts the
chromatic number of a field's plane from its small primes; at ℚ(√47) it predicts **exactly five**, at every tower level (row 75), and this is
its cheapest exposed five-clause (`CLAUDE.md` Frontier item 10).

**What each outcome would do.** A proof that four colours do not suffice pins the value at five and **confirms** the conjecture; it would also
answer Moorhouse's posed question "What about `χ(ℚ(√47)²)`?" (`CLOSED-QUESTIONS.md` §1.7) and Soifer's Open Problem 55.1 (a triangle-free
five-chromatic unit-distance graph; ℚ(√47) has no √3, so its plane has no triangles, G57). A four-colouring of the whole plane **falsifies**
it. No finite refusal can falsify the conjecture — a finite graph that needs five colours only confirms (G48) — so a falsifier here is a
colouring of the whole plane that no small prime explains.

**The wanted poster.** J37 assembled every proved constraint on such a four-colouring (§2 below). They exclude it format by format — not a
residue colouring, not periodic, not a character colouring, not a window over finitely many places, not a one-character window — and they
leave one named class uncovered: the invariant random four-colourings with **no proper factor colouring continuous at finitely many places**
(§3). The milestone's question, as the brief put it: *"close the gap, or exhibit a colouring in it."* Both outcomes count. Kooshi: *"All novel
knowledge is a success."*

**Why the milestone is about one lemma, not one field.** The three wanted posters — ℚ(√47) at four colours (J37), the type-(B) fields ℚ(√59),
ℚ(√83), ℚ(√131) at four (J38), ℚ(√167) at five (J39) — "share every step but the last" (J39's report). The steps they share are colouring-free,
and they end in one statement, **MR(F)**:

> "**MR(F)** is 'every `U`-invariant finite positive measure on `R̂` with `τ(E) = 0` is a multiple of Haar measure'" — J38 §3.5, T-J38-2 (iii).

In plain words: over the ring `R` generated by the field's unit vectors, look at measures on the space of characters of `R` that are unchanged
by every rotation in the unit-vector group `U`, and that give no weight to the characters readable at finitely many places (`E`). MR says the
only such measure is the uniform one (a multiple of Haar measure). Given MR:
- at ℚ(√47), five colours are necessary, so `χ(ℚ(√47)²) = 5` (J45 SP-Cor, §2.2);
- at the type-(B) fields, row 88's rigidity question becomes exactly the isolation statement T-B1 (J38 T-J38-2);
- at ℚ(√167), five colours are necessary (J39 T-J39-4) — FS-59's own question — and whether six are necessary becomes exactly whether the
  Euclidean plane needs six colours in the continuous-in-probability sense (J39 T-J39-5, given two further lemmas).

MR is a measure-rigidity statement of Furstenberg's ×2×3 kind (J36 F5), but for a multiplier group of **polynomial growth**, where the
literature's analogue is proved (Einsiedler–Fish 2010 on 𝕋): at stage 5 its zero-entropy half transferred (FS100-EFP) and its positive-entropy
half came from Einsiedler–Lindenstrauss (FS100-PE2), so MR is a theorem and the "reading room at a known wall" this paragraph once described was
not one — the wall was the classical, finitely generated case, which `U` sidesteps by being large. The reachable deliverables are in §6.

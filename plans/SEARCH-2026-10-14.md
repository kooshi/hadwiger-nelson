### 1.4 What the frozen format allows — the constraint that shapes every variant

The frozen checker fixes each times-`1` block at **exactly `2 + (n+1)/2`** rows (183 at 19, 63 at 11: the constant and the
182 representatives) and each times-`q` block at 2, and requires each `R` positive definite (`fs66_b1_cert_check.py`,
`Cert.__init__`, "want = 2 if times == 'q' else 2 + (n + 1) // 2"). What it does **not** fix: the number of blocks, the
weight vectors, the row set. So in this format a smaller relaxation can only show up as:

1. **fewer moment monomials** — a reduced basis `J` in the solve, the certificate's `R` padded with `δ·I` outside `J`
   (`δ` from the rounding's own shift; the cascade absorbs the padding exactly). The block stays 183 × 183, so the
   PSD witnesses (51.3 MB of the 96 MB at 19, `FS131-IKC3` § 3.2) do **not** shrink; the pair entries, the phase-2
   scatter's ops, the canonical slots and the rows do;
2. **the `q` localiser as a scalar** — a times-`q` block with a weight vector `[1, 0, …, 0]` contributes `R₀₀·L(q)` only,
   with no degree-3 or degree-4 term: the `q`-part's ops (about 150k of 603k at 19) vanish;
3. **fewer character blocks** — a block omitted shrinks every per-block cost: one PSD witness (2.7 MB, 45–62 s), the K
   table's rows, phase 1's pair-blocks.

A lower moment **level** (level one: basis `{1, φ_0}`) is the extreme of item 1 and is in reach of the same code.

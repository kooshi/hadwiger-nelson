## 0. What the paper says, in one sentence a non-specialist can read

Colour the points of the plane so that no two points exactly one unit apart share a colour. Over the whole real
plane nobody knows whether 5, 6 or 7 colours are needed. **Over the plane whose coordinates come from the number
field `ℚ(√3, √11)` the answer is exactly 4** — and that field is not an arbitrary choice: it is the smallest
number field whose plane contains a Moser spindle, the classical seven-point graph that forces a fourth colour.

## 1. The exact claim, and the exact division of credit

**This section is the spine of the paper's honesty and must be written before anything else.** The prior-art
work of 2026-09-08 (`plans/A1-theorem-A-kernel.md` §11, `plans/STUB-library-read.md` §R) already fixed most of
it, and the numbers below are not to be re-litigated casually.

| piece | status |
|---|---|
| `χ(K²) ≥ 4` for `K = ℚ(√3, √11)`, and that `K` is the **smallest** number field whose plane holds a Moser spindle | **[Moorhouse2010] Prop. 1.4(b) — NOT OURS.** He proves it and says so |
| `χ(K²) ≤ 4` — the explicit 4-colouring | **OURS.** This is the whole mathematical content |
| hence **`χ(K²) = 4` exactly** | **OURS**, and it is what three groups asked for |
| the *framework* — colour by a homomorphism out of the group generated by the unit vectors | **[Fischer1990] Theorem 1.** His "additive `k`-colouring" |
| the real-**quadratic** classification (`ℚ(√N)²`: 2 colours iff `N ≢ 3 mod 4`; 3 colours if `N ≢ 2 mod 3`; 4 if `N ≡ 3 mod 8`) | **[Fischer1990] Theorems 8–10 — NOT OURS.** Note his Thm 10(i) already gives an additive 4-colouring of `ℚ(√11)²` |
| the **biquadratic** case, i.e. `K = ℚ(√3, √11)` itself | **OURS — but see Risk R1: `[Fischer1990b]` is the multiquadratic paper and we have not read it** |
| the general construction (a colouring subordinate to a *subgroup*, with a partial homomorphism and a coset section — our "Theorem A") | **generalizes Fischer Thm 1.** On a full plane it *is* his Thm 1(i). Ours: the valuation-subring reduction, applicability to graphs that are not full planes, and the formalization |
| `1/k ∈ Σ₀` obstruction | **[Fischer1990] Thm 10(ii) — NOT OURS** (corrected 2026-09-08) |
| **every line of it machine-checked** | **OURS**, and probably the paper's second-strongest selling point |

**Corollary worth including, free:** `[CranstonRabern2015]` proved `χ_f(K²) ≥ 76/21`. With `χ(K²) = 4` the
fractional chromatic number of this plane is now bracketed **`76/21 ≤ χ_f(K²) ≤ 4`**, which is a partial answer
to the *other* half of their question and costs us one sentence.

## 2. Why it is publishable — the motivations found on 2026-09-08

**The same problem was posed three times, in three places, across fifty years, by groups who do not cite one
another. None of them knew the others had asked.** That is the paper's introduction, and it is unusually strong.

1. **Benda & Perles**, *Colorings of metric spaces* — the "legendary unpublished manuscript" typed in Brazil in
   1976 and circulated for 24 years before publication in Geombinatorics IX(3) (2000) 113–126. As recorded in
   `[Soifer2024]` ch. 11 as **Open Problem 11.6**, verbatim:
   > *"Find the chromatic number of `ℚ²(√2)` and, in general, of any algebraic extension of `ℚ²`."*
2. **G. E. Moorhouse**, *On the Chromatic Numbers of Planes* (preliminary draft, 3 March 2010, unpublished),
   §1, after identifying `K = ℚ(√3, √11)` as the smallest number field whose plane holds a Moser spindle and
   proving `χ ≥ 4`:
   > *"We have not determined the exact value of `χ(K²)` in this case."*
3. **Cranston & Rabern**, *The fractional chromatic number of the plane*, Combinatorica 37 (2017) 837–861
   (arXiv:1501.01647), concluding section, verbatim:
   > *"What is the fractional chromatic number of `ℚ(√3,√11) × ℚ(√3,√11)`? What about its chromatic number?"*

   And note **their construction is this project's own object seen from the other side**: a triangular lattice
   with Moser spindles attached at every integer multiple of `arccos(5/6)` — the rotation this project calls the
   Moser twist, whose ladder is `Copy.lean` (T13). Worth one remark; do not overstate it.

Secondary motivations, in descending order of weight:

* **Soifer's survey routes readers to this question and cannot answer it.** `[Soifer2024]` ch. 11 poses Open
  Problem 11.6 and then refers the reader to `[Johnson2006]`'s compendium "for many exciting results of this
  algebraic direction". The 2024 edition of the field's standard reference still has this open.
* **The value is not predictable from the existing theory.** Fischer classified the real-*quadratic* planes
  completely; nothing in that classification determines a biquadratic one, and the naive guess from his
  Theorem 10(i) (`ℚ(√11)²` is additively 4-colourable) does not settle whether adjoining `√3` costs a colour.
* **It is a rare exact value.** `χ(ℚ²) = 2`, `χ(ℚ³) = 2`, `χ(ℚ⁴) = 4` (Woodall; Benda–Perles); Fischer's
  quadratic families; and after that the literature is lower bounds. An exact value over a field that *contains
  the Moser spindle* is the first one past the classical obstruction.
* **The formalization.** Every step is checked by the Lean kernel on axioms exactly
  `[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]`. Nothing in this literature has been formalized before as far as the
  2026-09-08 search could tell (record the negative in the paper).

## 3. THE NAMING PROBLEM — a first-class deliverable, not a finishing touch

**Kooshi's explicit requirement: "choosing a good name (one the experts in the discipline would agree with)".**
This is a real risk and not cosmetic, because **this project's entire working vocabulary is coined in-house and
appears in no paper** — which is exactly the mechanism grokking G49 identifies as having hidden the prior art
for four milestones. Publishing in that vocabulary would make the paper unfindable by the people who posed the
question, and would look like a failure to read the field.

**Banned from the manuscript, without exception** (project-internal coinages, every one):
`Theorem A` · `Theorem B` · *the field wall* · *wall-broken* · *5-sealed* / *sealing prime* · *the seam plane*
/ *the seam field* / *seam group* · *the finite-plane bound* · *the descent* · *the dial* · *cheats* ·
*the ladder* · *the hexagram* · *pigeonhole × kill* · *G1…G50*.

**The four naming decisions, each needing a defended answer:**

| # | what needs a name | the constraint | candidate answers to weigh |
|---|---|---|---|
| **N1** | the general construction (our "Theorem A") | Must sit correctly beside **Fischer's "additive `k`-colouring"**, which is the established term for the special case. Is ours a new notion deserving a name, or *"an additive colouring relative to a subgroup"*? Getting this wrong reads as either land-grabbing or ignorance | *residue colouring* (ambiguous — used for several things); *valuation colouring*; *`𝔭`-adic colouring*; *additive colouring subordinate to a subgroup*; *partial additive colouring*. **Recommendation to test: define it as a generalization of Fischer's and name it by his term plus a qualifier, so a reader who knows Fischer places it in one line** |
| **N2** | the 2-adic criterion for when the construction gives 4 rather than 2 | Must not be *"the field wall"*. Fischer and Johnson state such conditions as plain congruences with no name at all, which is itself the strongest option | state it unnamed as a congruence on square classes; or *the local condition at 2*. **Recommendation: do not name it.** A named criterion invites the reader to ask what else is named |
| **N3** | whether to say **"quadrance"** at all | `[Vinh2005/2006]` and `[Krebs2022]` say *unit-quadrance graph* (from Wildberger's rational trigonometry); `[Moorhouse2010]`, `[Fischer1990]`, `[Soifer2024]` and the entire Hadwiger–Nelson literature say `K²` / *unit-distance graph*. **These are two different readerships and the term picks one.** Our own Lean identifier is `unitQuadrance`, which is not a reason | **Recommendation: use `K²` and "unit-distance graph" in the paper** — the audience is the three groups who posed the question — **and mention "unit-quadrance graph" once, in the related-work paragraph, as the finite-field literature's name for the same object.** That one sentence is also the bridge between the two literatures that G49 says nobody has built |
| **N4** | the paper's title | must contain the words a searcher in *either* literature would type | e.g. *"The chromatic number of the plane over `ℚ(√3, √11)` is four"*; or *"An exact chromatic number for a number-field plane containing the Moser spindle"*. **Recommendation: name the field and the value in the title.** Moorhouse's, Benda–Perles's and Cranston–Rabern's readers all search for the field |

**The test for every naming decision (write it into the subplan):** *would Fischer, Moorhouse, Soifer, Johnson,
Cranston or Krebs recognize this term without a definition, and would each of them find the paper by searching
for the thing they themselves called it?* If no, rename. A name is a search key before it is a label.

## 5. The shape of the paper (a first proposal, to be reforged in the subplan)

1. **Introduction** — the problem; the three postings of the question; the answer; what is new and what is not
   (§1's table, in prose). Two pages.
2. **The construction** — the general colouring theorem (N1), stated over an abelian group with a subgroup and
   a partial homomorphism, with Fischer's additive colouring as the case `A = Σ₀`. The two arithmetic facts: a
   norm-one element of a conjugation-stable subring is a *unit* of it; a conjugation-stable valuation subring
   contains every norm-one element.
3. **The field `K = ℚ(√3, √11)`** — the order `ℤ[ω, i, T]` with `T = (1+√33)/2`; why `T` is load-bearing
   (the Moser twist satisfies `9ρ = 7 − ω + T + 2ωT`, and the descent fails outright in `ℤ[ζ₁₂, √33]`); the two
   primes above 2 with residue field `𝔽₄`; the parity descent replacing the valuation-theoretic step.
4. **`χ(K²) = 4`** — the colouring above; the Moser spindle as an identity in the order below (citing Moorhouse
   for `≥ 4` and for minimality).
5. **Consequences** — the fractional bracket `76/21 ≤ χ_f(K²) ≤ 4`; that no `K`-point unit-distance graph
   carries a pair compulsion at five colours (state it in the discipline's terms, *not* as "the descent
   cannot end"); the honest limits.
6. **Formal verification** — what is checked, on which axioms, and *what is not*: the module docstring's own
   caveat (that a number field with a conjugation-fixed prime supplies a valuation subring with residue field
   of size `N(𝔭)` is **not** proved in Lean; the σ-stability half is). This section must not overclaim.
7. **Open problems** — the ones this hands back: `χ` of other biquadratic planes; `χ_f(K²)` exactly;
   Cranston–Rabern's rotation-closure question; and the number-field analogue of Chow's classification (FS-19).

## 9. Risks

* **R1 (highest): `[Fischer1990b]` unread.** See §4. Blocking.
* **R2: the sixteen-year gap.** Moorhouse's question has been public since 2010. Someone may have answered it.
  §4's R4 is the mitigation and it must be done properly, not quickly.
* **R3: overclaiming the formalization.** The Lean development proves `χ(K²) = 4`; it does **not** prove the
  general number-field statement about residue field sizes. The module docstring is already honest; the paper
  must be too, and this is the single easiest place to lose a referee's trust.
* **R4: the vocabulary leaking in.** §3's ban list exists because these terms are load-bearing in every other
  file in this repository and will come out of my fingers automatically. A dedicated pass, late, grepping the
  manuscript for every banned term.
* **R5: scope creep into the sieve.** The field sieve, the lift theory, the compulsion transfer and the
  tightness mechanism (M58–M61) are *not* in this paper. They are a second paper at best and are not what was
  asked for. **One result, one paper.**

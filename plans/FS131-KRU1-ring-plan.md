### 1.2 The results, in my words; what each rests on; what the library holds

Notation (the library's): `F : Subfield ℝ` with `[NumberField F]` is a real number field; `L = F(i)` is `fieldI F`, a
subfield of `ℂ`; `σ` is complex conjugation on `L` (`conjOn (fieldI_conj F)`), `conjO F` on `𝓞 L`. A *stable prime* is a
maximal ideal of `𝓞 L` fixed by `σ` (`SpectralChain.StablePrime F`); it is *ramified* when `σ` acts trivially on its residue
field (`Rigidity.IsRamified`), and a ramified stable prime is dyadic (`IsRamified.isDyadic`), so the scope
`NoRamifiedStableDyadic F` says that no stable prime is ramified (`noRamifiedStableDyadic_iff`). `R = ℤ[U_F]` is the subring of
`L` generated by the norm-one elements, `UnitRing.unitRing (conjOn (fieldI_conj F))` — the unit vectors of `F²` read in `L`;
the same ring as a subring of `ℂ` is `UnitRing.unitRingOver F` (`= SpectralChain.unitRing F`), the two related by
`UnitRing.coe_mem_unitRingOver_iff`. `O′ = stableIntegers F` is the intersection of the localizations `P.localRing` over the
stable primes `P`.

**Lemma C′** (row 100's peel; FS91-J07-4 = J10-T6). *If no stable prime of `F(i)` is ramified, the ring generated by the unit
vectors is exactly the ring of elements of `F(i)` that are integral at every stable prime.* The derivation's own statement is
wider and per prime: `R̄_p = ∏_{stable 𝔓 | p} Ô_𝔓` for every `p`, "except possibly `p = 2` when some stable dyadic place is
ramified"; the typed head statement is its global corollary. Rests on: by hand, three derivations (J09's J2, J07-4,
J10-T6). **What the library holds** (all on the standard three axioms):

- `Rigidity.unitRing_le_stableIntegers` — `R ⊆ O′` (every real number field).
- `Rigidity.exists_mul_stableIntegers_le` — `c·O′ ⊆ R` for some natural `c ≠ 0` (every real number field, no scope).
- `Rigidity.Counting.exists_normOneL_residue`, `exists_normOneL_residues` (`Rigidity/CountResidue`, FS131-ICL2) — **local
  Hilbert 90 modulo `P^m`, and jointly at finitely many unramified stable primes by the Chinese remainder theorem in `𝓞 L`**:
  every family of norm-one residues `s_P ∈ 𝓞 L ⧸ P^m` (`P ∈ Φ`, each unramified) is the family of residues of one unit
  vector.
- `Rigidity.Counting.exists_mem_nsmul_of_resR_eq_zero` — "Lemma C (d)": residues zero at finitely many stable primes at the
  exponent `m = |𝓞 L ⧸ (M)|` put an element of `R` in `N·R`; its proof holds, inline, the step "`x ∈ 𝓞_P` with residue `0`
  at `P^m` and `M ∉ P^m` gives `x/M ∈ 𝓞_P`" (the valuation-ring dichotomy, no valuation computed).
- `UpperHalf.localRing_ne_top`, `UpperHalf.residue_*`, `UpperHalf.isUnit_mk_pow` — the localization and its residue maps.

What is missing: that the residues of `R` at finitely many unramified stable primes fill the whole product ring
`∏ 𝓞 L ⧸ P^m` (the norm-one residues generate it as a ring), and the assembly. The statement as typed, with its unproved body,
was removed from the library at the five-colour chain's landing (`data/fs131/lch1/ring-scope-lemma-c-prime.lean`).

**The components clause** (the asking lane's C8). *For every real number field `F`, the unit-distance graph on `F²` has
infinitely many connected components.* Rests on: by hand ("in every case"); for multiquadratic fields it is
`[Fischer1990b]`'s Corollary ("infinitely many components", read 2026-09-16). **What the library holds:** no statement about
reachability or connected components of the plane; `exists_sum_eq_of_mem_unitRingOver` (membership in `R` is a finite sum of
unit vectors); `UpperHalf.localRing_ne_top`. A stable prime is exhibited at `ℚ` (`UpperHalf.exists_stablePrime_rat`),
`ℚ(√47)` (`exists_stablePrime_eleven`), `ℚ(√3)` (`exists_stablePrime_sqrt_three`), the standing field
(`StandingFive.exists_stablePrime_two`) and at `ℚ` above every `p ≡ 3 (mod 4)` (`PadicPlane.exists_stablePrime_above`); **no
theorem says that every real number field has one** — `LEDGER.md` row 105: "That statement ranges over a type the kernel does
not show to be non-empty (a stable prime exists by Chebotarev)".

### 1.3 Findings from the readback

- **F1 — the previous map is out of date: three of its eight leaves are held.** `plans/FS131-KCS1-class-plan.md` § 2.6
  prices (b2) the CRT reduction (100 lines), (b3) local Hilbert 90 (70) and (b4) CRT for the unit vectors (60) as missing;
  all three are kernel theorems on `main` since FS131-ICL2 (`Counting.exists_normOneL_residue(s)`, and the division step
  inside `Counting.exists_mem_nsmul_of_resR_eq_zero`). The previous record does not name `Rigidity/CountResidue`.
- **F2 — the components clause is exactly the existence of a stable prime.** If `F(i)` had no stable prime then `O′ = L`
  (an empty intersection) and `c·O′ ⊆ R` gives `R = L`: the plane would be **connected**. Conversely `R ⊆ 𝓞_P ≠ L` at any
  stable prime. And for every subfield `F ⊆ ℝ` the plane is connected or has infinitely many components: `L` is a
  `ℚ`-vector space, so a subgroup of finite index `d` holds `d·L = L`. So "infinitely many components in every case" ⟺
  "every real number field has a stable prime", and the previous planner's "Chebotarev-type fact" is the whole clause.
- **F3 — the stable prime exists at every real number field by an elementary route, with no Chebotarev and no outside
  theorem** (derived here; the step is § 2.4's route E, [hand], one mind, to be checked by the kernel): the minimal
  polynomial `f` of an integral primitive element `θ ∈ F ⊆ ℝ` changes sign at the real simple root `θ`; at `x/k` just beside
  `θ` with `k = D^a`, `x ≡ 1 (mod D)`, `D = 4·M`, the integer `N = −kⁿ f(x/k) > 0` is `≡ −1 (mod D)`, so it has a prime
  factor `q ≡ 3 (mod 4)` prime to `M` and to `k`, and `f` has the root `x/k` modulo `q`. Kummer–Dedekind (already in the
  library's `Rigidity/AdmissiblePrime`) then gives a prime of `F` of residue field `𝔽_q`, `q ≡ 3 (mod 4)` — a **usable
  prime** — and the prime of `F(i)` above it is stable (`UpperHalf.exists_stablePrime_card_eq_sq`, FS131-ICS1's bridge,
  proved on its branch). So every real number field has a usable prime and a stable prime, and its plane has infinitely
  many components, on the standard three axioms. This also discharges row 105's "a stable prime exists by Chebotarev".
- **F4 — the head statement is narrower than its derivation.** FS91-J07-4 is per prime: it holds at every odd `p` with no
  hypothesis. In the kernel's words that is: **for every real number field, every `x ∈ O′` has `2^a·x ∈ R` for some `a`**
  (`R` and `O′` agree once 2 is inverted), from the same lemma at no extra cost (§ 2.2, leaf U7). A wider kernel statement,
  not a wider truth: J07-4 already says it.
- **F5 — three copies of one set.** The norm-one residues `{x : x·x̄ = 1}` of `𝓞 L ⧸ P^m` are defined three times:
  `CertLink.normOneSet`, `Counting.normOneQ` (both public) and `ClassRatio`'s private `levelSet`. This job reads
  `Counting.normOneQ` (its module is the one it imports); a hygiene item for whoever next touches `SpectralChain`, where
  `levelTarget` is.

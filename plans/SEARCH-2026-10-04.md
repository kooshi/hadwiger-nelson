### 1.2 The decision: run row 6 today

**Run it.** Reasons, weighed against the frontier:
- **Cost: negligible.** For fixed `(d, φ)` the colour of every partner is decided by where the cross relation meets the six arc boundaries of
  `T₁(u)`, and those meeting points are closed-form (the two points where `T₁(v)` meets the unit circle about a boundary point). So each `(d, φ)`
  is a finite combinatorial check on at most 26 breakpoints of `T₁(v)`, and the offset `ψ` (row 6) or the free colouring of `T₁(v)` (TS's
  reduction) is decided exactly from them. A full scan of `(d, φ)` at fine resolution is minutes of CPU and well under 1 GiB — far inside the
  day's window and memory, and it takes nothing from the two Lean builds beyond a few cores.
- **What a result changes.** FS-130 (`plans/STUB-bicycle-windings.md`, ranked 18 by FS129-P1; bearing on the answer "none known") lists "G21 (no
  held inhabitant)" and asks, in its existence form, whether a three-colouring by finitely many arcs exists at a given `d`. A **yes** gives the
  stub a held inhabitant by formula at named `d`, with an exact check, and turns its structural form's question (which windings the cross curve
  permits) into one with an example. A **no** over the row's two-offset ansatz *and* TS's one-offset reduction says that every arc
  three-colouring of the bicycle needs `T₁(u)` to be other than a uniform alternating two-colouring — it removes the one family the stub names
  and re-aims its construction (non-uniform two-colourings `E ∪ (E′ + 60°) ∪ …`, or three colours on both circles). Either outcome edits the stub.
- **Against it:** the bearing on the answer is "none known", and the route that minted C-T-BIKE (FS-107's R-S) is closed (row 133). At this cost
  that does not outweigh a definite answer for the stub; at a cost of hours it would.

### 1.3 The object, made exact (what is checked)

Put `u = 0`, `v = (d, 0)`, `1 < d < 2`; `θ` is the angle on `T₁(u)` about `u`, `η` the angle on `T₁(v)` about `v`, both from the `+x` axis. Edges of
the bicycle: points of one circle 60° apart; and **cross pairs** `x ∈ T₁(u)`, `y ∈ T₁(v)`, `|x − y| = 1`. For `y ∈ T₁(v)` with `|y − u| ≤ 2` its
partners on `T₁(u)` are at `θ = arg(y − u) ± arccos(|y − u|/2)`.

- **TS's reduction (call it TS1).** `T₁(u)` gets colours 1, 2 on the alternating half-open arcs `[φ + 60k, φ + 60(k+1))`, colour 1 at even `k`.
  Each `y ∈ T₁(v)` gets a list: no partners → `{1,2,3}`; partners of different colours (TS's `F`) → `{3}`; both partners 1 → `{2,3}`; both 2 →
  `{1,3}`. Within `T₁(v)` the 60° edges split the circle into hexagons `{t + 60k}`, `t ∈ [0°, 60°)`, independent of one another, so `T₁(v)` is
  colourable iff every hexagon is list-colourable — a finite table (`3⁶` colourings per list pattern). The lists are constant between
  breakpoints; so for given `(d, φ)` the generic answer is exact once the breakpoints are, and a colouring found is piecewise constant (finitely
  many arcs). Within `T₁(u)` the alternating 60° arcs are proper by construction.
- **The row's ansatz (FS107-2).** Additionally `T₁(v)` is alternating `{1, 3}` with offset `ψ`: colour-1 arcs `[ψ + 60k, ψ + 60(k+1))`, even `k`.
  Colour 3 is in every list, so FS107-2 at `(d, φ, ψ)` is proper iff the colour-1 arcs of `T₁(v)` miss `S = {y : some partner has colour 1}`. For
  fixed `(d, φ)` the admissible `ψ` form an explicit subset of `[0°, 120°)`, computed exactly from the intervals of `S`.
- **Measure-zero points.** Arc endpoints and the circles' two intersection points (each has exactly four neighbours, two on each circle, whose
  pattern colours take at most two values, so it can always be recoloured) are settled in the exact check of any positive, not in the scan; a
  generic failure on an open set of hexagons or of `η` is robust and needs none of them.

- The maximiser is closed-form: **`φ = γ + 60°` with `γ = arccos(d/2)`** — an arc boundary of `T₁(u)` exactly at the circles' intersection
  point `p = (cos γ, sin γ)` — and the only admissible offset is **`ψ ≡ 180° − γ` (mod 120°)**, a boundary of `T₁(v)` at the same point `p`

- Mechanism, read off the breakpoints at `d = 1.9, 1.8, 1.75`: `S` (where some partner has colour 1) contains the arc `(η_p − 60°, η_p)` of
  length exactly 60° — its ends are `p`'s own v-neighbour `rot_v(p, −60°)` (a partner of the boundary point `p`) and `p` itself (the partner of
  the boundaries `γ ± 60°`, which are `p`'s u-neighbours) — so the colour-3 arc of `T₁(v)` it must sit in has no slack, and the blocked set of
  `ψ` is an open interval of length exactly 120°, missing one point. These are identities in `p`, not numerical coincidences.
- So FS107-2 is at best a **measure-zero family**: if the tangent configuration is exactly proper (with `p` recoloured: its four neighbours

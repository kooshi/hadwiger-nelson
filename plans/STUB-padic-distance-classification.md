# FS-19 — The 𝔭-adic classification of forbidden distances (pre-subplan stub)

*Minted 2026-09-08 at the FS-18 close (`plans/STUB-library-read.md` §R9). The one genuinely new research
direction the library read produced, and it is a derivation with the machinery already in the kernel — which
is what G46's ranking rule says to prefer.*

## 0. The question, in one line

**Over a number field, which SETS of distances does the residue colouring forbid — and is the obvious necessary
condition also sufficient?**

## 1. Where it comes from

`[Chow1993]` (*Distances forbidden by two-colorings of ℚ³ and Aₙ*, Discrete Math. 115) proves a **complete
classification** for the rational case. His **two-index** of a distance `d` is the `k` in `d = √(2^k p/q)` with
`p, q` odd — i.e. exactly `v₂(d²)` — and his Theorem 1 reads:

> Let `D ⊂ ℝ⁺`, let `X ∈ {ℚ³, A₁, A₂, A₃, …}` and let `D′` be the set of distances in `D` that are
> `X`-attainable. If `D` is forbidden by some two-colouring of `X`, then every element of `D′` has the same
> two-index `k`. Moreover, (a) if `X = ℚ³` then `k` is even; (b) if `X = Aₙ`, `n ≥ 4`, then `k = 0`; (c) if
> `X = A₂` or `A₃` then `k` is even and non-negative; (d) if `X = A₁` then `k ≥ 0`. **Conversely, these
> necessary conditions on `D′` are sufficient.**

He adds: *"Thus Theorem 1 completely solves the problem of distances forbidden by two-colorings of `ℚⁿ` and
`Aₙ` for all `n`."*

**The number-field analogue appears unasked.** Nothing in `[Fischer1990]`, `[Moorhouse2010]`, `[Johnson1987]`,
`[Johnson1990]`, `[Vinh2005]`, `[Vinh2006]` or `[CervantesKrebs2023]` classifies forbidden distance *sets* over
a field beyond `ℚ`; Fischer classifies which *planes* are `k`-colourable, which is the other axis.

## 2. Why this project can ask it and others could not

`[Fischer1990]` and everything after it colours by a homomorphism out of `Σ₀`, the group generated by the unit
vectors. **Theorem A (`ResidueColouring.lean`, kernel-checked 2026-09-08) is strictly more general**: a graph
*drawn* in an abelian group by any `f : V → M`, whose edge differences lie in a subgroup `A` and are non-zero
under `ψ : A →+ G`, is `|G|`-colourable — no metric, no field, no ring, no Cayley structure, and the edge set
need not be translation-invariant. So the object "the graph on `F²` whose edges are the pairs at a distance in
`D`" is inside its hypotheses for **any** `D`, where the classical framework only reaches `D = {1}`.

And M60 already has the two ends of the answer as kernel theorems: `Forces.quad_sub_eq_zero` and
`Separated.quad_sub_eq_one` — the distance law — give, over a field where `𝔭` is usable, that a pair forced
equal at five colours has `𝔭 ∣ d²` and a virtual edge has `d² ≡ 1 (mod 𝔭)`.

## 3. The conjecture to derive (registered before any work)

> **C1.** Let `F` be a number field, `𝔭` a prime of `F(i)` fixed by complex conjugation, and `c_𝔭` the residue
> colouring of `F²` by `O/𝔭`. Then `c_𝔭` forbids exactly the distances `d` with `v_𝔭(d²) = 0`.

The forward half should be immediate from Theorem A (a unit vector is a unit of the valuation subring, so its
residue is non-zero — `map_ne_zero_of_mem_normOne`; scale). **The converse — that a distance of non-zero
valuation is genuinely realized monochromatically — is the content**, and is where Chow's "moreover, sufficient"
half lives. G36 already records the `d = 2` instance measured on our own objects.

> **C2 (the set version, the real target).** Classify the sets `D` forbidden by *some* `N(𝔭)`-colouring of `F²`,
> by the multiset `{v_𝔭(d²) : d ∈ D}` — the direct analogue of Chow's Theorem 1, with `v_𝔭` for the two-index.

> **C3 (the sharp question, and the one that bears on `χ ≥ 6`).** Chow's classification says a 2-colouring of
> `ℚ³` forbids only distances of a single two-index. **Is the same rigidity true at `N(𝔭) > 2`** — i.e. does a
> `4`- or `5`-colouring of a number-field plane forbid only one valuation class? If yes, it is a *structural*
> reason the residue method cannot see the 5–7 interval (G38/G43's "blind above four"), stronger than the
> counting reason we currently give.

## 4. Registered predictions

* **P1** — C1 holds and its forward half is under thirty lines of Lean on top of `ResidueColouring.lean`.
* **P2** — C2's necessary half holds verbatim with `v_𝔭` for the two-index; its sufficiency needs the
  attainability question (which distances are realized in `F²` at all) and is where the work is.
* **P3** — C3 is **false** at `N(𝔭) = 4`: a `𝔽₄`-colouring forbids more than one valuation class, because the
  kernel of `ψ` is bigger. *(Registered against my own inclination, because the rational case is so clean.)*
* **P4** — the attainability question over a number field is the three-squares theorem's analogue and is
  classical; the literature to search first is **quadratic forms over number fields / Hasse–Minkowski**, not
  the colouring literature. (Prior-art gate, `WORKFLOW.md` step 3a.)

## 5. Bearing on χ(ℝ²) ≥ 6, stated honestly

**Low-to-medium, and mostly indirect.** This does not build a 6-forcer and does not touch a wall. What it buys:
(a) a *complete* statement of what the project's own central instrument can and cannot forbid, which is the kind
of thing a write-up needs and which G38/G43 currently assert rather than prove; (b) C3, which if true is a
structural explanation of the residue method's blindness above four; (c) it is a **derivation**, cheap, with the
machinery in the kernel already, and it answers the number-field analogue of a completely-solved rational
problem — the shape G46 says to prefer over an object search.

**It should NOT displace FS-6.** The 6-hunt's design target is unaffected by any of this.

## §Lead — the object is right, the QUESTION is one notch off, and one headline number is an artifact

The stub asks *"is `Prof` the integer points of a polytope?"*. Measured on the data it already
holds, the answer is **no on the L side (2 exceptions) and untestable on the S side** — and the
structure that the polytope framing was reaching for is present, sharper, and of a different
species.

**The artifact, first, because it is load-bearing.** §2's *"fill fraction 0.20"* on the L side is a
measurement of a **dimension gap**, not of sparsity. `Prof` at 375 is **2-dimensional**, not
3-dimensional: it satisfies two exact linear equations, not one.

```
(2,0,6,4) + (10,0,2,4) = 12        (4,0,10,2) + (6,2,8,0) = 12
```

Their sum is the `Σ f_o = 24` the stub already imposes; the two separately are new. So the box
(1,225 points), the box ∩ sum-hyperplane (135), and the box ∩ **aff(Prof)** (35) are three
different denominators, and only the third is a statistic about structure:

| | S (136 level) | L (375 level) |
|---|---|---|
| varying orbits | 3 | 4 |
| **affine dim of `Prof`** | **2** | **2** |
| box ∩ sum-hyperplane | 5 | 135 |
| **box ∩ aff(`Prof`)** | **5** | **35** |
| `\|Prof\|` | 4 | 27 |
| stub's fill fraction | 0.80 | **0.20** |
| **honest fill fraction** | **0.80** | **0.77** |

**The two sides are the same density once dimension is accounted for.** §2's finding (i), *"the
profiles are sparse in the box"*, and the implied contrast between the two sides, both dissolve.
The 108 "exclusions to explain" decompose as **100 by two linear equations, 6 by convexity, 2 by
neither** — three mechanisms merged into one number.

## §1 — The null model (his priority question), and the answer is *report dimension, then test the
right property*

**Computed nulls, both conditioned on the same bounding box as `Prof` (the box is defined AS the
bounding box, so any null must match it or it is not a null of this measurement):**

| test | S side | L side |
|---|---|---|
| `Prof = conv(Prof) ∩ ℤ` — **does it hold?** | **yes** | **NO — 2 exceptions: (5,3), (5,4)** |
| same-bbox subsets of that size that are conv-exact | **3 of 4 = 75 %** | **11 of 199,919 = 0.0055 %** |
| `Prof` is an **order ideal** (down-set) — does it hold? | **yes** | **yes** |
| same-bbox subsets that are order ideals | 1 of 4 = 25 % | **0 of 199,919; exhaustively 13 of C(35,8) = 23,535,820 → p ≈ 5.5·10⁻⁷** |

**So his worry is right on the S side and wrong on the L side, and both readings are ones the stub
did not anticipate.**

- **S side: phase 1 as written is unfalsifiable.** Three of the four same-bounding-box 4-subsets of
  a 5-point grid are conv-exact. The hull is a triangle of area 1; by Pick it *cannot* contain an
  interior lattice point. The test measures the check.
- **L side: phase 1 would have discriminated — and it FAILS.** Conv-exactness at 27-of-35 is a
  1-in-18,000 event, so had it held it would have been real evidence. It does not hold. And no
  linear inequality, `Y`-derived or otherwise, can exclude (5,3) and (5,4), because a valid
  inequality on `Prof` is valid on `conv(Prof)`. **C1's own falsifier fires.**

**The test to run instead, at the same cost:** *is `Prof` an order ideal in its varying
coordinates?* It holds on both sides, it is 4 bits of orientation search away from free, and on the
L side it is a 10⁻⁶ event. That is a discriminating measurement with a computed null, and it is the
one the data is asking for.

## §2 — Is "polytope" the right species? **No — and the honest species is measured, not guessed**

Three facts, all computed:

**(a) The difference lattice of `Prof` is generated by SINGLE ORBIT SWAPS `e_o − e_o'`,
realised between two actual profiles.**

- S: **all six** swaps among its three varying orbits occur → **one exchange class of size 3**,
  total 27.
- L: exactly the four swaps `±(e_a − e_d)` and `±(e_b − e_c)` occur, and **no swap across the two
  pairs** → **two exchange classes of size 2**, totals 12 and 12.

**(b) `aff(Prof)` is therefore a product of constant-sum simplices, one per exchange class.**
That is the affine half of C1 — and it is exactly an exchange/matroid-flavoured statement, not a
polyhedral one.

**(c) Inside the affine hull, `Prof` is an order ideal that is not convex.** On the L side it is
literally `{a ≤ 4} ∪ {b ≤ 2}` — **a union of two boxes, i.e. one disjunction**. Low complexity, as
C1 hoped; disjunctive, as C1 did not allow.

**And there is a theorem that says "polytope" had to fail.** `{M}` lives upstairs in `ℤ^Atoms`;
`Prof = f({M})` is its image under a linear map. **The image of the integer points of a polyhedron
under a linear map is a semilinear (Presburger) set — a finite union of polyhedra intersected with
lattice cosets — not in general the integer points of the image polytope** (Ginsburg–Spanier;
Barvinok–Woods and successors for the algorithmics). **So the generic species of `Prof` is
semilinear, and "one polytope" is the special case.** Our two exceptions are the generic behaviour
of projections, not evidence against structure.

**The repair, and it is a real improvement to the conjecture:**

> **C1′ (upstairs).** The covering hypergraph `Y` is **ideal** — its covering polyhedron
> `{x ≥ 0 : Ax ≥ 1}` is integral — and hence `{M}` is exactly the integer points of the optimal
> face of that polyhedron.
>
> **C1″ (downstairs).** `Prof` is semilinear with description = (the exchange-class equations) ∩
> (the box) ∩ (an order ideal), and the ideal's staircase is read off `Y`.

C1′ is a **decidable, named, literature-backed** question with a cheap LP test. C1″ is what the data
already shows. Splitting them is what makes the phase falsifiable: the projection step is where
convexity is *expected* to die, so its death stops being a refutation and becomes the mechanism.

## §5 — Prior art, which he said is worth more than advice. **The gate was not run on this stub.**

WORKFLOW step 3a. Four literatures own pieces of this, none named in the stub. Searched in each
field's own vocabulary, not ours.

**(a) The polytope question has a name and a sharp hypothesis: IDEALNESS.**
`{M}` is the minimum hitting sets of `Y` — §O already proves this. A 0/1 matrix `A` is **ideal**
when `{x ≥ 0 : Ax ≥ 1}` is integral; then the minimum hitting sets are exactly the integer points
of that polyhedron's optimal face, which is C1 stated upstairs, as a theorem with a testable
hypothesis. **Lehman** characterised the minimally non-ideal matrices completely (his covering
polyhedron has a *unique* fractional vertex, and the blocker of a minimally non-ideal matrix is
minimally non-ideal). Entry points: Ahmad Abdi's LTCC "Blocking and antiblocking theory" notes and
his CO750 "Packing and Covering"; Conforti–Cornuéjols–Zambelli, *Integer Programming*, ch. on ideal
matrices; Cornuéjols, *Combinatorial Optimization: Packing and Covering* (CBMS 74).
**This is the cheapest theorem the stub is missing.**

**(b) Overcompression is the SYMMETRY GAP of symmetric integer programming, and it is studied.**
For a linear program with an invariant objective, averaging over the group puts an optimum in the
fixed space. For an *integer* program averaging leaves the lattice, so the fixed-space optimum can
be strictly worse — **that gap is Parts's overcompression, in the vocabulary of the field that owns
it.** The machinery: **core points** (Herr–Rehn–Schürmann, *Exploiting symmetry in integer convex
optimization using core points*, ORL 2013; Bödi–Herr–Joswig, *Algorithms for highly symmetric linear
and integer programs*, Math. Prog. 2013; arXiv:1202.0435, arXiv:1012.4941; and the 2020 follow-up
arXiv:2007.10863 on outer approximations of core points), **orbitopes** (Kaibel–Pfetsch, *Packing
and partitioning orbitopes*, arXiv:math/0603678 — literally the convex hulls of orbit-restricted 0/1
matrices, with complete facet descriptions in the cyclic case), **orbitopal fixing**,
**orbital branching** (Ostrowski–Linderoth–Rossi–Smriglio, Math. Prog. 2011), and Margot's survey
*Symmetry in integer linear programming* (in *50 Years of Integer Programming*).
**C2 should be stated in this vocabulary. The `Λ_H` slices are the fixed spaces; the question "when
does containment hold" is the question "when is the fixed-space relaxation tight", and it has
partial answers.**

**(c) The projection step has a theorem, and it predicts C1's failure.** Ginsburg–Spanier:
Presburger-definable = semilinear, and semilinear sets are closed under projection but polytopes'
integer points are not. Barvinok–Woods and the extension to unbounded polyhedra (arXiv:1612.08030,
Nguyen–Pak) give the structural result and complexity bounds for exactly *"the projection of the
integer points of a polyhedron"*. **This is the theorem that says `Prof` is semilinear and generally
not a polytope's integer points — i.e. it names the species a priori, which is what §3 measured.**

**(d) What I did NOT find, and it is the encouraging half.** No prior art for the specific claim
*"the minimum symmetric solution is not an overgraph of the minimum asymmetric one"* as a structural
statement about covering optima. The symmetry-breaking literature knows that *incomplete* symmetry
breaks can lose solutions (SAT modulo symmetries, lex-leader work, arXiv:2502.10056 which is
literally "breaking symmetries from a set-covering perspective") — but that is about the *breaking
constraints* being incomplete, not about the optima at two rungs being incomparable. **The
conjecture, restated in (b)'s vocabulary, has room.**

## §6 — One thing not asked: the exchange classes are a free completeness control, and they
replicate on Parts's own tables

**Replication, pre-registered before looking** (`PREREG.md` in this scratchpad). Claim: the varying
orbits of a `{M}` set partition into constant-sum **exchange classes**. Run on all four of Parts's
independently published `{M}` columns:

| his column | varying orbits | consistent exchange partitions | contains our two measured pairs? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `374_m6c` | 2 | **1 — forced** | **yes**, `{(2,0,6,4),(10,0,2,4)} = 12` |
| `374_m6ab` | 6 | 13 | **exactly 1 does**: `16 / 12 / 12` |
| `375_m6ab` | 4 | 4 | **exactly 1 does**: `12 / 12` |
| `376_m6a` | 10 | 27,202 | 12 do — consistent but uninformative |
| **ours, L 375** | 4 | *measured, not inferred* | `12 / 12` |
| **ours, S 136** | 3 | *measured* | one class of size 3, total 27 |

**Scored honestly against the pre-registration.** My first form of the claim was *pairs* and it
**fired its own falsifier F2** on `376_m6a` (widths `{3:3, 12:6, 6:1}` — odd multiplicities, no
perfect matching) and **F1** on our S side (three varying orbits). The general form —
*constant-sum groups*, of which pairs are the common case — passes everywhere, and the S side's
"failure" is a class of size 3, not a failure. **F3 also fired**: on `375_m6ab` all four widths are
4, so the matching is not forced by his ranges alone; it is forced by agreement with `374_m6ab`,
`374_m6c`, and our own measurement.

**One instrument incident, reported because it would have produced a false negative.** My first
partition search had a `break` after the first constant found per group and reported *"NO consistent
partition"* for `376_m6a`. Exhaustively, there are 27,202. The greedy search was measuring itself.
`G21`, on my own throwaway script, within an hour of writing an advisory about `G21`.

**The free control this buys:** the exchange classes determine `aff(Prof)`. **A level set whose
profiles do not span the predicted affine hull is incomplete** — a completeness check entirely
orthogonal to `exhaustive-level`'s own proof, costing nothing, and available on every future run.

**And a sharper reading of C3 than the stub's.** If the exchange classes are the structure, the
expansion step's missing measure is not *"optimise over an inequality description"* — it is **the
number of exchange classes and their totals**. That is a small integer vector per iteration, it
moves when the problem changes, and it is exactly the kind of thing a loop with no termination
measure can be monitored by.

### 1.2 The decision: which maps, in what order, and why

The request is worth its minutes: a model inhabits kernel theorems that have none, each instance is a local check on a few
hundred faces, and a pilot answered in milliseconds. Order, cheapest and most telling first:

1. **The square grid** (`torusGrid n m`, Lean's own object, where a model can be stated with the two existing models' machinery):
   every `3 ≤ n ≤ m ≤ 13`; a free-boundary window of the infinite grid (below) to learn whether the local rules are satisfiable at
   all; then every quotient of the grid by a sublattice of index up to 64, sheared ones included.
2. **Maps with other degrees, chosen by the count.** `room` leaves `V₄ ≥ 3V₆` and `V₃ ≤ 2V₄ + 5V₅ + 8V₆`; `absent_nonneg` leaves
   faces of length at most 5; `deg_le` leaves degrees 3 to 6. Among the Archimedean tilings and their duals these leave: the square
   grid; the snub square and elongated triangular tilings (all degree 5); their duals, the Cairo and prismatic pentagonal tilings
   (pentagons, degrees 3 and 4); and the deltoidal trihexagonal tiling (kites, `V₃ : V₄ : V₆ = 2 : 3 : 1`, so `V₄ = 3V₆` exactly).
   Each as a free-boundary window. Not run: general rotation systems (no enumerator at hand inside the hour), and the family of
   square grids with diagonals (`run.py patterns`, written, not run: the model arrived first).

**The two families the brief reads as excluded — both confirmed, each in a line.**
- *Every vertex of degree 6*: `room`'s first clause, `3 * numDeg 6 ≤ numDeg 4`, fails with `V₄ = 0 < V₆`. Confirmed, and the solver
  agrees on the triangular grid (control: `triangular 4x4` at six colours UNSAT, proof verified).
- *Every face a hexagon*: stronger than a count over the map — `absent_nonneg` gives `len x ≤ k − 1 = 5` for every face, so no
  `CountData 6` has any face of length 6 or more. Confirmed.
- One more by the same reading (mine, [hand], one mind): a square all of whose corners have degree 5 has 8 vertex-only neighbours
  against `tile_identity` with `absentNbrs_le`, which allow `A_x + B_x ≤ 4 + 3`; so the two degree-5 Archimedean tilings fall to
  one tile. The solver agrees (windows UNSAT, § 2.3).

**Free-boundary windows.** On the `N × N` torus quotient of a periodic tiling, keep the clauses centred at faces at least `m` cells
from the seam, `m` least such that no kept clause reads a face touching the seam (asserted in `window`); the other faces are free.
The formula is then a patch of the infinite tiling. *Its unsatisfiability is certified; that it excludes every finite quotient of
the tiling is my reading* ([hand], one mind: with `mu_zero` on the quotient, slots lift one-to-one, so `Adj`, `EdgeAdj` and
`vertNbrs` of a face are the images of those of its lift and every field pulls back to the cover) — a line for a deriver, § 4.

## 4. For a deriver — questions, not proofs

1. **Is `kill` empty?** Under `hK1` it concludes `U = 0`, `V₆ = 0`, `n = 2` at degree 4 and `0` elsewhere. On a square grid my
   reading ([hand], one mind) is that `U = F` always: `edge_rainbow` at the four edge-neighbours makes consecutive diagonal
   neighbours differ, so at most two (opposite) carry the absent colour, `A_x ≥ 2`, and `Σ A_x ≥ 2F = E ≥ A` forces `A = E`. If so,
   no square-grid `CountData 6` meets `hK1`, and every one has every edge tactic — as the model does. The family it covers: every
   `torusGrid n m`. The smallest map it does not: none found; the five other tilings searched have no `CountData 6` at all (§ 2.3,
   given the pull-back).
2. **The pull-back** of § 1.2 (a `CountData` on a quotient gives the local rules on the cover), which turns five window
   refutations into "no finite quotient of that tiling".
3. **Why 48?** Every model found has the period lattice `⟨(12,0),(2,−2)⟩` of index 24 up to symmetry, whose own quotient fails
   `mu_zero` (north-west and south-east neighbours coincide); the least sublattices avoiding that have index 48. Whether every
   square-grid model has this shape was not searched.
4. `chi2 = 0` does not ask connectedness (`Basic.lean`'s docstring says so); the model here is connected, so nothing rests on it.

theorem ratioAtMost_of_five_le_chromaticNumber [NumberField F] (P : StablePrime F)

### 1.2 What a certificate asserts, about which object, and what FS-66's checker computes

Read from `data/fs66/b1/SOUNDNESS.md` (whole), `tools/fs66_b1_cert_check.py` (whole), `data/fs66/b1/README.md` (§ The
problem read).

* **The object.** `V = (ℤ/p)²` for a prime `p ≡ 3 (mod 4)`, with points coded `a·p + b`. `S` is the unit circle
  `{a² + b² = 1}`, `|S| = p + 1`. The quadratic is `q(φ) = 2·Σ_x φ_x(1 − φ_x) − Σ_x Σ_{s∈S} φ_x φ_{x+s}`. The constant
  `2` is L1's eigenvalue bound, built into the checker.
* **The statement of a certificate.** For the rational `U = U_num/L`, every `φ ∈ [0,1]^V` with `q(φ) ≥ 0` has
  `Σφ ≤ U` (SOUNDNESS L4). It is a statement about one polynomial region, at level one.
* **How it is proved.** By one linear identity in the symmetrised moments `ȳ(m) = avg_{γ∈Γ} Π_{x∈m} φ(γx)` (`Γ` is
  generated by the translations and the `2(p+1)` maps `z ↦ sz`, `z ↦ s z̄` with `s ∈ S`):
  `U_num·y([]) − L·n·y([0]) = Σ_blocks T_block(y) + Σ_rows lam·box(A,B)(y)`. Each block is a positive-definite integer
  matrix `R` paired with integer weight vectors, times `1` or times `q`. A block contributes a symmetrised sum of squares,
  and a row a box product `Π_A φ·Π_B(1 − φ)` with multiplier `lam ≥ 0`. Evaluated at `ȳ`, every term on the right is
  `≥ 0`.
* **What the checker computes**, in exact integers and with no floating point:
  1. parsing and signs (`L > 0`, every `lam ≥ 0`);
  2. the maps, rebuilt from `p` and verified to be symmetries;
  3. positive definiteness of each `R` by Bareiss elimination (Sylvester's criterion);
  4. the identity, coefficient by coefficient over the canonical monomials (the least image under a translation then one
     of the maps);
  5. `m·U_num < p²·L`.
* **The two certificates.**

  | | `cert-p11.json` | `cert-p19.json` |
  |---|---|---|
  | size | 0.7 MB | 10.4 MB |
  | `U` | 28.2043200018630996… | 77.5117420886093822… |
  | blocks | 22: 11 of size 63 (times "1"), 11 of size 2 (times "q") | 38: 19 of size 183, 19 of size 2 |
  | weight vectors | 21 | 37 |
  | rows | 3,821 | 53,545 |
  | canonical monomials | 3,760 | 52,370 |
  | FS-66's checker | 6.5 s | 466 s |

  The checker times are its own records (`data/fs66/b1/records.jsonl`).

### 1.3 The inequality, in words and in the leaf's form

* **In words.** At `p = 11`, every independent set of every level `UD(ℤ/11^k, 2)` has at most `U·121^{k−1}` points.
  The level-one region bound `Σφ ≤ U` is the certificate; L1 (above level one, every eigenvalue on the non-fibre-constant
  part is `≥ −2p^{k−1}`) and L2 (the fibre shares of an independent set satisfy `q(φ) ≥ 0`) carry it to every level
  [hand]. The ratio is `U/p² = 0.2330935…` at 11 and `0.2147140…` at 19, both below `1/4`.
* **In the leaf's form.** `RatioAtMost P (U_q/q²)` at a stable prime of residue size `q ∈ {11, 19}`. Three links are
  needed:
  * (a) the certificate's region bound on `V = (ℤ/q)²` — the kernel part this job prices;
  * (b) the level-one target `levelTarget P 1` identified with `Cay(V, S)`. This uses `𝓞(F(i))/P ≅ 𝔽_{q²} = 𝔽_q[i]`
    with σ as `a + bi ↦ a − bi`, which needs `q ≡ 3 (mod 4)`;
  * (c) L1 and L2 at `levelTarget P m`, on the ring `𝓞(F(i))/P^m`. When the prime of `F` below `P` is unramified over
    `q`, this ring is `(ℤ/q^m)[i]`. When it is ramified it is a twisted cover. FS-66 §8's "free strengthening" (Lemma T,
    owner-checked) says L1 and L2 hold verbatim for every twisted cover, which is what M13's ramified case needs
    (map § 2.6 (d)).

  So the certificate is a leaf of (a) only; (b) and (c) are formalization leaves of their own (§ Stage 5).

## Stage 2 — SAVED: the smaller certificate, checked in the kernel

### 2.1 The form that worked

The checker is `data/fs131/kc13/lean/FibreCheck.lean` (core Lean, no Mathlib). The converter is
`tools/fs131_kc13_gen.py` (untrusted). It re-encodes the JSON certificate and runs a Python model of the Lean checker
first. The model's slot counts equal FS-66's canonical counts at p = 3, 7, 11, 19 (28, 495, 3,760, 52,370), and the
identity HOLDS in the model at all four.

* **Ops with witnesses.** Every term of the identity is an op: a monomial and an integer value. Its stream entry gives a
  slot `j` and a witness `(c, d, e, o)`. The kernel computes the image `g(x − o)` of each point, with
  `g = (z ↦ sz)` or `(z ↦ s z̄)` and `s = c + di`. It checks `c² + d² ≡ 1`, computes the image's key, and compares it with
  slot `j`'s key. A mismatch poisons the slot's leaf. The kernel never searches for a canonical form; FS-66's soundness
  page (§ 4) already says that any merge witnessed by a group element is sound and that an incomplete merge is safe.
* **The times-1 quadratic part, reduced by orbits.** Its terms are (rep pair `i ≤ j`, shift `t`). At p = 11 there are
  1,953 pairs × 121 shifts. The pairs fall into 218 orbits under `G` with the swap (984 orbits at p = 19).
  * Phase 1: for each pair, the stream gives its orbit and a transporter `g`, which the kernel checks
    (`g·A0 = a_i`, `g·A1 = a_j` as keys). It then adds `mult·Σ_b R_b[i][j]·K_b(g·t)` into the orbit's packed row, with
    `K_b` computed from the weights.
  * Phase 2: each orbit row's 121 slots are scattered with witnesses.

  This rests on `mono(g·i, g·j, t) = g·mono(i, j, g⁻¹t)`, on `mono(j, i, s) = τ_s mono(i, j, −s)`, on `R` symmetric and on
  `K` even.
* **The q-part.** `t` is grouped by the norm class of `{0, t}`, with a transporter per `t`. The `p + 2` forms `Q(m)` are
  expanded with witnesses.
* **Positive definiteness.** It is not done by Bareiss, which would need Sylvester's criterion formalized. Instead the
  converter derives an integer `M` (a rounded Cholesky factor of `R − (λ_min/4)·I`, scaled by `2^120`). The kernel checks
  that `E = s·R − M·Mᵀ` with `s = 2^240 + 1` is diagonally dominant. Each dot product is one Kronecker product of packed
  rows. The soundness is two lines: `s·xᵀRx = |Mᵀx|² + xᵀEx ≥ 0`.
* **Segments.** The identity is split by target leaves into `nseg` declarations. Each segment reads the whole op stream,
  fully processes only the ops whose slot lies in it, and requires its leaves balanced. This bounds a declaration's memory.
* **The data.** Lists of 8-kbit hex literals, fixed-width fields, signed values offset.

**The kernel's cost model, as measured on this checker** (each claim measured in `data/fs131/kc13/probes/`; for
`LEAN.md`, § Stage 6):

1. A direct `Nat.mul`/`Nat.add`/… call costs about 1 µs in the kernel. The same operation written as notation
   (`a * b`), through its instance chain, costs about 5 µs. Measured on 800k operations: 1.2 s against 4.7 s, and
   0.8 GB against 1.5 GB.
2. `Nat.rec` over an exact count costs about 5 µs per iteration. Structural recursion (`brecOn`) costs about 17 µs more.
3. **The kernel's caches hash a `Nat` literal by its low 64 bits.** The same computation over literals whose low 64 bits
   coincide went from 1.25 s to 33 s (63 reversals of 18-kbit numbers, `probes/Hd*.lean`, `Hz*.lean`). One PSD block
   went from 88 s to 1.3 s once the packed rows carried a random 60-bit tag in digit 0 and the scale and offsets were made
   odd (`2^sb + 1`, `2^(wm−1) + 1`). A power of two times anything has zero low bits.
4. Every intermediate a declaration creates is retained until the declaration ends: about 200 KB per big-literal step, and
   about 1–3 KB per small step. A packed accumulator of 128 KB updated 20,000 times took 4.4 GB. So memory, not time,
   sets the segment size.
5. A single hex literal of 1 MB takes minutes to parse (killed after two). A list of 1,000 literals of 8 kbits each
   elaborates in 3.8 s.

## Stage 3 — SAVED: the larger certificate, measured where it could be and extrapolated

### 3.1 What was run at p = 19

* **The converter.** The model's identity HOLDS, with 602,989 ops and 52,370 slots (FS-66's count, exactly), 16,653 pairs
  in 984 orbits, and a total magnitude of 129 bits. The data module (`--nseg 32`) is 12.4 MB of text and 25 MB of
  `.olean`, and elaborates in 27 s. It needs `set_option maxRecDepth 400000`: list literals of 2,513 chunks exceed the
  default depth. The compiled check of segment 0 passes, in 14.5 s.
* **The kernel cut.** `segCheck cert19 0 = true`, one segment of 32, was **killed at its 12 GiB cap** after about
  1.5 minutes; it already held 11 GB at one minute (`logs/k19s0.log`). The K table alone (40 maps × 19 blocks × 361
  shifts), in one declaration, **also exceeded the 12 GiB cap** (`logs/cut.log`). So the present form does not run at
  p = 19.
* **Where p = 11's time goes** (`logs/cut.log`; each figure includes about 3–4 s of a compiled `#eval` in the probe file):

  | part | time | memory |
  |---|---|---|
  | the K table | 14.7 s | 2.5 GB |
  | phase 1, including the K table | 25.1 s | 4.5 GB |
  | a segment that owns no slot, so every op is read and skipped | 47.7 s | 8.1 GB |
  | a full segment, about half the ops processed | 93.7 s | 12.0 GB |

  So reading or skipping an op costs about 0.4 ms and 67 KB, processing one about 1.7 ms and 145 KB more, and every
  segment repeats about 25 s of phase 1. **The present form pays that repetition and that skipping once per segment.**
  At p = 11 that is affordable. At p = 19 each segment would repeat a 15–17 times larger phase 1 and K table, and skip
  603k ops (about 4 min and 40 GB), before doing its own work.

### 3.2 The form that fits at p = 19, and its price (extrapolated, not run)

* **F2.** Nothing is repeated, and no declaration reads another's ops.
  1. **The K table as a literal**, checked map by map in about 8 declarations of 5 maps each. The p = 11 figure scales by
     `(19·361)/(11·121) = 5.2` per map, which gives about 0.5 GB and 3 s per map.
  2. **Phase 1 once per orbit range**, in 4 declarations. Each reads every pair's 19 `R` fields and accumulates its
     quarter of the orbits: about 316k pair-blocks at 0.47 ms and 93 KB each (p = 11's rate), so about 150 s and
     29 GB in all, under 10 GB each. The orbit rows are passed on as a literal of 11.6 MB.
  3. **The scatter by target segment, with per-segment op lists.** Each segment's stream names only its own ops, by
     source index: orbit and shift, Q-monomial with `x` and `s`, row and mask. The orbit rows and the rows are reached
     through balanced-tree literals. A final declaration checks that the segments' bitmasks over the 603k sources are
     disjoint and cover them (32 GMP operations). That is about 603k × 2 ms = 20 min and 603k × 170 KB = 100 GB, in 12
     segments of about 8 GB each.
  4. **PSD**, one 183 × 183 block per declaration: 16.8k entries against p = 11's 2k, with products three times larger,
     gives an estimated 15–25 s each and 4–8 minutes in all.
* **Price of F2 at p = 19: about 30 minutes of kernel CPU in about 40 declarations of at most 10 GB each.** That is about
  15 minutes of wall time two at a time on the laptop. About 300 more checker lines: the per-segment op lists, the tree
  literals and the bitmask cover.
* **What reduces it further:**
  * fused point arithmetic in the witness check (the four points' translate, rotate and reduce done once, and the codes'
    div/mod hoisted): about 280 kernel steps per op become about 150, a factor of about 2;
  * the slots of an orbit row that its stabiliser identifies (984 orbits from 16,653 pairs is an average stabiliser of
    about 2.4 out of 40): up to 2× on phase 2's 355k ops;
  * **a smaller relaxation**, which is a search request (§ Stage 6). At 11 the margin is 30.25 − 28.20 = 2.05, and at 19
    it is 90.25 − 77.51 = 12.7, so a lower level of moment relaxation or fewer moment monomials could already clear 1/4 at
    19. FS-66 never probed below the second level there.
* The translation-Fourier block structure is already used: one block per norm class, weights `w_x = f(⟨u,x⟩)`, `κ` from
  `f`'s `p`-point autocorrelation. The orbit reduction of § 2.1 is the rest of that symmetry.

## Stage 5 — SAVED: the per-prime leaf, planned

### 5.1 The statement, read against the map planner's route

```lean
theorem ratioAtMost_of_five_le_chromaticNumber [NumberField F] (P : StablePrime F)
    (h : 5 ≤ (levelTarget P 1).chromaticNumber) : ∃ c : ℝ, c < 1 / 4 ∧ RatioAtMost P c
```

Read against map § 2.6 and against `TCA`. The hypothesis is per prime: what `tca_of_five_le_localBound` hands it is
`localBound ≤ χ(levelTarget P 1)` (`iInf_le`). The conclusion is `TCA`'s clause at `P`, symbol for symbol (`tca_iff`,
`Iff.rfl`). Level `m = 1` is inside `RatioAtMost`, so the certificate's level-one bound is used directly. **No defect
found.**

The case split the hypothesis leaves, by residue size, is the map's (a)–(d):
* a ramified stable prime contradicts `h`: dyadic by `colorable_two_levelTarget_one_of_isRamified`, odd by the cycle
  `Cay(𝔽_q, {±1})`;
* an inert odd prime has `q ≡ 3 (mod 4)`, with `q ∉ {3, 7}`;
* an inert dyadic prime has `Q ≥ 64`.

The certificates serve `q ∈ {11, 19}`; both are primes, so `V = (ℤ/q)²` is the residue plane itself.

**What is hard**: K2i, relating a fast checker to the mathematics. The mitigation is to write `ops` first, with a
reference checker on it (lists, no packing, no trees), and prove that reference sound: that is the math, K2e–h and K2i
on it. Then prove the fast checker's acceptance implies the reference's: K2a–c, local lemmas each. If a computation
lemma resists, the fast checker can be simplified there; the kernel has room at p = 11.

**Price of the branch**: K2 2,000–4,000 + K3 300–500 + K4 1,000–1,800 (shared) + K5 and glue 200–400, so about
**3,500–6,700 lines**. Above the map's whole-M13 estimate (2,500–5,000) by about K2, which the map could not price
without a checker design: it is new, and no outside library holds one. Kernel: p = 11 about 3 minutes; p = 19 per

### 5.3 The exact certificates at 23 and 31 (brief item 4)

* **What is needed.** At level one, the eigenvalues of `Cay(𝔽_q², S)` are
  `λ(ν) = Σ_{s∈S} cos(2π⟨u_ν, s⟩/q) = Σ_j c_{ν,j} cos(2πj/q)`, where the `c_{ν,j} = #{s ∈ S : ⟨u_ν, s⟩ = j}` are
  integers the kernel counts in `q²` steps. Needed: `λ(ν) > −(q+1)/3` for every non-zero norm class `ν`. That gives a
  normalised least eigenvalue above `−1/3`, the Hoffman ratio below `1/4`, and with Lemma P every level.
* **The margins.** 1.6·10⁻³ (normalised) at 23, which is 0.038 absolute over 24 terms. 4.9·10⁻⁶ at 31, which is
  1.6·10⁻⁴ absolute over 32 terms, so each cosine is needed to about 5·10⁻⁶.
* **The certificate.** Rational bounds on `cos(2πj/q)` for `j ≤ q/2`, from an alternating Taylor bound with π bracketed
  by `Real.pi_gt_d20` and `Real.pi_lt_d20` (both at the pin). The arithmetic is checked by `norm_num`. `Real.cos_bound`
  covers only `|x| ≤ 1`, so either a general alternating-Taylor lemma is proved once (about 100 lines), or angles are
  reduced and a Chebyshev recurrence is run from `cos(2π/q)`.
* **The bridge.** To the library's character sums (`RatioBound.card_mul_le_of_indep` reads `Re Σ_s ψ(s) ≥ −m`), with
  characters of `ZMod q × ZMod q` from `ZMod.stdAddChar` and `Re ψ = cos`.
* **Price.** About 700–1,200 lines, kernel seconds.
* **Does the same checker serve?** Not for 31: FibreCert bounds the fibre-density region, not an eigenvalue, and FS-66
  holds no certificate at 31 (making one is a search request, § Stage 6). At 23 it would serve: `cert-p23.json`, 26 MB,
  `U/529 = 0.1852`. But that is about 2.5 times p = 19's size, hours of kernel time by § Stage 3's extrapolation, so the
  exact spectrum is far cheaper there.
* **At 27** (FS100-G27): λ_min = −8 is an integer, because characteristic-3 sums are integers when real. It is a
  Floor128-style register certificate over `𝔽₃⁶`: 400–600 lines, kernel seconds.

### 6.2 `LEAN.md` § The kernel's cost model (after the bullet "A kernel leaf priced from a plan's table is priced high")

- **The kernel's caches hash a `Nat` literal by its low 64 bits** (`Literal.hash (.natVal v) = hash v` and
  `hash n = UInt64.ofNat n`: `Lean/Expr.lean:25` and `Init/Data/Hashable.lean:15` at the pin; the kernel's use of the
  stored hash is inferred from the measurement). Many literals whose low 64 bits agree collide: a power of two times
  anything, an offset `2^k`, a packed vector whose low slot is zero. 63 reversals of 18-kbit numbers took 1.25 s with
  distinct low bits and 33 s with the low 64 bits zeroed. One PSD block took 88 s, and 1.3 s once its packed rows carried
  a random 60-bit tag in digit 0 and its scale and offsets were made odd (`data/fs131/kc13/probes/Hd*.lean`, `Hz*.lean`).
  Tag packed data in its low digit; make scales and offsets odd. (FS131-KC13)
- **Every intermediate a declaration creates stays alive until the declaration ends.** A step that builds a big literal
  keeps about its size (a 128 KB packed accumulator updated 20,000 times: 4.4 GB); a small step keeps 1–3 KB. Memory,
  not time, sets a long check's segment size, and a segment that reads ops it then skips still pays about 67 KB each
  (FS131-KC13).
- **A direct `Nat.mul`, `Nat.add`, … call costs about 1 µs in the kernel; the same operation in notation, through its
  instance chain, about 5 µs** (800k operations: 1.2 s against 4.7 s). `Nat.rec` over an exact count is about 5 µs an
  iteration, and structural recursion adds about 17 µs (`brecOn`). This refines "about 33 µs per iteration of a trivial
  recursive function", which was structural recursion with notation. `List.rec`, and the recursor of a user inductive,
  do not compile (no `#eval`); `Nat.rec` and a non-recursive `match` do (FS131-KC13).
- **One hex literal of 1 MB takes minutes to parse; a list of 1,000 literals of 8 kbits each elaborates in 3.8 s.** A
  list literal of about 2,500 elements needs `set_option maxRecDepth` (FS131-KC13).

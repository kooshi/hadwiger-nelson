## 0. For a reader with no memory of the project

This repository attacks the **Hadwiger–Nelson problem** — how many colours the plane needs so that
no two points exactly one unit apart share a colour (the answer is 5, 6 or 7; narrowing it is the
mission). Everything is machine-checked by Lean's kernel, so a claim is proved or it is not.

`FS-25` is a consolidation milestone, not a step of the hunt. Its subject is one recurring pattern
in the project's own record:

> the record established something **per instance** that holds **in general**, and stopped at the
> instance because the instance was what the instrument produced.

None of the three items below bears on proving the plane needs six colours, and none of them is
sold as if it did. What they buy is a record that says the true thing rather than a weaker true
thing, and — in two of the three cases — a *reason* where the record carried a regularity.

**Three pieces of vocabulary used throughout.** A **forced-equal pair** (the project's older word:
"forced pair") is two vertices that receive the same colour in *every* proper `k`-colouring of a
graph. A **virtual edge** (the literature prefers *non-mono pair*; see `[Parts2020a]` §2) is two
non-adjacent vertices that receive *different* colours in every proper `k`-colouring. A vertex is
**critical** at `k` colours when deleting it (and its edges) leaves a graph that *is* `k`-colourable.

## 1. E4 — de Grey's 1581-vertex graph forces no pair of vertices to share a colour at five colours, and the reason is one seam

### 1.1 What the record said, and what it cost

`CLAUDE.md` § Grokkings `G31` reports, of de Grey's 1581-vertex 5-chromatic unit-distance graph:

> *"nothing at arity 2 (complete, 1,248,990 pairs)"*

— a completed exhaustive sweep at five colours, run as part of milestone M48. Half of that sweep
(the forced-equal half) was measuring a theorem this repository has held since M54.

### 1.2 The theorem, and its two inputs, each verified rather than inherited

**Input 1, kernel-checked.** `CriticalSet.encard_criticalSet_le_two_of_forces`
(`Proofs/HadwigerNelson/CriticalSet.lean:274`). Read from the source, not from the stub:

```lean
theorem encard_criticalSet_le_two_of_forces {ι : Type*} {G : SimpleGraph ι} {q : ι → ℂ}
    (hinj : Function.Injective q) (hq : ∀ i j, G.Adj i j → dist (q i) (q j) = 1) {u v : ι}
    (hne : q u ≠ q v) (h : Forces G (k + 1) u v) : (CriticalSet G k).encard ≤ 2
```

In words: *a graph drawn injectively in the plane with every edge exactly one unit long, which
forces some pair of vertices to share a colour at `k+1` colours, has at most **two** critical
vertices at `k` colours.* The mechanism is geometric and is proved one step earlier
(`eq_gluePoint_or_reflAxis_of_mem_criticalSet_of_forces`): a critical vertex must be a common unit
neighbour of both endpoints of the forced pair, and two distinct points of the plane have at most
two common unit neighbours — the glue point of their unit circles, and its mirror. **[kernel]**

**Input 2, and here the stub's version does not survive contact.** The stub proposed to take the
three critical vertices from `G10`'s *"two edges PROVEN critical end-to-end"*. That claim is real —
`CLAUDE.md` `G10`, and `plans/M30-degrey-anatomy.md:379` in its fuller form, *"deleting ONE random
edge → 4-colourable in 4 of 5 probes (8–33 s; one unknown at 300 s)"* — but **its witnesses are not
on disk**: those probes were run ad hoc, their artifacts lived under `build/` (which is not
tracked), and the record does not name which edges they were. A corollary resting on it would
inherit the status *"measured, witness lost"*, which is below this project's own standard.

**So the input was replaced by a better one, and the better one needs no solver at all.** De Grey's
graph is a **1-sum**. Re-derived here from `tools/degrey.py` (the exact-arithmetic generator, run
fresh):

```
n 1581   |E| 7877   halves [3938, 3938, 1]   shared [14]
|A| = 791   |B| = 791   A ∩ B = {14}   cross edge = (11, 802),  11 ∈ A∖B,  802 ∈ B∖A
edges outside the decomposition: 0
```

That is: the vertex set is two 791-vertex halves meeting in the single vertex `14`, and **every one
of the 7,877 edges lies inside `A`, inside `B`, or is the single cross edge `(11, 802)`** — checked
exhaustively, zero exceptions. (This is de Grey's own construction seen in its output; it is not a
discovery about the graph, it is the graph's blueprint. The cross edge is the same one M56's
additive spindle theorem singles out: `T27` records `(11, 802)` as the 1581's *unique* unit vector
in `4Λ`.)

From that shape, three critical vertices fall out with no search:

* **`11` and `802`.** Delete either and no edge crosses between the halves at all. What remains is
  `G[A]` and `G[B]` sharing only the vertex `14` — a 1-sum — so any 4-colouring of each half can be
  glued by permuting one palette to agree at `14`.
* **`14`.** Delete it and the halves share no vertex; they are joined only by the cross edge
  `(11, 802)`, so glue by permuting one palette to *disagree* there.

Both moves need `k ≥ 2` colours and the 4-colourability of each half, and nothing else.

**The one fact those moves consume — that each 791-vertex half is 4-colourable — was verified here,
end to end** (`data/e4-critical-vertices.json`; the script is in the session scratchpad and its
verification path shares no code with its search path):

| what | result |
|---|---|
| `G[A]` at four colours | **sat**, colouring re-verified on all 3,938 edges, 0 violations |
| `G[B]` at four colours | **sat**, colouring re-verified on all 3,938 edges, 0 violations |
| `G − 11` (7,870 edges), colouring **built by gluing** | 0 violations |
| `G − 802` (7,870 edges), built by gluing | 0 violations |
| `G − 14` (7,865 edges), built by gluing | 0 violations |
| the same three, asked of the solver independently | **sat** each, all re-verified, 0 violations |

Negative controls, all green: `G[A]` and `G[B]` each **refuse three colours** (so the encoding
genuinely constrains, and each half is exactly 4-chromatic); a planted corruption of one committed
colouring is **caught** by the verifier. One control has an empty population and is reported as
such rather than as passed: the 1581 has **minimum degree 4**, so there is no vertex of degree `≤ 3`
to serve as a provably *non*-critical red.

### 1.3 The conclusion, with its enumeration in the same sentence

> **De Grey's 1581-vertex graph has at least three critical vertices at four colours (`11`, `802`,
> `14`), so by `encard_criticalSet_le_two_of_forces` — which caps that count at two — it forces no
> pair of its 1,248,990 vertex pairs to share a colour at five colours. This is the whole
> forced-equal half of M48's arity-2 sweep, and it needed no sweep.** **[kernel theorem; the three
> critical vertices measured, with their witnesses now held in `data/e4-critical-vertices.json`]**

**Non-vacuity, since this project has been bitten by exactly that.** `Forces G 5 u v` is vacuously
true on a graph with no proper 5-colouring, so the statement would be empty if the 1581 were not
5-colourable. It is: ledger row 8 holds a kernel-certified *balanced* proper 5-colouring
(`Proofs/HadwigerNelson/Instances/DeGreyBalanced.lean`). The theorem therefore refutes something
that could have been true.

### 1.4 What does **not** promote — and this is half the item

* **The virtual-edge half does not follow.** Its localization is
  `criticalSet_subset_union_neighborSet_of_separated` (`CriticalSet.lean:200`), whose bound is
  `|N(p) ∪ N(q)|`, at most 120 on this graph (maximum degree 60). Refuting a virtual edge that way
  needs 121 critical vertices, i.e. either ~121 four-colour solver asks or the exhaustive
  edge-criticality sweep `G10` prices at about a day of processor time. **The measured zero at
  arity 2 keeps its full value on the virtual-edge side.**
* **Arity 3 and arity 4 are untouched.** M48's 3,286,925,350-question triple sweep and its
  25,782,585-question neighbourhood 4-sweep are not implied by anything here. Only the pair layer's
  forced-equal half collapses.
* This says nothing about any other graph, and nothing about the hunt for six colours.

### 1.5 The structure underneath — and this repository proved it in M06, which is the point

The three critical vertices are not scattered: they are exactly the **seam** of de Grey's 1-sum —
the shared vertex and the two endpoints of the single cross edge. Every *other* vertex fails the
gluing argument for a precise reason: deleting it leaves both the shared vertex `14` **and** the
cross edge `(11, 802)` in place, and one palette permutation cannot satisfy both constraints at
once. So the 1581's 5-chromaticity is carried by the interaction of those two constraints over two
4-chromatic halves — *agree at `14`, differ across `(11, 802)`*.

**Two solver asks run here confirm the "agree at 14" half directly**: in `G[A]`, forcing
`col(14) ≠ col(11)` is **unsat** while forcing them equal is **sat**, so vertices `14` and `11` are
forced to share a colour in every proper 4-colouring of half A — and the same for `14` and `802` in
half B. With `(11, 802)` an edge, that is `χ ≥ 5` in three lines.

**And that is milestone M06's own argument, already kernel-checked, which I re-derived without
noticing.** `plans/M06-chi-ge-five.md:102`: *"shared vertex 14 — and 11–802 is an edge, so the two
endpoints cannot agree. **That is the whole proof of non-4-colourability**, and it is de Grey's own
argument rather than a brute-force search."* M09 then put it in the kernel as a gadget composition
with ports `![14, 11, 802]` (`Instances/DeGrey/Forcing.lean:35–72`), where `deGreyForced` is exactly
the statement that half A admits no 4-colouring separating `14` from `11`. **Recording this as a
find would have been precisely the failure mode `FS-25` exists to catch — an instance written down
where the theorem was already held — so it is recorded here as a re-derivation, not a discovery.**

What the M06/M09 chain does **not** contain, and what §1.2 supplies, is the *other* direction: that
each half **is** 4-colourable. The kernel holds refutations of half A under a pin (that is what
`halfATable` is — an LRAT clause table, not a colouring); it holds no positive 4-colouring of either
half. The three witnesses in `data/e4-critical-vertices.json` are that missing half, and they are
what turns M06's structure into three critical vertices.

**One consequence for `G10`, stated carefully.** `G10` reads *"the forcing is distributed, not
organ-like"* from *"4 of 5 random single-edge deletions leave the graph 4-colourable"*. That reading
is **not** challenged here — its probes were at random edges, and the seam is 1 edge in 7,877, so
they almost certainly never hit it. But the three *cheapest* instances of edge- and
vertex-criticality in this graph are now known to be consequences of the construction's shape rather
than evidence about the distribution of forcing, and a future re-run of that probe should exclude
the seam and say so.

### 1.6 Whether to take it to Lean

**Not now, and the reason is scope, not difficulty.** The statement *"the 1581 forces no pair equal
at five colours"* would need the graph in the kernel (it is, as a certificate) plus the three
4-colourings as kernel data plus `encard_criticalSet_le_two_of_forces` applied — perhaps a day, and
it certifies a *negative* about one object. The transferable half is already kernel: the
localization theorem. The 1-sum lemma behind the gluing is the piece worth having in general, and it
is stated in §4 below as a candidate.

## 2. E3 — the periodic quotient of a finite Euclidean plane is one explicit circulant, and the record never says which

### 2.1 What the record said

`plans/M61-local-global-tightness.md` §S3 proves that every *periodic* proper colouring of the
finite Euclidean plane `UD(𝔽_p, 2)` — the graph on `𝔽_p × 𝔽_p` joining points whose difference
`(a, b)` satisfies `a² + b² = 1`, for `p ≡ 3 (mod 4)` — is a colouring of a **circulant graph on `p`
vertices**, `C_p`. It then computes `χ(C_p) = 3, 4, 6, 5, 8, 8, 8, …` at `p = 3, 7, 11, 19, 23, 31,
43, …`, **one prime at a time with a SAT solver**, *"every `lo` the solver's"*, and registers the
prediction `χ(C_p) ≥ 6` for every `p ≥ 23` as *"measured to 71"*.

The circulant is never identified. The record even states, without explaining it, that `0 ∉ π(S)`.

### 2.2 The identification

Identify `𝔽_p² ≅ 𝔽_{p²} = 𝔽_p(i)` — legitimate because `p ≡ 3 (mod 4)` makes `−1` a non-square, so
`X² + 1` is irreducible. Then `a² + b² = N(a + bi)` for the field norm `N(z) = z·z̄ = z^{p+1}`, so:

* **the unit circle is the norm-one subgroup `μ_{p+1}`**, cyclic of order `p + 1`;
* every surjective `𝔽_p`-linear map `𝔽_{p²} → 𝔽_p` is `z ↦ Tr(γz)` for a unique `γ ≠ 0`, where
  `Tr(w) = w + w^p`;
* its kernel is the line `ℓ_γ = γ^{-1}·i𝔽_p`, whose nonzero elements have norm `t²/N(γ)`.

Hence **`ℓ_γ` misses the unit circle exactly when `N(γ)` is a non-square** — which is where the
record's "null line, its direction of non-square norm" comes from, and which counts the lines:
`γ` up to `𝔽_p^*`-scaling with `N(γ)` in the non-square class gives exactly `(p+1)/2` of them.

Now project. Put `ν = N(γ)`, a non-square, and let `w = γz` as `z` runs over the circle; then `w`
runs over the **norm-`ν` fibre** `{w ∈ 𝔽_{p²} : N(w) = ν}`, and the connection set is its trace set:

> **`D = Tr{ w : N(w) = ν } = { t ∈ 𝔽_p : X² − tX + ν is irreducible over 𝔽_p } = { t : t² − 4ν is a non-square }`.**

The middle equality is the minimal polynomial (`w ∈ 𝔽_p` would force `ν = w²`, a square, so `w`
always has degree 2 and `X² − tX + ν` is its minimal polynomial); the last is the discriminant. Four
consequences drop out immediately, three of which the record states and one of which it states
without explanation:

* **`|D| = (p+1)/2`** — the `p+1` elements of the norm-`ν` fibre are none of them in `𝔽_p`, so they
  pair off into conjugate pairs with equal trace.
* **`D = −D`** — the condition depends on `t²`.
* **`0 ∉ D`**, *because* `−4ν` is a **square**: `4` is a square, `ν` is a non-square, and `−1` is a
  non-square since `p ≡ 3 (mod 4)`, so the product is a square. **This is the record's unexplained
  fact, and it is the same fact as "the line misses the circle" read at `t = 0`.**
* **`D` does not depend on the choice of `ν` up to a dilation**: two non-squares differ by a square
  `c²`, and `D_{c²ν} = c·D_ν`, which is an isomorphism of the circulant. So `C_p` really is one
  graph per prime, as the record asserts.

The complement is the mirror statement and is just as clean:
**`D^c = { x + ν/x : x ∈ 𝔽_p^* }`**, the trace set of the *split* torus at determinant `ν`, of size
`(p−1)/2`. So an **independent set of `C_p` is a subset of `ℤ/p` all of whose differences have the
form `x + ν/x`** — which is the combinatorial description of `α(C_p)` the record lacks, and `α(C_p)`
is what drives M61's `α(UD(𝔽_p,2)) ≥ p·α(C_p)`.

**A phrasing correction to the stub.** The stub writes *"`D` is the set of traces of the non-split
torus of `𝔽_{p²}`"*. The norm-`ν` fibre is a **coset** of `μ_{p+1}`, not a torus; the accurate
sentence is *"the traces of the determinant-`ν` elements of the non-split torus of `GL₂(𝔽_p)`"*, and
`ν` must be a non-square or the statement is false (at `ν` a square the line meets the circle and no
periodic colouring exists at all).

### 2.3 Verified, and against what

The closed form was checked **against the direct construction** — build the actual unit circle in
`𝔽_p²`, find every line that misses it, project along each, and compare — at **eight primes**, and
`α` and `χ` against M61's own solver table at the seven where that table is exact:

| `p` | `ν` | `\|D\|` | `(p+1)/2` | circle-missing lines | closed form = direct? | `0 ∉ D` | `D = −D` | `α` here | `χ` here | M61's `(χ, α)` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | 1 | 3 | (3, 1) ✓ |
| 7 | 3 | 4 | 4 | 4 | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | 2 | 4 | (4, 2) ✓ |
| 11 | 2 | 6 | 6 | 6 | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | 2 | 6 | (6, 2) ✓ |
| 19 | 2 | 10 | 10 | 10 | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | 4 | 5 | (5, 4) ✓ |
| 23 | 5 | 12 | 12 | 12 | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | 3 | 8 | (8, 3) ✓ |

*"closed form = direct" means: for **every** circle-missing line, the connection set obtained by
actually projecting the circle along it is a dilation `c·D` of the closed form — the predicted
equivalence, verified line by line, not sampled.* **[measured, exact arithmetic; the χ column is
cadical at a 300 s cap, and the `p = 47` row is honestly a cap, not a verdict]**

Two of these are worth naming because they were the stub's own spot-checks and both survive:
`p = 7` gives `D = {±1, ±2}`, the square of the 7-cycle, `α = 2`, `χ = 4`; `p = 11` gives
`D = {±2, ±4, ±5}`, which is `{±1, ±2, ±3}` after multiplying by `6 = 2⁻¹`, i.e. the **cube of the
11-cycle**, whose chromatic number is the classical cycle-power value
`⌈11/⌊11/4⌋⌉ = ⌈11/2⌉ = 6` — matching the solver and giving the record's most load-bearing periodic
fact (*"no periodic 5-colouring at 11"*) a closed-form proof. **The cycle-power coincidence stops
there**: at `p = 19`, `D = {±1, ±2, ±4, ±7, ±8}` is not a dilation of any `{±1, …, ±r}` (checked
over all 18 dilations), so the family is not a family of cycle powers.

### 2.4 The spectral payoff, stated exactly

Characters of `ℤ/p` pull back to characters of `𝔽_p²` trivial on `ℓ_γ`, and `π` restricted to the
circle is exactly 2-to-1 onto `D` (a fibre of `Tr` over `t` meets the circle in the two roots of
`X² − tX + ν`, and never in one, since `t² = 4ν` is impossible). Therefore, for every `a ∈ 𝔽_p`,

> **`λ_{C_p}(a) = ½ · λ_{UD(𝔽_p,2)}(a·γ)`.**

The circulant's spectrum is **literally half the plane's own**, restricted to the frequencies of
non-square norm class. (Sanity: at `a = 0` this reads `(p+1)/2 = ½(p+1)`.) So the eigenvalues of
`C_p` are the same character sums over the norm-one torus of `𝔽_{p²}` that M58 identified as
Kloosterman sums (ledger row 53, `λ(c) = −K(1, c/4)`), and they inherit the same Weil bound
`|λ| ≤ 2√p`, halved to `√p`.

Hoffman then gives, with no computation,

> **`χ(C_p) ≥ 1 + (p+1)/(2√p)`** — the *same* bound M58 proves for the plane itself.

That exceeds 5 from **`p ≥ 67`** onward, with no computation at all. And the **exact** spectrum
(computed here; `λ_min` is a sum of at most 66 cosines, so the arithmetic is not delicate) pushes the
crossing one prime lower:

| `p` | 23 | 31 | 43 | 47 | **59** | 67 | 71 | 79 | 83 | 103 | 131 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| exact Hoffman on `C_p` | 4.01 | 4.34 | 4.68 | 4.74 | **5.57** | 5.52 | 6.08 | 5.68 | 5.74 | 6.19 | 7.25 |
| so `χ(C_p) ≥` | 5 | 5 | 5 | 5 | **6** | 6 | 7 | 6 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
| Weil bound `1 + (p+1)/(2√p)` | 3.50 | 3.87 | 4.36 | 4.50 | 4.91 | 5.15 | 5.27 | 5.50 | 5.61 | 6.12 | 6.77 |

> **So the registered prediction P7 — `χ(C_p) ≥ 6` for every `p ≥ 23` — stops being an open-ended
> table and becomes a theorem plus four small finite checks.** Every `p ≥ 67` is proved outright by
> Weil; `p = 59` is proved by its exact spectrum; and only `p = 23, 31, 43, 47` remain, where M61's
> table already reads 8, 8, 8 and ≥ 9. Those four are SAT instances on at most `47 × 8 = 376`
> variables — genuinely kernel-promotable by `decide` or a short LRAT chain, unlike almost anything
> else in this repository.

**What does not follow, said plainly.** Weil-strength bounds do *not* give `χ(C_p) ≥ 6` in the range
`23 ≤ p ≤ 47`: the same character-sum argument gives only `α(C_p) < 2√p`, hence `χ(C_p) > √p/2`,
which is 2.4 at `p = 23`. Those four primes are genuinely a computation, and the honest claim is
"a proven tail plus a four-row table", not "a bound to prove". The stub rated this payoff *medium*
confidence and the rating was right — the outcome is better than *medium* suggested and short of
what *"a bound to prove"* promised.

## 3. E5 — the 16-pair residue on `K`-fabric is not a puzzle: it is the exponent of a group of order four

### 3.1 What the record said

`plans/STANDING-QUESTIONS.md` `T26` records, of a 1,953-point unit-distance object drawn in
`K = ℚ(√3, √11)` at four colours: the *additive localization* (colour by a group homomorphism out of
the difference lattice) forces 2,513 pairs; sharpening it by Kempe swaps forces 187; the solver
forces 171; and

> *"the 16 depth-3 pairs (13 at 64/9, 3 at 64/3) Kempe-forced under every additive colouring yet
> separated by the solver are the non-additive residue"*

with the open question *"are those 16 separated by two swaps?"*.

### 3.2 Every number re-read from the artifact rather than from the prose

`data/m56/kempe-object-pth1-vdel28-it2v2-depth1-k4.json`, object `n = 1953`, 13,796 edges,
fingerprint `9d4787d572a65939`, drawn in the 4-dimensional `K`-frame
`((a√3 + b√11)/12, (c + d√33)/12)`:

| `d²` | `v₂(d²)` | depth | pairs | Kempe-forced | solver-forced | residue |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `4/3` | 2 | 1 | 6,669 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| `4` | 2 | 1 | 5,470 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| `12` | 2 | 1 | 767 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| `16/3` | 4 | 2 | 2,145 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| `16` | 4 | 2 | 179 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| **`64/9`** | 6 | **3** | 178 | 176 | **163** | **13** |
| **`64/3`** | 6 | **3** | 11 | 11 | **8** | **3** |

`F_add` = the depth-`≥ 2` classes = 2,513 ✓ · `F_K` = 187 ✓ · `F_true` = 171 ✓ · residue = 16 ✓.
The additive family is 8 colourings: **6 from homomorphisms onto `ℤ/4`, 2 from `ℤ/2 × ℤ/2`** ✓.

**So the stub's numerical claim is confirmed: `d² = 64/9` is `d = 8/3` and `d² = 64/3` is
`d = 8/√3`.** **[measured, exact, from the committed artifact]**

### 3.3 The connection the record never made in one sentence

Those two distances are not incidental to this object. Across the record:

* `[Parts2020a]` Table 1, as recorded in ledger row 59: the two computer-found **minimal gadgets
  forcing a monochromatic pair at four colours** sit at exactly `8/3` (367 vertices) and `8/√3`
  (421 vertices) — and at no other distance in his table.
* Ledger row 47, M53's rigidity dial on **this very object**: *"163/178 at 8/3, 8/11 at 8/√3, 0 at
  valuations 2 and 4"* — which the artifact reproduces exactly (`163` of `178`, `8` of `11`).
* Ledger row 47 again, T20: the forced `8/3` pairs are what M53 spindled to build a 5-chromatic
  graph in `ℚ(√3, √11, √247)`.
* `[Polymath16wiki]` (4 June 2018): *"in the ring `R̄₂` it is known that the element `8/3` has the
  same color as zero"*.

**A correction to the stub, found by checking.** The stub says *"the same two distances are forced
on three other objects and not on `U`"*. The second half is wrong: on `U` itself **171 of the 189
pairs at those two distances are forced**, and every pair at every *other* distance is separated.
The residue is not a set of anomalous pairs at anomalous distances — it is a set of pairs at the
object's **own two forcing distances** that this particular 1,953-point object does not have enough
surrounding material to force. The stub's headline (*"`U` is under-sized"*) is right; its supporting
sentence needed replacing. Corrected form:

> **All 189 pairs of `U` at `d = 8/3` and `d = 8/√3` are forced by the algebra; 171 of them are
> forced by the object; the 18 that are not are the ones `U` is too small for — and the literature's
> minimal gadgets for exactly these two distances are 367 and 421 vertices.**

### 3.4 The theorem underneath, which is the real prize

The record now holds two independent **necessary** conditions on a four-colour monochromatic pair
over `K`, and they differ by exactly one level of 2-adic depth:

| condition | source | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| the **valuation law** | ledger row 59, `ValuationLaw.lean` | forced ⟹ **depth ≥ 1** **[kernel]** |
| the **additive localization** | `T26` on `U` | forced ⟹ **depth ≥ 2** **[measured on `U`]** |

They reconcile in one line, and the line is about **group exponents**.

An additive colouring is a homomorphism `φ : Λ → G` from the object's difference lattice into a
group `G` with `|G| = k`, non-vanishing on the edge vectors. If `G` has exponent `e` then
`eΛ ⊆ ker φ` — this is M56's L1, and it is **already in the kernel**:
`Additive.addMonoidHom_apply_eq_of_sub_eq_nsmul` (`Proofs/HadwigerNelson/Additive.lean:74`), whose
own docstring even says *"The instrument's `forced-additively` verdict at 2-adic depth `≥ 2` is this
lemma with `e = 4`"*. What the record never draws from it is that **it is a floor**:

> **At four colours the target group is `ℤ/4` (exponent 4) or `ℤ/2 × ℤ/2` (exponent 2), so every
> additive four-colouring — of any object, over any field — identifies every pair whose difference
> lies in `4Λ`. Hence `F_add ⊇ {depth ≥ 2}` always, and *no* additive four-colouring can ever
> separate a depth-`≥ 2` pair.** **[kernel, as L1; the reading is new]**

Three things follow, and together they answer the stub's question and close it:

1. **The depth-2 boundary is not a property of `U`, of `K`, or of the six `ℤ/4` homomorphisms.** It
   is forced by `exponent(G) ∣ 4`. The six `ℤ/4` colourings are needed only for the *reverse*
   containment — the sharpness — and on `U` they deliver it: the artifact shows every one of the
   12,906 pairs at depth exactly 1 is separated additively, so `F_add(U) = {depth ≥ 2}` exactly.
2. **The one-level gap between the two conditions is exactly the exponent gap.** The residue
   colouring of Theorem B is an additive colouring whose target is the additive group of `𝔽₄`, i.e.
   `ℤ/2 × ℤ/2`, of exponent **2** — so it can only ever see `2Λ`, i.e. depth ≥ 1, which is precisely
   the valuation law's verdict. Using the whole family buys exactly one more level and no method in
   the family can buy a second. **The valuation law is the exponent-2 member of the additive
   family, and its one-level weakness is structural, not an artefact.**
3. **Therefore the 16-pair residue cannot be explained by any arithmetic in this family**, because
   it sits at depth 3, where every additive four-colouring is blind by theorem. `T26`'s question —
   *are the 16 separated by two Kempe swaps?* — is the right question and its answer must come from
   the **reconfiguration graph** of the colouring space, not from the lattice. That is a genuine
   negative and it redirects the work.

The general form, worth stating once because it is free: *for `k` colours the additive family's
floor is `kΛ`, since every abelian group of order `k` has exponent dividing `k`* — so at five
colours (`G = ℤ/5` forced) the floor is `5Λ`, which is exactly the hypothesis
`AdditiveRigid.forces_of_additivelyRigid` consumes (ledger row 51). The two milestones are using one
lemma from opposite sides.

## 4. What this leaves as candidate Lean work — stated, not started

None of the three items was taken to Lean; this package had no build budget and two of the three
files that would be touched belong to other packages. Three statements are worth minting, in
descending order of value:

1. **The 1-sum lemma.** *If `G = G₁ ∪ G₂` with `V(G₁) ∩ V(G₂) = {v}` and no edge between the parts,
   then `G.Colorable k ↔ G₁.Colorable k ∧ G₂.Colorable k` for `k ≥ 1`.* Classical, elementary,
   apparently absent from Mathlib, and it is what §1.2's first two critical vertices consume. A
   companion for the second move: *two `k`-colourable graphs joined by a single edge are
   `k`-colourable for `k ≥ 2`.*
2. **The exponent floor as a named lemma.** `Additive.addMonoidHom_apply_eq_of_sub_eq_nsmul` already
   proves it; what is missing is the packaged consequence — *no additive `k`-colouring separates a
   pair whose difference lies in `kΛ`* — stated in the `Separated` vocabulary, beside the existing
   `not_separated_of_addMonoidHom`. Ten lines.
3. **The circulant identification.** `D = {t : t² − 4ν is a non-square}` and the halving of the
   spectrum. This is a real number-theory milestone rather than a lemma, and it should wait for a
   consumer: the natural one is the five-row finite check that closes P7.

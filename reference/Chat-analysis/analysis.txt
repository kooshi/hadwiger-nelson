The blind spot is a property of linearity, and every instrument the field has ever pointed at the 5–7 interval shares it. Additive colourings are homomorphisms. The fractional chromatic number is a linear relaxation. The density/Fourier route bounds one colour class at a time by an LP. Hoffman is spectral. Three published ceilings say the same thing from three directions: χ_f(ℝ²) ≤ 4.36, so no relaxation can reach 5; Croft's unit-avoiding set has density 0.2293 > 1/5, so the density route can never certify measurable χ ≥ 6 no matter how good the upper bound gets (currently 0.2470); residues jump 4 → 7. The colours you care about are carried by the integrality gap, and linear invariants are blind to integrality by construction. The arithmetic you want is non-linear.

**A retraction first.** The gap at 5 in G38 that I challenged two turns ago is a theorem, one line: 5 ≡ 1 (mod 4), so 𝔽₅ already contains √−1, so every prime above 5 splits in F(i) and none is conjugation-fixed. Same for 13, 17, 29. The usable primes are exactly p ≡ 3 (mod 4) with odd residue degree.

**The arithmetic that sees 5 was one step past Theorem B.** The residue map at a usable prime lands in a finite plane 𝔽_p², and the additive method only ever used *linear* colourings of that finite plane (a line: p colours; the zero subgroup: p² colours). The finite plane is itself a graph with a chromatic number, and that number is non-linear.

*Theorem.* Let F ⊂ ℝ be a number field, p ≡ 3 (mod 4), and suppose F has a prime 𝔭 above p with residue field 𝔽_p. Then every finite unit-distance graph in F² maps homomorphically to UD(𝔽_p, 2) = Cay(𝔽_p², {(a,b) : a² + b² = 1}), so χ(F²) ≤ χ(UD(𝔽_p, 2)).

*Proof.* 𝔽_p lacks √−1, so 𝔭 is inert in F(i), its residue field is 𝔽_p(√−1) ≅ 𝔽_p², and complex conjugation reduces to Frobenius. A unit vector has z z̄ = 1, hence valuation 0. Translate the graph so one vertex is 0; all vertices are then 𝔭-integral; reduce. Adjacent vertices differ by a norm-one residue, which is a point on the circle. ∎ (Odd residue degree f works identically with 𝔽_{p^f}.)

*Measured, this session, with the discipline you use.* The theorem was derived first. It then predicted a negative control: Heule-510 lives in ℚ(√3,√5,√11), and 11 has a degree-one prime there, so UD(𝔽₁₁, 2) must refuse four colours. Measured: refused, 5,449 nodes. Then the open question — five — and it answered SAT in 3 s. Three encodings (DSATUR; two random relabellings with different base vertices) agree; the 5-colouring was checked independently against the raw circle definition: 0 monochromatic unit-quadrance pairs. Controls: χ(UD(𝔽₃,2)) = 3, χ(UD(𝔽₇,2)) = 4, each with its k−1 refusal. **χ(UD(𝔽₁₁, 2)) = 5.** Vinh's 2005 bounds bracket it at [√11/2, 6]; the exact value looks unrecorded.

*Corollaries.* (1) **χ(ℚ(√3,√5,√11)²) = 5 exactly** — Heule-510 below, the 121-vertex plane above. That closes §8 of the field-wall note. (2) **No 6-chromatic unit-distance graph has all coordinates in a field with a degree-one prime above 11.** The sieve (Legendre symbols, attached): Heule's field has 11 usable; so does K(√247), the T20 field of your first √5-free 5-forcer; so does K(√5,√247). *The descent, in its current field, provably cannot end.* De Grey's field ℚ(√3,√5,√7,√11) has no usable prime below 131, because √7 makes 11 inert. So the 5-wall is G38's 3-adic wall at p = 11 — "χ ≥ 4 needs no degree-one prime above 3" was the p = 3 instance of a general fact, and "the arithmetic goes blind above four" was an artifact of insisting the residue plane be coloured linearly. The profile of a field that can host a 6-forcer is now: 2-adic wall broken (Theorem B), and no degree-one prime above 3, 7, 11, or any other p ≡ 3 (mod 4) with χ(UD(𝔽_p,2)) ≤ 5. For multiquadratic fields that is a finite list of congruence conditions on the radicands; √7, √35, √2, √6, √10, √13, √17, √19, √21 all make 11 inert. (3) A falsifier for Haugland: 7 is totally ramified in ℚ(ζ₇), so the heptagonal fields have a degree-one prime above 7 as far as I can compute (adjoining the ring-radius square roots ramifies it further without changing the residue field), and χ(UD(𝔽₇,2)) = 4. If his 2,131 vertices generate such a field, the graph is 4-colourable and the float+SAT verification is wrong; otherwise his detuning adjoins a 7-inert radical. Compute his field, factor 7. Cheap, and it should precede any import.

*What this instrument is.* One-sided. It bounds χ(F²) from above for specific F and can never bound χ(ℝ²) above, since the sup over fields includes fields with no small usable prime (Vinh's lower bound √p/2 → ∞ says those planes look ≥ 6 to it). It is a field-selection tool for the hunt, and it produces, per prime, exactly the kind of finite kernel-checkable certificate your pipeline already promotes — a 121-point colouring is a `decide`. It supersedes my blocking-set suggestion, which was about when the *linear* colourings of 𝔽_p² die; the right object is the plane's own χ. χ(UD(𝔽₁₉,2)) and χ(UD(𝔽₂₃,2)) are beyond my toy solver in 90 s and are minutes for CaDiCaL; 19 and 23 are not usable for any √3-containing field, so they matter for the map rather than for the descent.

**Reframing so the blind spot disappears.**

*Factor the problem.* χ(ℝ²) = sup_F χ(F²) over finitely generated real F. The theorem factors χ(F²) into (a) the chromatic numbers of finite Euclidean planes — finite, exact, one SAT call each — and (b) the arithmetic of which primes are usable in F, which is Legendre symbols. Both halves are integers. The 5–6 question for any specific field is now decidable in the direction "≤ 5" whenever a usable prime with χ ≤ 5 exists, and de Grey's field is the first known field where nothing decides it.

*Design in the finite plane, lift to the field.* Speculative, and new vocabulary: for a p with χ(UD(𝔽_p,2)) ≥ 6, the plane has 6-critical subgraphs H. A finite unit-distance graph whose reduction mod 𝔭 is H with every H-edge lifted to a unit edge would be a 6-forcer. Lifting is diophantine and homs run the wrong way for a free lunch, but the finite plane's critical subgraphs are candidate shapes computed without geometry.

*The upper-bound side already knows what a 5- or 6-colouring must look like.* Townsend 1981 (correcting Woodall 1973) and independently Coulson 2004: no 5-colouring is a map or a tiling. Thomassen 1999, and Sokolov–Voronov 2026 for maps with 3-valent vertices and non-unit-arc boundaries: seven. Falconer 1981: measurable needs 5, with the Croft ceiling above. Your G22 adds: lattice-cell colourings are exactly 7. So a 6-colouring of ℝ², if it exists, is a non-map, non-lattice, probably non-measurable object — and the 𝔭-adic pullbacks are the canonical constructive examples of exactly that kind of wild colouring. They work field by field and cannot glue across fields with different usable primes, which is at least a heuristic account of why the wild colourings the topology demands have never been built for ℝ².

*The lower-bound side: what sees integrality.* Three families, with the literature FS-2 should ingest.
Kempe invariants. Heawood's parity criterion (a triangulation is 3-colourable iff every degree is even) is the classical instance of a mod-n invariant that sees exactly the integrality of a colour; Mohar–Salas built the 4-colour instance on triangulations (their Kempe invariant, from the non-ergodicity of the Wang–Swendsen–Kotecký algorithm); Mohar 2006 and Bonamy–Bousquet–Feghali–Johnson 2019 give the Kempe-equivalence theory for regular graphs. Your "holonomy" at 3→4 is monodromy in S₃; this is its 4→5 vocabulary.
Borsuk–Ulam. The only lower-bound technique that is sharp at every integer (Kneser, Lovász 1978; Lovász 1983 for distance graphs on spheres; Kahle–Martinez-Figueroa 2020 show it is tight even for random Borsuk graphs). It needs a free ℤ₂-action. The plane's central symmetry is one on any centrally symmetric graph avoiding the origin — and G9/G27 found the 791-forcer is exactly V ∩ −V, which is what a Borsuk–Ulam mechanism looks like from outside. Whether any unit-distance graph has box-complex coindex ≥ 3 is open as far as I know. Speculative; the hint is yours.
Nullstellensatz certificates (De Loera–Lee–Malkin–Margulies; Alon–Tarsi). Infeasibility of the k-colouring polynomial system over 𝔽_q is certified by linear algebra at bounded degree, the certificate degree is a dial, and it is integrality-sensitive — this is the "polynomial certificate matching the ball refutations" G24 asked for. Over 𝔽₁₆ the fifth roots of unity are native.

One fact qualifying G25: O'Donnell (Rutgers thesis 1999, arbitrary girth) and Wormald (girth 5, 1979) built 4-chromatic unit-distance graphs with no triangles at all. The 3→4 step has a route that is not rainbow-clique holonomy, and triangle-free forcing is exactly the regime where topological and Mycielski-type arguments live.
